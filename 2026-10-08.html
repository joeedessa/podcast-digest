DOMINANT THEMES

Uranium's Secular Bull Market (2 shows)

THESIS: Uranium is in the early stages of a long-term secular bull market driven by a structural supply deficit that will take years to resolve. Growing, non-discretionary demand from reactor life extensions, new builds in Asia, and the power needs of AI data centers is colliding with a decade of underinvestment in new mine supply, creating a powerful setup for significantly higher prices.

BULLS (Justin Yoon, John Ciampaglia) The demand side is increasingly de-risked as utilities extend the lives of existing reactors to 80 years and countries like China and India aggressively build out new nuclear capacity for energy security, argues Justin Yoon — Wealthion. He sees uranium as the primary bottleneck in the nuclear fuel cycle. The supply side is highly inelastic; John Ciampaglia — Money of Mine notes it takes a very long time to permit and build new mines. He also explains how the Sprott Physical Uranium Trust (SPUT) helped resuscitate the industry by creating a transparent price discovery mechanism and absorbing loose spot supply, forcing utilities into the term market at higher prices. Justin Yoon — Wealthion projects prices will reach a minimum of $150-$200 per pound this cycle to incentivize the necessary new production.

KEY TENSION: Whether the uranium price can rise high enough to bring sufficient new and restart production online to meet long-term demand without a major nuclear accident or technological breakthrough in energy that curtails nuclear's growth trajectory.

SPOTLIGHT: Sprott: The Best Business in Mining? (John Ciampaglia)

Host: Travis Ricciardo, JD Guest: John Ciampaglia, CEO, Sprott Asset Management

  • John Ciampaglia detailed Sprott's transformation from a founder-led firm to a $55 billion AUM specialized asset manager by focusing on capital-light physical commodity trusts.
  • The genesis of the physical trusts was a response to counterparty risk concerns during the 2008-2009 crisis, leading them to use the Royal Canadian Mint as a low-risk custodian.
  • The Sprott Physical Uranium Trust (SPUT) was a multi-year, contrarian effort, launched when the uranium market was "completely broken" at $18/lb.
  • Ciampaglia argues SPUT helped resuscitate the uranium industry by providing transparent price discovery and absorbing spot supply, forcing utilities to engage in the term market.
  • Sprott's deep specialized knowledge and trading relationships in niche markets like uranium create a "deep moat" that is difficult for larger, generalist competitors to replicate.
  • A return of generalist and retail investors to commodities is being driven by themes of energy security, national security, and reshoring.

SPOTLIGHT: Cupani Metals Targets a Raglan-Scale Copper-Nickel System at Blue Lake

Host: Ian Wagner Guest: Brian Boss, CEO, Cupani Metals

  • Cupani Metals' Blue Lake project in Quebec is being explored based on a geological theory of a "magmatic river" similar to Glencore's Raglan mine.
  • Recent drilling confirmed mineralization a mile away from the known historical (non-compliant) resource of 4.3 million tons at 2.2% copper equivalent.
  • CEO Brian Boss highlighted the economic advantage of the project's shallow, flat-lying ore bodies, which should result in lower operating costs compared to competitors.
  • The deposit is poly-metallic, with an estimated revenue breakdown of 55% copper, 30-40% nickel, and the remainder from PGEs and cobalt.
  • Boss believes the project has the potential to be the "next Raglin mine" and offers tremendous long-term value.

SPOTLIGHT: How to NOT value Ethereum and how to do it RIGHT

Host: Pascal Hügli Guest: Solo episode.

  • Traditional valuation methods for Ethereum (cash flow analysis, TVL multiples) are flawed because its utility and fee structure are fundamentally changing.
  • The "ultrasound money" narrative is dismissed as a "meme" because the Denun upgrade in early 2024 caused Ethereum's supply to grow, making it no longer deflationary.
  • Ethereum is evolving from "money you hold" (like Bitcoin) into a "commodity you spend" to access the network's block space.
  • There has been a strategic shift from a Layer 2 scaling vision to a Layer 1 scaling focus by increasing the main chain's gas limit, which makes transactions cheaper but introduces decentralization trade-offs.
  • Ethereum's value should be increasingly tied to the utility and demand for its block space as a commodity.
