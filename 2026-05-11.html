MACRO ROOM TEMPERATURE

The market's narrow, AI-driven leadership is facing a direct challenge from a rising real-economy narrative, championed by voices like Peter Tchir and Tavi Costa. The central tension is whether the parabolic move in semiconductors, which Patrick Ceresna and Kevin Muir call a "blow-off top," will collapse into a broader rotation towards tangible assets. This potential shift is underpinned by geopolitical imperatives for reshoring ("ProSec" trade), a structural bull case for commodities, and a contrarian bullish turn on China, creating a complex environment where momentum could violently cede to value.

DOMINANT THEME(S)

The AI Paradox: Frothy Top or Foundational Shift? (5 shows)

The consensus around AI's dominance is fracturing. On one side, Patrick Ceresna and Kevin Muir — The Market Huddle see a classic "blow-off top" in semiconductors, noting the SMH is a speculative 53% above its 50-week moving average with poor market breadth, signaling an imminent 20-30% pullback. This view is subtly supported by David Wright — The Monetary Matters Network, who suggests that corporate spending expectations for chips and cloud computing are "very very toppy" as firms like his become more efficient at training models.

However, others see a more durable, albeit complex, transformation. Dror Poleg — Jacob Shapiro argues AI is a fundamentally nonlinear force, rewiring the economy around intangible assets and making volatility the new normal. This isn't a simple tech bubble but a paradigm shift where value creation is unpredictable, as seen in Nvidia's 10x growth. In this new world, specific niches thrive. Chris Mayer — Excess Returns identifies vertical market software companies as prime beneficiaries, as their deep integration and proprietary data give them a moat to leverage AI for higher margins. Meanwhile, Leonid Mironov — The Market Huddle notes that China is pursuing a more pragmatic AI strategy focused on "ROI not AGI," applying it to manufacturing and supply chains, suggesting a different, more industrial path for AI adoption globally.

The Real Economy Strikes Back: Geopolitics, Commodities, and Reshoring (4 shows)

A powerful counter-narrative is emerging, focused on tangible assets and geopolitical necessity. Peter Tchir — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money frames this as the "ProSec" (Production for Security) trade, a bipartisan, long-term shift towards reshoring critical industries. He argues investors should focus on electricity generation, grid infrastructure, critical minerals, and domestic chip manufacturing, with opportunities concentrated in the American heartland.

This theme is echoed by Tavi Costa — Milk Road Macro, who sees Latin America as the "biggest opportunity of the decade" due to its vast commodity resources and a favorable political realignment. He is aggressively bullish on gold, silver, and copper, expecting a weaker US dollar and suppressed interest rates to provide a major tailwind. Leonid Mironov — The Market Huddle adds to this, seeing asymmetric upside in oil and flagging upcoming shortages in lithium. He also introduces a crucial macro element: China's policy shift away from "involution" (competing to the bottom) means it will begin exporting inflation rather than deflation, which will lift prices for industrial commodities like aluminum and benefit producers globally. This shift from intangible tech to hard assets is presented as a multi-year trend driven by security needs and changing global trade dynamics.

DIVERGENT VIEWS

The Investment Case for China

Leonid Mironov — The Market Huddle: BULLISH Reasoning: China is undergoing a significant pro-market policy shift with its "anti-involution" campaign, which discourages value-destructive price wars. This is causing a turn from deflation to inflation (seen in recent PPI prints), which will lead to healthier corporate earnings. With Chinese consumers holding healthy balance sheets, equities offer a traditional inflation hedge, making Chinese stocks an attractive investment.

Peter Tchir — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: STRUCTURALLY CAUTIOUS / AVOID Reasoning: The dominant US geopolitical and economic strategy is to counter China. The entire "ProSec" trade is built on reshoring, shortening supply chains, and developing "fortress North America" to reduce dependency on China. US actions, from trade wars to diplomatic efforts in South America, are aimed at gaining bargaining power against and diminishing the influence of China, making it a region to hedge against, not invest in.

US Market Outlook for Summer 2026

Patrick Ceresna & Kevin Muir — The Market Huddle: BEARISH on leadership, capped S&P 500 Reasoning: The semiconductor sector is in a parabolic "blow-off top" phase, evidenced by the SMH trading 53% above its 50-week moving average on very narrow breadth. They expect this to resolve with a 20-30% pullback in the sector starting in May, which will not crash the overall market but will put a ceiling on the S&P 500 for the summer as a sector rotation occurs.

Jim Paulsen — Excess Returns: NEUTRAL on leadership, bullish on breadth Reasoning: The current environment is disinflationary, driven by slow labor force growth, with recent inflation being a temporary supply-side issue. He does not anticipate a tech rollover or market collapse. Instead, he expects a broader economic recovery where other sectors, which have been flatlining, begin to catch up to the booming "new era" tech sector, leading to a broadening of the market rally rather than a correction.

The Primary Driver of Inflation

Leonid Mironov — The Market Huddle: INFLATIONARY pressures from China Reasoning: China is pivoting from its long-standing role as an exporter of deflation. New "anti-involution" policies are forcing companies to stop competing on price to the point of unprofitability. This will lead to higher producer prices and a sustained period of higher inflation being exported globally, a dynamic for which most investors are unprepared.

Jim Paulsen — Excess Returns: DISINFLATIONARY pressures dominate Reasoning: The fundamental driver of the US economy is low labor force growth (0.5-1% annually), which prevents the kind of demand-pull inflation seen in the 1970s. Recent inflation spikes were temporary phenomena caused by supply-side shocks like the pandemic, tariffs, and war. The underlying trend is disinflationary.

SPOTLIGHT: Generative AI Cant Generate Alpha But Machine Learning Can

Host: Jack Farley Guest: David Wright, Pictet Asset Management

  • Generative AI is Unsuitable for Alpha: David Wright — The Monetary Matters Network argues that generative AI (like ChatGPT) is not effective for forecasting market returns because it can "hallucinate" or invent information, cannot be tested effectively, and its outputs are not easily interpretable — all highly undesirable traits for an investment process.
  • Machine Learning is the Key: Pictet's approach uses machine learning, specifically thousands of decision trees (gradient boosting), on structured, tabular data. This method is transparent, testable, and produces interpretable return forecasts, moving quantitative investing from a "black box" to a "crystal box."
  • Portfolio Construction: The firm's AI-enhanced ETFs (PQNT, PQUS) are designed as replacements for passive index funds. They aim for an index-like risk profile (beta of one) while targeting 1-2% in annual outperformance by using the model's forecasts to overweight attractive stocks and underweight unattractive ones.
  • Cloud Spending May Be "Toppy": Despite using increasingly complex models, Wright notes his firm has become so efficient at training them that their cloud computing costs have not risen as expected. He extrapolates this to suggest that market-wide expectations for spending on chips and cloud services may be "very very toppy."
