DIVERGENT VIEWS

US Treasury Bonds Outlook

Lance Roberts — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money®: BULLISH Reasoning: Current yields (e.g., 5% on 5-year Treasuries) are a "gift" that investors should lock in. Bonds provide capital preservation and predictable income, covering a significant portion of portfolio return goals with less equity risk. He notes that the 10-year Treasury yield is three standard deviations above its 50-month moving average, a condition that has historically preceded a drop in yields. He believes disinflationary trends are emerging and the Fed may be over-hiking.

Andy Constan — Excess Returns: BEARISH (with a tactical long) Reasoning: His primary view is short bonds. He believes strong economic growth from consumption and AI compute buildout, combined with large government deficits and increased debt supply, are the main drivers of rising long-term rates. He does not see a significant shift in inflation expectations that would justify a bond rally. However, he is taking a small, speculative long position (risking 3% of his portfolio) for a potential short-term recovery, while acknowledging bonds now have a role in a long-term 60/40 portfolio where they didn't before.

US Equity Market Outlook

Andy Constan — Excess Returns: BEARISH Reasoning: Does not expect significant equity returns unless the economy weakens. The market is in a "higher for longer" regime where strong growth supports decent returns, but significant acceleration is unlikely with 30-year yields at 5.5%, 10-year yields at 5.25%, and an ongoing Fed hiking cycle.

Lance Roberts — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money®: BULLISH (through year-end) Reasoning: Predicts a move higher in markets through the end of the year, driven by the return of corporate stock buybacks, strong earnings, and potential gridlock in midterm elections, which Wall Street typically favors. He expects a rotation from growth into beaten-down value sectors like small caps and mid-caps, which will lift the broader market.

Andrew Sheets — Morgan Stanley: CAUTIOUSLY CONSTRUCTIVE Reasoning: Equity market resilience is explained by strong corporate earnings growth (S&P 500 profits up ~30% YoY), which has so far offset the negative valuation impact of rising bond yields. The equity risk premium has remained stable. However, higher yields "raise the bar," meaning profits must continue growing quickly to sustain current valuations.

DOMINANT THEMES

The End of Cheap Money & Rising Rate Pressures (8 shows)

THESIS: The era of cheap credit that defined the post-2008 period is definitively over, with the 10-year Treasury yield above 5.3%. This new regime is creating immense pressure on interest-rate sensitive sectors of the economy, forcing a repricing of risk and raising the probability of a systemic credit event.

STRESS & BREAKAGE CAMP (Steve Eisman, Chris Whalen, Andy Constan, Jay Martin) The current rate environment is unsustainable and will lead to failures. Steve Eisman — Steve Eisman states the 5.3% 10-year yield means "we are getting closer to something breaking," specifically identifying highly-levered private equity software buyouts as "dead money" where equity will be wiped out. Chris Whalen — The Julia La Roche Show argues the K-shaped economy is becoming an "L" as inflation and high rates pressure even affluent consumers, leading to a significant economic slowdown and rising defaults. Andy Constan — Excess Returns believes rates are being pushed higher by strong growth and debt supply, which will cap equity upside and create a challenging environment. Jay Martin — The Jay Martin Show adds a geopolitical layer, arguing Iran is actively trying to break the US bond market by engineering oil price spikes that force the Fed to hike rates, increasing US debt service costs.

RESILIENCE & OPPORTUNITY CAMP (Andrew Sheets, Lance Roberts) While acknowledging the pressure, this camp sees either offsetting positive forces or opportunities within the new rate regime. Andrew Sheets — Morgan Stanley explains that equity markets have remained resilient because powerful corporate earnings growth has counteracted the negative valuation impact from higher rates. Lance Roberts — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® views the high yields as a "gift" for investors, arguing that buying bonds now offers significant income, capital preservation, and a hedge against a potential equity bear market.

KEY TENSION: Will strong corporate earnings and economic growth continue to provide a sufficient buffer against rising rates, or will the cumulative pressure on debt servicing costs trigger a cascade of defaults in vulnerable sectors like private credit and consumer finance?

Structural Inflation from Energy & Geopolitics (5 shows)

THESIS: A significant portion of current inflation is driven by structural energy shortages and geopolitical risk, factors that are largely immune to the Federal Reserve's monetary policy tools. The Fed's attempts to combat this supply-side inflation by crushing demand risk causing a recession without addressing the root cause.

SUPPLY-SIDE DRIVERS CAMP (Eric Pacman, Chris Whalen, Jay Martin, Steve Eisman) This group identifies non-monetary factors as the primary inflationary impulse. Eric Pacman — Excess Returns points to "crisis level" diesel crack spreads ($85) and critically low inventories as a structural issue that could add 0.5% to CPI on its own, a problem the Fed cannot solve. Chris Whalen — The Julia La Roche Show concurs, citing scarcity and wars in Iran and Ukraine as the real drivers of inflation in essentials. Jay Martin — The Jay Martin Show details how Iran can weaponize oil choke points to manufacture inflation and pressure the US bond market. Steve Eisman — Steve Eisman simplifies the entire macro picture to two variables that matter: oil prices and interest rates.

FED'S DILEMMA CAMP (Lance Roberts, Chris Whalen) This camp focuses on the inadequacy of the Fed's response. Lance Roberts — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® argues the Fed is "making a mistake" by hiking rates, as underlying PCE data is weak and rate hikes only punish the US consumer without affecting global oil prices. Chris Whalen — The Julia La Roche Show acknowledges the Fed is using a "very blunt instrument" but has little choice; when elevated energy prices seep into the broader economy, the central bank feels compelled to raise rates to slow activity and anchor expectations, even if it risks a recession.

KEY TENSION: Can the Fed successfully tame inflation by hiking rates to crush demand when the primary drivers are supply-side energy costs and geopolitical actions, or will this policy simply induce a recession while the core inflationary problem remains?

SPOTLIGHT: Scenes from IETA North America Climate Summit 2026

This series of episodes from the SmarterMarkets™ podcast provides a deep dive into the current state and future of the voluntary and compliance carbon markets, featuring key industry leaders.

SPOTLIGHT: Scenes from IETA North America Climate Summit 2026: Alexia Kelly, MD, High Tide Foundation

Host: David Greely Guest: Alexia Kelly, Managing Director of Carbon Policy and Markets Initiative, High Tide Foundation

  • The voluntary carbon market has bottomed and is now on an upswing, with market value exceeding $1 billion, up from a low of about $273 million in 2023.
  • A major demand shift is underway: by 2035, an estimated two-thirds of global carbon credit demand will come from compliance markets, with only one-third from voluntary action.
  • The Integrity Council for Voluntary Carbon Markets (ICVCM) has successfully established a global quality benchmark, which is harmonizing the market and attracting institutional investment.
  • Critical market infrastructure is being built, including a "permanence trust" using insurance-like products to guarantee carbon storage and clearer accounting standards (from FASB) for booking credits as assets.

SPOTLIGHT: Scenes from IETA North America Climate Summit 2026: Guy Turner, MD, MSCI Carbon Markets

Host: Derek Guest: Guy Turner, MD, MSCI Carbon Markets

  • Carbon markets are showing resilience and price increases in compliance schemes (EU ETS, California) despite macro headwinds like high oil prices and inflation.
  • A government-backed initiative, the Coalition to Grow Carbon Markets, could increase annual demand four-fold, from 200 million tons to 600-800 million tons.
  • The market has shifted away from simple, liquid benchmarks towards a more project-specific, OTC structure, requiring more granular data for pricing and risk, which MSCI provides.
  • A key trend is the development of emissions trading systems in middle-income countries (Japan, India, Brazil), often modeled on the EU ETS, which will likely become interconnected over time.

SPOTLIGHT: Scenes from IETA North America Climate Summit 2026: Sandeep Roy Choudhury, Co-Founder, VNV

Host: David Greely Guest: Sandeep Roy Choudhury, Co-founder & Director, Value Network Ventures (VNV)

  • The "integrity debate" in carbon markets is largely resolved, and the focus must shift to getting buyers to participate and integrating credits into corporate accounting.
  • Artificial Intelligence is a "game-changer" that can shrink carbon credit delivery time from three years to less than three months, unlocking cash flow for project developers.
  • AI is a double-edged sword: its massive energy and water consumption will exponentially increase emissions, making carbon credits an "absolute must" to offset its impact.
  • Regulatory confusion between different national schemes is a major hurdle; policy uniformity is needed to improve liquidity and fungibility.

SPOTLIGHT: Scenes from IETA North America Climate Summit 2026: Benoît Clément, VCM

Host: David Greely Guest: Benoît Clément, Fellow, Financial Infrastructure for Carbon Markets at the Verified Carbon Markets Collaborative

  • The carbon market's biggest challenge is a lack of financial market infrastructure; it must "copy paste" existing frameworks from mature asset classes like commodities.
  • Foundational issues of legal title and data standardization must be solved first to enable custody, settlement, collateralization, and accounting for carbon credits.
  • Solving these infrastructure gaps is the key to unlocking large-scale institutional capital from pension funds, asset managers, banks, and insurance companies.
  • Demand is set to grow from the second phase of Corsia (the airline industry scheme) and the implementation of Article 6 of the Paris Agreement.

SPOTLIGHT: Scenes from IETA North America Climate Summit 2026: Thomas McMahon, Co-Founder, Abaxx

Host: David Greely Guest: Thomas McMahon, Co-Founder, Abaxx Technologies

  • The future of environmental markets is brighter than a year ago, with a "bottoms-up market formation" as countries like Singapore, Japan, and India develop their own carbon regimes despite some financial institutions stepping back.
  • The convergence of compliance and voluntary markets, driven by policy, is the future of the industry.
  • Europe's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) is a crucial bridge, incentivizing investment in voluntary projects in other countries to achieve equivalence or face tariffs.
  • The lack of standardized project financing is a major challenge; an exchange-traded model for pre-issuance financing is needed to scale the market.

SPOTLIGHT: Scenes from IETA North America Climate Summit 2026: Alex Saer, CEO, Cercarbono

Host: David Greely Guest: Alex Saer, CEO, Cercarbono

  • Cercarbono, a standard from the Global South, recently received a key endorsement from ICVCM, boosting its credibility and project registrations.
  • The organization focuses on nature-based methodologies (REDD+, reforestation, blue carbon) that are designed to align with national-level data and policies, facilitating integration with schemes like Article 6.
  • Convergence of principles across different national carbon schemes is critical to reduce complexity for project developers operating in multiple jurisdictions.
  • AI presents "enormous opportunities" to improve the efficiency, traceability, and transparency of the carbon credit certification process.

SPOTLIGHT: Deal Fever: NST Dodges, LYC Deals

Host: Travis Ricciardo, JD Guest: Solo episode.

  • The mining sector is seeing significant M&A activity, highlighted by Goldfields' (GFI) rejected A$38.7B bid for Northern Star (NST).
  • A key driver for consolidation among producers is the ability to achieve tax synergies (US$4-5B in the GFI/NST case) through asset write-downs and accelerated depreciation.
  • Northern Star's board rejected the bid as undervaluing the company, but the public nature of the leak suggests shareholder pressure tactics are at play, and an alternative bidder like Agnico Eagle could emerge.
  • In the rare earths space, Lynas' (LYC) A$968M all-share acquisition of Meteoric (MEI) is notable for occurring during a CEO transition, suggesting it may have been a defensive "plan B" move after other M&A talks fell through.

SPOTLIGHT: The Millionaires You've Never Heard Of: Owen Zidar on How Americans Really Get Rich

Host: Jimmy Connor Guest: Owen Zidar, Professor of Economics and Public Affairs, Princeton University

  • A significant portion of America's wealthy are "Everywhere Millionaires"—owners of non-glamorous, scaled businesses like auto dealerships, convenience stores (e.g., Buc-ee's), and restaurant franchises.
  • Wealth is often generated not through radical invention but through incremental innovation, operational efficiency, and the relentless scaling of a successful model over decades.
  • The US tax system, particularly the rise of "pass-through entities" (S-corps, partnerships) since 1986, has been a major tailwind, allowing business owners to avoid corporate tax and reinvest profits more efficiently.
  • Common traits of these entrepreneurs include relentlessness, competitiveness, a willingness to take significant risks, and often a history of being "troublemakers" or boundary-pushers in their youth.
