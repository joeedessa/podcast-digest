TL;DR (read this first)

The primary tension is whether this resilient growth can withstand the very policy tightening it is causing, creating a stark bifurcation in equities between a handful of mega-cap leaders and a rapidly deteriorating broader market.

  • TOP THEME: Bond Market Bearishness — A strong consensus expects higher yields due to sticky inflation, AI capex, and a hawkish Fed.

DOMINANT THEMES

The Bond Market's Bearish Consensus (7 shows)

THESIS: A powerful consensus has formed that the path of least resistance for bond yields is higher. This view is underpinned by a resilient US economy, sticky inflation fueled by energy and tight industrial capacity, and a Federal Reserve that is seen as credibly hawkish and likely to hike further than previously expected.

HIGHER FOR LONGER (Lakshman Achuthan, Neil Dutta, DCP, Dario Perkins, Patrick Ceresna) The case for higher yields is multi-faceted. The US is in a cyclical "inflationary boom" that is not yet over, which will continue to push rates up, according to Lakshman Achuthan — Mauldin Economics. This resilient growth and elevated oil prices mean the Fed must maintain a restrictive stance and will likely hike at least three more times, argues Neil Dutta — RenMac. The bond market cannot turn favorable for longs until AI capex slows, crack spreads roll over, or the Fed explicitly signals an end to hikes, none of which are imminent, states DCP — Forward Guidance. The bond market is "accelerating the crash to the downside," with rising real yields indicating the move is no longer just about oil, adds Patrick Ceresna — Macro Voices. Given this backdrop, it is too early to buy the bond sell-off, concludes Dario Perkins — GlobalData TS Lombard: Perkins Vs Beamish.

KEY TENSION: What catalyst will be powerful enough to break the bearish bond narrative — a slowdown in AI investment, a geopolitical de-escalation, or a credit event forced by the higher rates themselves?

The Great Equity Divergence: AI Leaders vs. The Rest (4 shows)

THESIS: Headline equity indices are masking severe underlying weakness across the broader market. A handful of mega-cap technology stocks, buoyed by the AI narrative, are driving performance, while most other sectors are breaking down under the pressure of rising interest rates, creating a dangerously bifurcated and vulnerable market.

NARROW LEADERSHIP (Patrick Ceresna, Jeff deGraaf, Michael Batnick) The divergence is stark: the S&P 500 index appears bullish, but the equal-weight S&P 500, Russell 2000, and financials are breaking to new lows, with only 28% of stocks above their 50-day moving average, notes Patrick Ceresna — Macro Voices. This is confirmed by relative strength data, where 350 names in the Russell 1000 are hitting relative lows versus only 39 hitting highs, with the laggards concentrated in capital-intensive businesses, according to Jeff deGraaf — RenMac. The environment is so difficult that "quality is not bailing anybody out this year," with even stalwarts like McDonald's seeing steep declines, adds Michael Batnick — The Compound.

KEY TENSION: Will the AI-driven mega-caps eventually succumb to the broader market's weakness and rising rates, or will their earnings power be strong enough to pull the rest of the market up with them?

  • The Fed will hike again in October and continue hiking through year-end.

SPOTLIGHT: The Xi-Trump risk nobody's pricing in

Host: Andrew Polk Guest: Joe Mazur, Trivium China

  • The Xi-Trump meeting on Sep 24-25 was high on atmospherics but low on substantive deliverables, with only 30 minutes of scheduled discussion.
  • The main outcome was a short, two-month extension of the Busan trade deal (to Jan 10th), which pauses the trade war and China's rare earth export controls.
  • China's primary goal is to be "left alone" and minimize instability in the relationship, a realist pursuit of self-interest rather than an ideological one.
  • A subtle rhetorical shift was noted, with Xi now willing to use the term "healthy competition."
  • A key risk is that if this meeting fails to build momentum, two subsequent leader meetings (APEC in Nov, G20 in Dec) could be canceled, removing a key ballast for the relationship.
