DOMINANT THEME(S)

The AI & Semiconductor Super-Cycle: Boom or Bubble? (5 shows) The narrative around technology, particularly AI and semiconductors, is one of explosive growth clashing with bubble fears. Jeff Hirsch — Maggie Lake Talking Markets — argues a "super boom" is underway, driven by AI, quantum computing, and DeFi, comparing it to the productivity explosion of the 1990s and asserting that traditional valuation metrics are "dated" for these new-era companies. This view finds some fundamental support from David Woo — Wealth Building Blueprint Vladyslav Grab — who notes AI CapEx accounted for a staggering 50% of US Q1 GDP growth. The market's price action reflects this exuberance, with Rupert Mitchell — Benny The Squirrel — describing the semiconductor ETF (SMH) as being in a "parabola phase," and John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call — pointing to "almost parabolic ramping" in names like NVIDIA (now at a $5T valuation) and Samsung (over $1T).

However, this optimism is met with significant caution. Hardy explicitly questions if this is an "incipient bubble," advising prudence. Mitchell's analysis of market internals reveals a rally built on a weak foundation, with 50% of the S&P 500 in a technical downtrend and breadth near historical lows, indicating extreme concentration. From a macro policy perspective, Megan Greene — Odd Lots — identifies AI as a potential "significant deflationary force" and a positive supply shock, a long-term structural view that contrasts with the market's immediate price-driven focus.

Geopolitics and Persistent Inflation as Tail Risks (4 shows) A strong undercurrent of geopolitical and macroeconomic risk runs counter to the market's bullish sentiment. David Woo — Wealth Building Blueprint Vladyslav Grab — presents the most acute warning, framing the Iran war as a US-China proxy conflict that the market is misreading. He believes the expectation of a quick resolution is wrong and that persistently higher oil prices will ultimately undermine the stock market rally. This aligns with the broader framework from Megan Greene — Odd Lots — who argues central banking has entered a new era of "continuous successive shocks" where the old playbook of "looking through" supply-side events is no longer effective.

Adding to this, Marcus Thielen — Less Noise More Signal — forecasts US inflation will be sticky around 5.5%, significantly above consensus, implying the Federal Reserve will remain on hold for longer than markets expect. John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call — is closely watching the upcoming Xi-Trump summit as a "development for the ages" that could redefine US-China relations and have major implications for the US dollar's role in the global financial system.

DIVERGENT VIEWS

Outlook for FICO (FICO)

Kelsey Zhu — Steve Eisman: SHORT Reasoning: FICO has engaged in predatory price increases in its mortgage vertical (from $0.60 to $10 per pull), alienating the industry. This has triggered a regulatory shift, with the FHFA mandating the use of competitor VantageScore 4.0 starting in July 2025. VantageScore is priced at a fraction of FICO's cost (~$1 vs $10), and the credit bureaus (who own VantageScore) are now incentivized to promote it over FICO. Zhu forecasts FICO will lose 40% of its mortgage market share within three years, a risk she believes is completely ignored by street consensus, leading to her 2027 and 2028 EPS estimates being 13% and 24% below consensus, respectively.

Street Consensus (as described by Kelsey Zhu) — Steve Eisman: LONG / HOLD Reasoning: The market consensus, which is reflected in current sell-side analyst estimates, assumes FICO will lose 0% market share in the mortgage vertical. This view implicitly relies on FICO's historical monopoly power and brand strength being sufficient to withstand the challenge from VantageScore, despite the massive price differential and regulatory changes.

Outlook for Gold

Jeff Hirsch — Maggie Lake Talking Markets: NEUTRAL / BEARISH Reasoning: Believes gold has already topped out and has limited upside from current levels. He views commodities in general as a harder asset class to trade than equities due to their rapid price movements.

David Woo — Wealth Building Blueprint Vladyslav Grab: CONDITIONALLY BULLISH Reasoning: Gold is currently trading as a "real yield play." Its upside is capped as long as the AI trend continues or oil prices keep rising, as both scenarios tend to push real yields higher. However, gold could attract significant interest and rally if energy prices remain high, the US economy slows, and the market starts to price the geopolitical situation as a growth shock rather than just an inflation shock.
