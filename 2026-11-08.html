DOMINANT THEMES

The AI Narrative Under Siege (5 shows)

THESIS: The Democratic sweep in the midterm elections has acted as a powerful catalyst, shattering the prevailing market narrative of an unstoppable AI-driven boom. The focus has abruptly shifted from limitless growth to the tangible risks of political headwinds, regulatory scrutiny, and prohibitive capital costs, forcing a broad-based repricing of the entire AI ecosystem.

BEARISH CAMP (Steve Eisman, David Woo, Brent Kachuba, Chris Whalen) The consensus in this camp is that the best days of the AI trade are over. Steve Eisman — Steve Eisman argues that a slowdown in AI capex, which he estimates accounts for at least 50% of GDP growth this year, will inevitably lead to a broader economic slowdown. David Woo — Wealth Building Blueprint – Vladyslav Grabarskyy is explicitly short AI, arguing the "Trump put" is gone and the new Democratic Senate will unleash a wave of investigations, creating major headwinds. He calls AI valuations "completely laughable." Brent Kachuba — Excess Returns points to the options market, where a "giant volatility expansion" and a spike in correlations signal a rotation "out of equities," not just within them. He advises liquidating any bounces. Chris Whalen — The Julia La Roche Show warns that AI-hyped stocks like Micron and AMD were overvalued and could fall further.

CAUTIOUS CAMP (Andrew Sheets, Michael Lebowitz, Matt Zeigler) This group sees a more nuanced picture rather than an outright collapse. Andrew Sheets — Morgan Stanley believes the midterms don't change the "big picture on AI" but could lead to an "elongated buildout" due to new state-level regulations, which could actually ease bond supply pressures. Michael Lebowitz — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® views the selloff as primarily technical and believes it's "premature to call the top," suggesting the AI buildout will simply move to other countries if blocked in the US. Matt Zeigler — Excess Returns notes that governorship flips in states representing 37% of planned data center capacity create a "totally new environment," but he expects a policy compromise rather than a complete halt.

KEY TENSION: Resolution depends on whether the political and regulatory threats translate into concrete policy actions that materially slow or halt data center construction, validating the bearish thesis.

Structural Inflation in Energy & Logistics (3 shows)

THESIS: Beyond headline CPI, persistent and broad-based inflationary pressures are building within the physical economy's plumbing, particularly in energy transportation and logistics. These costs are not transitory and are expected to remain elevated for years, creating a structural headwind for the global economy.

EVIDENCE (Sean Evers, Eric Pacman, Chris Whalen) The core of this theme comes from the divergence between financial markets and physical reality. Sean Evers — SmarterMarkets™ explains that while a Brent futures contract trades at $110, the actual delivered cost of a physical barrel to Asia is closer to $200 due to shipping and insurance costs that are at a "multiple of 20" what they were pre-war. He expects these costs to remain elevated "well into 2027." Eric Pacman — Excess Returns describes the situation as "chaos in the tanker market," with daily rates exploding from $20-30k to $1.2-1.3 million, effectively halving tanker supply. He also warns the Strategic Petroleum Reserve is being drained for financial arbitrage by traders, not for refining, setting up a worse shock in 2027. Chris Whalen — The Julia La Roche Show adds that US diesel stocks are at "the bottom of the barrel," predicting physical shortages in some markets and a significant bump in food prices next year as a direct consequence.

KEY TENSION: The market's focus on Fed policy and demand destruction will be tested against the reality of inelastic demand and structurally higher physical delivery costs for essential commodities.

SPOTLIGHT: We're Still Early in This Mining Boom (Stephen Gorenstein)

Host: Travis Ricciardo, JD Guest: Stephen Gorenstein, RI Resources Fund

This episode provides a deep dive into a non-consensus, long-term bullish thesis for the mining sector, arguing that the market is in the "very early stages of a multi-year mining boom." This boom is not driven by typical cyclical factors but by a structural shift as Western nations, led by the US, move to nationalize critical mineral supply chains to counter China's dominance.

  • Thesis: The West's push to secure supply chains for defense, AI, and robotics will create a secular demand driver for critical minerals, similar to Japan's steel boom in the 1970s or China's urbanization in the 2000s. This is not yet priced into mining equities.
  • Commodity Selection: Gorenstein prioritizes getting the commodity right first. He is long Gold (real rates, central bank buying), Copper (generational supply deficit from net-zero), and Uranium (5+ year opportunity). He actively avoids Lithium and Nickel due to oversupply.
  • Portfolio Construction: The fund is ~40% Gold, ~30% Copper, and 15% Uranium. It uses a "fat head and a long tail" approach with core positions (2-5%) and smaller exploratory "tail" positions (0.5%).
  • Key Stock Ideas (LONG):
  • Gold: Asara, Many Peaks, PDI, Vault (Genesis), Benz, Pantoro.
  • Copper: Andina, Firefly, Midas, Solstice.
  • Uranium: NextGen ("best undeveloped uranium mine globally"), Atomic Eagle.
  • Rare Earths: Lindian, Viridis.
  • Investment Philosophy: Gorenstein emphasizes bottom-up research, including management meetings and site visits, combined with network validation for core positions. He is also highly selective on jurisdiction, avoiding countries like Burkina Faso and Mali due to political risk.
