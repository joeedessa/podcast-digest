DOMINANT THEMES

The AI Reckoning (8 shows)

THESIS: The consensus AI trade, centered on hardware and compute providers like NVIDIA, is fracturing. A growing chorus of investors now sees the sector as a classic capex-driven bubble, warning of a near-term bust due to over-investment, drying liquidity, and a fundamental shift in power from chip monopolists to compute buyers.

BEARS (Porter Stansberry, David Orr, Jason Shapiro, Matt Caruso, Josh Brown) The current AI boom is a CAPEX cycle in its "final inning" that will end when the bond market stops funding it, leading to a bust before the end of 2027 and a 50% drop in NVIDIA's revenues (Stansberry). This is a classic commodity "boom-bust cycle" in compute, which is approaching an "oversupply situation" that could cause AI stocks to fall 80% (Orr). The trade is dangerously crowded with retail "weak money," particularly in NVIDIA, and broader liquidity is drying up, creating the potential for a "fiasco" (Shapiro, Caruso). The balance of power is now shifting away from chip companies towards compute buyers like OpenAI and Anthropic (Brown).

CAUTIOUS BULLS (Brett Rintoul, David Shapiro, Seth Carpenter) While we are in the "early innings" of the AI revolution, the market may be "a little ahead of ourselves," warranting tactical caution for a potential correction (Rintoul). The long-term optimistic case remains immense, with AI potentially creating a $5-10 trillion TAM if it delivers on productivity promises (Shapiro). At a macro level, the global AI phenomenon is the primary driver of resilient global growth and CapEx spending, providing a powerful underlying momentum to the economy (Carpenter).

KEY TENSION: Resolution depends on whether the bond market continues to fund the massive capex buildout and whether AI software applications can generate revenue fast enough to absorb the coming wave of compute supply.

The Inevitable Fiscal Reckoning (4 shows)

THESIS: The United States is on a collision course with a debt crisis, driven by intractable deficits that are historically unprecedented for a late-stage economic expansion. This fiscal imbalance is entrenching inflation, forcing the Fed's hand, and setting the stage for a painful resolution through higher taxes and spending cuts, which will directly impact corporate earnings and long-term growth.

HAWKS (Dr. Lacy Hunt, Jesse Felder) Dr. Hunt states he is the "most concerned" in his career, pointing to a 6.3% of GDP budget deficit at the "top of the boom." He argues this is an "intractable" problem with no political solution, which will lead to a rising risk premium on Treasury debt, a structurally higher trend in long-term yields, and a gradual erosion of the US dollar's reserve status. Felder directly connects this to a future "earnings crisis," arguing that the only way to solve the debt crisis is to raise taxes, which will make current long-term earnings estimates unattainable.

MODERATES (Seth Carpenter) While acknowledging inflation pressures will force the Fed to hike two more times (December and March), Carpenter's base case remains one of resilient global growth. This view implies the economy, powered by AI investment, can navigate the "test" from higher energy prices and interest rates without succumbing to a fiscal-driven crisis in the near term.

KEY TENSION: The 10-year Treasury yield will signal the breaking point. As Jesse Felder states, it is "the most important chart in the world" for tracking how close the market is to a debt crisis that forces fiscal austerity on politicians.

Dr. Lacy Hunt — The Julia La Roche Show

Thesis: The US economy is in a "Keynesian endgame" defined by an intractable budget deficit (6.3% of GDP at the top of a boom) and entrenched inflation. This combination, along with deglobalization and a capital shortage, will force the risk premium on Treasury debt higher, leading to a secular trend of rising long-term interest rates and declining real per capita growth. He is the most concerned he has been in his career because there are no politically viable solutions.

Predictions:

  • A mild recession would cause the budget deficit to expand to 10-10.5% of GDP.
  • Interest expense will reach 20% of the federal budget in approximately five years, possibly sooner.
  • The trend in long-term interest rates is higher and will be very volatile.
  • The US dollar's role as the world's reserve currency will gradually erode.

CONVICTION & SCENARIOS

Seth Carpenter — Morgan Stanley: Outlined four alternate scenarios to his base case of resilient growth:

  • Scenario 1 (Faster Growth/Higher Inflation): Stronger aggregate demand leads to higher inflation and more central bank hikes.
  • Scenario 2 (Faster Growth/Stable Inflation): Faster aggregate supply (productivity from AI) leads to faster growth without significant inflation, but potentially higher neutral interest rates.
  • Scenario 3 (Downside Shock): Oil prices rise significantly, forcing a sharp central bank reaction and slowing growth.
  • Scenario 4 (Slowdown): The AI CapEx build-out falls short of expectations, hitting both direct spending and indirect wealth effects, leading to a slowdown.

SPOTLIGHT: A Gold Mine's Second Chance: Inside 1911 Gold's Restart Strategy

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Sean Hinrich, CEO of 1911 Gold Corp

  • 1911 Gold Corp is focused on restarting the True North mine, a brownfield project with existing infrastructure, which reduces capital needs and permitting risk.
  • The previous operators failed due to declining gold prices and critical execution errors, primarily using an inappropriate bulk mining method that diluted high-grade ore.
  • Hinrich's new strategy is "drill before you mine," using smaller equipment and focusing on quality tons from multiple mine faces rather than pushing for high tonnage at low grades.
  • The company's cash position is low, and it will need to draw on its second tranche of debt and restructure existing debt to fund operations until reaching cash flow, targeted for mid-2027.
  • Hinrich is targeting full production by 2028-2029 and believes a $4,000 gold price would allow for more aggressive expansion.

SPOTLIGHT: Awalé Resources: Connecting Charger and Expanding the Gold Resource

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Andrew Chubb, CEO, Awale Resources

  • Awale Resources is focused on expanding its gold resource in Côte d'Ivoire, which it views as a politically stable and favorable jurisdiction.
  • Recent drill results have connected two key zones (Charger 1 and 2), supporting an internal goal to grow the resource to half a million ounces.
  • The company has a line of sight to increase its overall resource from 1.7 million ounces towards 2.5 million ounces.
  • Due to the significant potential for resource growth from pending drill results, the company has strategically paused its Preliminary Economic Assessment (PEA) to incorporate the new, larger-scale data.
  • The company is well-funded with 35 million CAD in cash, sufficient for its exploration budget through the end of 2027.

SPOTLIGHT: Blue Moon's $220 Million Focus to American Critical Minerals

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Christian Cargle Samard, CEO of Blue Moon Metals

  • Blue Moon Metals has strategically sold its Norwegian assets for $220 million in shares of another company (ACG), simplifying its story to focus on US critical metals.
  • The company is positioned to address US supply chain vulnerabilities for tungsten, germanium, and gallium, which Samard calls "three of the four military metals."
  • The price of germanium has surged from $4,500/kg in March to $17,000/kg, making its Apex mine extremely valuable, with ore valued at the equivalent of 4 ounces of gold per ton.
  • The Apex mine requires only $15 million in capex to start and could generate $225 million in annual EBITDA at current prices.
  • Samard believes the company's four US projects are sufficient to create a multi-billion dollar company.

SPOTLIGHT: Cassiar Gold: Why Insiders Are Buying Amid the Junior Mining Selloff

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Marco Rok, CEO, Cassiar Gold

  • Cassiar Gold recently completed a 10,000-meter drill program and expects a resource update in Q1 2027, followed by a maiden Preliminary Economic Assessment (PEA).
  • Rok believes the market is severely undervaluing the company, which trades at an enterprise value below $20 CAD per ounce in the ground, versus a historical M&A average of $200/oz.
  • Management's conviction is high, demonstrated by a recent $3 million insider-led private placement, with Rok personally investing an additional $200,000.
  • The recent conservative win in the British Columbia provincial election is seen as "very positive" for the mining sector, with expectations of a pro-business environment and faster permitting.

SPOTLIGHT: Scorpio Gold Hits 9.4 g/t Over 13 Metres at April Fool, Eyes Goldwedge Restart

Host: Ian Wagner Guest: Zane Kalyan, CEO, Scorpio Gold

  • A recent high-grade drill result (9.4 g/t gold over 13 meters) is a significant step-out that demonstrates the district-scale potential of Scorpio's Nevada project.
