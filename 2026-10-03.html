The US economy presents a starkly bifurcated picture, with an AI-driven capex boom masking significant weakness on Main Street and in the housing sector. Key voices like Aiden Garb and Jesse Felder argue this K-shaped recovery is unsustainable, with Felder predicting a new, sustained Fed hiking cycle will pop the AI bubble and drive the 10-year yield towards 6-7%. DOMINANT THEMES

The AI-Driven Economy: Bubble or New Paradigm? (6 shows)

THESIS: The US economy's apparent strength is almost entirely dependent on a massive, concentrated AI and hyperscaler capex boom, creating a K-shaped divergence that masks weakness elsewhere. The central debate is whether this is a sustainable new economic driver or a speculative bubble fueled by cheap capital that is now facing the reality of higher rates and questionable returns on investment.

SKEPTICS (Jesse Felder, Edson, Isabel Kaminska, Quinn) The current AI boom is a speculative bubble nearing its end, argues Jesse Felder — Wealthion, citing tightening credit for AI-related firms and oversupply in data centers. The path to superintelligence is "bloody inflationary" due to the massive capital investment, warns Isabel Kaminska — Benny & The Squirrel, who questions if it will generate real productivity gains or just become a "Chinese ghost city" of unused capacity. This investment is squeezing out the rest of the economy, notes Quinn — Forward Guidance, creating a policy choice to support an "AI only economy" at the expense of Main Street. The bubble is most acute in private markets, according to Edson — The Compound, where investors are "delusional" about valuations for companies with massive operating losses and concentrated revenue streams.

PRAGMATISTS (Josh Brown, Aiden Garb, Jeff deGraaf) While narrow, the AI boom is a real economic force. US nominal GDP growth is being driven by this hyperscaler spending, states Aiden Garb — Forward Guidance. For these foundational, winner-take-all technologies, focusing on scale and user acquisition over immediate profit is the correct strategy, counters Josh Brown — The Compound, pointing to Amazon's history. However, the boom's impact on the broader market is fading; Jeff deGraaf — RenMac notes that only a few semiconductor names still qualify as momentum leaders, and secondary beneficiaries like industrials and utilities have "cracked" and not recovered.

KEY TENSION: The resolution depends on whether AI investments can generate tangible, widespread productivity growth before tightening financial conditions and rising rates choke off the funding required for the next phase of development.

Rising Rates & The Bond Market Capitulation (5 shows)

THESIS: After years of suppression, interest rates are exerting immense pressure across global markets, with the US bond market in a "tail spin" and European sovereign debt showing signs of cracking. The key question is whether this repricing will be orderly or lead to a disorderly "accident" that forces central bank intervention.

HIGHER FOR LONGER (Jesse Felder, Tony Greer, Jeff deGraaf) The market is facing a new, sustained rate hiking cycle, according to Jesse Felder — Wealthion, who sees the 10-year Treasury yield potentially reaching 6-7%. This is creating an exceptionally difficult trading environment, says Tony Greer — Competent Man Podcast - Tom Bodrovics, who is waiting for a clear capitulation in the bond market (TLT) before changing his defensive posture. The pressure is building, as Jeff deGraaf — RenMac points to rising triple-C credit spreads, indicating the cycle is in its "latter innings" and higher rates are beginning to bite.

EUROPEAN STRESS (John J. Hardy, Johan van der Burgt) The situation is particularly acute in Europe. John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call highlights a "climax" in sovereign debt stress, with the French 10-year spread over Germany blowing out to 149 basis points. This is reminiscent of the 2012 European debt crisis, adds Johan van der Burgt — Less Noise More Signal, and is a clear negative signal for the Euro.

KEY TENSION: The market is waiting to see if rising yields will trigger a financial "accident" in credit or sovereign debt markets, which would force the Fed and ECB to pivot away from their inflation-fighting stance.

SPOTLIGHT: Inside China's two-decade campaign to win over Latin America

Host: Andrew Polk Guest: Leland Lazarus, Founder and CEO of Lazarus Consulting

  • China's engagement in Latin America has evolved from opportunistic commodity purchasing to a strategic five-pillar plan focused on political, economic, and cultural influence.
  • Chinese firms have funded, financed, or operated 37 ports in the region, viewing them as "strategic support points" with potential dual commercial and military use. The Port of Chancay in Peru is a key project that will cut shipping times to Asia by 20 days.
  • Chinese companies have secured significant stakes in the "lithium triangle" (Argentina, Bolivia, Chile), which holds 60% of global reserves, often by engaging directly with provincial governments.
  • Latin American nations are growing concerned about over-dependence on China, leading to rising "resource nationalism" and a search for investment diversification from the US and Europe.
  • A new concern is "geocriminality," where Chinese criminal networks involved in money laundering and trafficking are potentially being leveraged to further Beijing's political aims, such as pressuring countries to drop diplomatic recognition of Taiwan.

SPOTLIGHT: What It Really Takes to Build a Mine

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Paul Cronin, CIO, Admiralty Fund Resource Fund

  • Building a mine, especially in a frontier jurisdiction, requires intense and early community and government engagement. Water management is a paramount concern that can make or break a project.
  • The Admiralty Fund is focused on three core themes: monetary debasement (driving gold higher), militarization (driving demand for specialty metals like tungsten), and electrification (driving a major, imminent copper shortage).
  • The market applies an "appropriate risk premium" to complex polymetallic projects; extensive metallurgical testing is non-negotiable to de-risk these assets.
  • Common flaws in mining management teams include underestimating the complexity of underground mining (ground/water conditions) and rushing into production without sufficient exploration.
  • Cronin predicts the Fed will not hike again this year, oil prices will fall, and gold will perform very well by year-end.
