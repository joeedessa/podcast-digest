DOMINANT THEMES

The Fed's Policy Mistake & The Bond Opportunity (2 shows)

THESIS: The Federal Reserve is tightening into a supply-side shock, a classic policy error that ignores powerful underlying disinflationary trends, creating a significant opportunity in government bonds as the market reprices for slower growth and lower inflation.

THE CASE FOR A POLICY MISTAKE (David Rosenberg) David Rosenberg — Maggie Lake Talking Markets — argues forcefully that the Fed is making a "classic policy mistake." He contends the bond market is not fearful of inflation itself—as TIPS break-evens are stable—but of the Fed's hawkish reaction function to supply shocks like diesel prices. These shocks, he states, are a "deflationary tax" on consumers and do not lead to sustained wage-price spirals. He believes the Fed, under Kevin Warsh, is overly focused on AI's productivity potential while ignoring the "canary in the coal mine": massive shelter disinflation that is not yet reflected in official CPI data but will eventually "swamp the inflationary impulse."

BONDS BECOME VIABLE AGAIN (Andy Constan, David Rosenberg) This perceived policy error creates an opportunity in bonds. David Rosenberg — Maggie Lake Talking Markets — is actively adding duration, citing the attractive 2.8% real yield on the 10-year note and the historical tendency for Treasury yields to fall after mid-term elections result in a split government. Andy Constan — Excess Returns — offers a more measured view, stating that after a "5-year bond disaster," bonds are now "acceptable" for a long-term beta portfolio with a positive Sharpe ratio of 0.17, a vast improvement from the negative Sharpe offered in 2020.

KEY TENSION: Whether the Fed will pivot its focus from supply-shock inflation to slowing growth and disinflation before causing a significant economic downturn, or if growth and inflation expectations will remain high, keeping yields elevated.

AI Concentration Risk in Public Markets (3 shows)

THESIS: The AI boom has driven a massive, concentrated bet within the S&P 500, forcing investors to question the diversification of passive strategies and assess the durability of a trend heavily dependent on a few hyperscalers.

Steve Eisman — Steve Eisman — agrees the AI story "is for real" and will likely continue as long as a price war between the major AI models is avoided.

KEY TENSION: Whether the AI demand from a handful of hyperscalers is sustainable enough to justify current valuations, or if a price war, demand saturation, or infrastructure bottlenecks (like power) will burst the bubble.

SPOTLIGHT: Tungsten The War Over the Metal of War

Host: Chris Berry Guest: Solo episode.

  • Market Bifurcation: Chinese export controls have split the global tungsten market in two: a Chinese domestic price and an ex-China scarcity price. The spread between the two is now around $2,000 per MTU.
  • Structural Deficit: The West faces a potential structural deficit of 20,000 tons per year if Chinese controls remain. There are no good substitutes for tungsten at scale in its key defense and industrial applications.
  • Bottleneck Shift: The binding constraint for the Western supply chain is no longer mining but midstream conversion capacity (APT and oxide plants). Chris Berry — Mining Stock Daily — argues every dollar for mining should be paired with a dollar for processing.
  • US Policy Response: The US has used the Defense Production Act to restrict tungsten scrap exports, but this is insufficient. DFARS rules effective Jan 1, 2027, will trace tungsten origin back to the mine, meaning recycled material does not reset origin for defense use.
  • Investment Thesis: Policy support (price floors, offtakes) is necessary to de-risk investment in a Western supply chain. Investors should focus on companies that can execute on building out this midstream capacity, not just on exploration press releases.

SPOTLIGHT: Trump Accounts Explained How 1000 Could Grow Into Much More

Host: Maggie Lake Guest: Brandy Mabin, Director at Windrock Wealth Management

  • Account Type: "Trump accounts" are new tax-deferred custodial accounts for children, similar to an IRA, where ownership transfers at age 18.
  • Funding: Eligible children (born 2025-2028) can receive a $1,000 government deposit. Other funding can come from employers ($2,500 gift) or family contributions (up to $5,000 annually).
  • Power of Compounding: A key benefit is early compounding. Brandy Mabin — Wealthion — noted that the initial $1,000, growing at 4%, would nearly double by age 18 without any additional contributions.
  • Flexibility: Unlike 529 plans, funds are not restricted to educational expenses, making them a flexible savings vehicle for families uncertain about a child's future path.
  • Strategic Use: Brandy Mabin — Wealthion — suggests a strategy of rolling the Trump account into a Roth IRA when the young adult is in a low tax bracket to maximize long-term tax-free growth.
