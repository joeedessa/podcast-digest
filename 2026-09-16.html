TL;DR (read this first)

The bond market's "revolt," as described by Trey Rake — Wealthion, is the dominant force in today's analysis, with the 10-year Treasury yield breaching 5% for the first time since 2007. This has sharpened the divide between two camps: those like John Rubino — Competent Man Podcast and Michael Oliver — Wealth Building Blueprint who see an imminent global debt crisis and monetary reset, and those like Jason Shapiro — Markets Unscripted who are struck by the equity market's resilience in the face of such clear macro headwinds. The resolution hinges on tomorrow's FOMC decision, which will determine if the Fed validates the bond market's fears by hiking into a precarious setup, potentially breaking the market's composure.

  • CENTRAL TENSION: The bond market's "revolt" signaling a debt crisis versus the equity market's stubborn resilience.
  • TOP THEME: The Inevitable Debt Crisis — Rising global debt and incoherent policy are forcing a "death spiral" towards a monetary reset.
  • DIVERGENT: US STOCK MARKET — Michael Oliver is explicitly bearish, calling a multi-year top, while Jason Shapiro notes its "incredible resilience" and believes a rate hike could even be bullish.
  • HIGH-CONVICTION TRADE: LONG GOLD — Supported by four speakers (Trey Rake, John Rubino, Peter Alexander, Michael Oliver) as a hedge against fiscal decay and an eventual pivot to easier money.
  • CALENDAR: The FOMC rate decision tomorrow, which multiple speakers see as a critical inflection point.

DOMINANT THEMES

The Inevitable Debt Crisis & Monetary Reset (5 shows)

THESIS: A confluence of unsustainable government debt, rising interest rates, and incoherent policy is creating a "death spiral" for the current financial system. This is seen not as a cyclical downturn but a structural crisis that will force a major reallocation from financial assets to hard assets and likely culminate in a global monetary reset.

CRISIS CAMP (John Rubino, Michael Oliver, Trey Rake, Vincent Deluard) The system is fundamentally broken. John Rubino — Competent Man Podcast describes a "death spiral" where rising rates on massive US debt ($10 trillion to be rolled over this year) accelerate deficits with no viable fix. Michael Oliver — Wealth Building Blueprint calls the global government debt situation a "nuclear event" that will trigger a multi-year bear market in stocks. This view is supported by Trey Rake — Wealthion, who sees the US moving toward "yield curve control light," which is "essentially money printing," as the bond market is in "complete revolt." Vincent Deluard — Maggie Lake Talking Markets sees a similar debt crisis brewing specifically in Europe, which he believes is on a path to stagnation and massive currency depreciation.

RESILIENCE CAMP (Jason Shapiro, Matt Caruso) While acknowledging the severe macro headwinds, this camp is focused on the market's surprising durability. Jason Shapiro — Markets Unscripted notes that despite oil over $100 and yields at 5%, the S&P 500 is only 1% off its highs, demonstrating "incredible resilience." Both he and Matt Caruso — Markets Unscripted emphasize that the market's reaction to news is more important than the news itself, suggesting the market may have already priced in these risks or is "sniffing something out" that the bears are missing.

KEY TENSION: Whether the market's resilience will finally break under the weight of rising yields and fiscal unsustainability, or if it has already discounted the worst outcomes.

Stagflationary Pressures from Commodities (6 shows)

THESIS: A surge in energy prices, particularly crude oil and diesel, is feeding into persistent inflation even as economic growth shows signs of slowing. This creates a classic stagflationary environment that severely complicates central bank policy, forcing them to choose between fighting inflation and supporting a weakening economy.

STAGFLATION DRIVERS (Vincent Deluard, John Rubino, Trevor Hall, Ole Hansen) The evidence for stagflation is mounting. Vincent Deluard — Maggie Lake Talking Markets states explicitly that stagflation is the "most likely outcome." The mechanism is clear to others: Trevor Hall — Mining Stock Daily notes the oil shock from geopolitical events is feeding directly into an inflation picture that already surprised to the upside. John Rubino — Competent Man Podcast identifies soaring diesel prices, now twice as expensive as the oil they are derived from, as a key inflationary driver that could cause the next recession. Ole Hansen — Saxo Market Call agrees, highlighting the "exceptional tightness" in the fuel product market, which is creating a "pickle" for central banks.

POLICY IMPLICATIONS (Trey Rake, John J. Hardy) This environment forces policymakers into a corner. Trey Rake — Wealthion observes that recent inflation readings are forcing the Fed's hand on a rate hike. John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call notes the "uncomfortable" multi-sigma moves in bond yields are a direct result of this tension, and that there will be "some kind of comeuppance" if the pace continues.

KEY TENSION: Whether central banks will be forced to hike rates into a slowing economy to fight commodity-driven inflation, thereby triggering a deeper and more painful recession.

SPOTLIGHT: PCOY Copper Expands South Breccia and Targets Major Resource Growth

Host: Ian Wagner Guest: Vincent Metcalfe, President and CEO of PCOY Copper

  • PCOY Copper has consolidated assets in southern Peru to define a maiden resource of 6.45 billion pounds of copper.
  • The company is in the midst of a 40,000-meter drill program aimed at expanding this resource and locating the deeper porphyry center.
  • Recent drill holes have extended over 1,000 meters each and returned grades higher than the existing resource grade, particularly in the gold-rich South Breccia zone.
  • Upcoming catalysts include results from a recently completed MT survey to define deeper targets, the start of drilling at the potentially much larger Torume asset, and a continuous flow of drill results on a monthly basis.
  • The corporate strategy is to de-risk the project to attract a major partner or acquirer, as developing a deposit of this scale would require over $2 billion in capital.

SPOTLIGHT: Phoenix Metals Greenlit 51,500 Metres and Four More Rigs at Greenwood

Host: Michael McCrae Guest: Nick Campbell, CEO, Phoenix Metals

  • Phoenix Metals has received an amended permit for a 51,500-meter drill program at its Greenwood Copper Gold project in British Columbia.
  • The company is accelerating drilling at its highest-priority target, the Phoenix area, with four rigs planned by the end of the month.
  • The goal is to define both near-surface, open-pitable bulk tonnage targets and high-grade zones.
  • The first drill results are expected from the Lexim target in early October, with a steady monthly news flow anticipated from November 2026 through September 2027.
  • The company would be "pretty happy" with continuous mineralization above 0.2-0.22% copper equivalent, and very happy with 0.5-1.0 g/t copper equivalent, which would establish it as a higher-grade district.

SPOTLIGHT: Verdera Energy Clears Key SEC Milestone

Host: Jimmy Connor Guest: Janet Lee-Sheriff, CEO, Verdera Energy

  • Verdera Energy is focused on uranium development in New Mexico's Grants Energy Belt, historically a major global producing district.
  • The company's key asset is a massive historical database from Kerr-McGee and URI, covering over 90% of all uranium drilling ever done in the state.
  • The strategy is to gain a "social license" with local communities, including the Navajo Nation, and advance projects using in-situ recovery (ISR) technology, which is safer than historical mining methods.
  • The West Largo project is described as the "highest grade ISR project in the US," with a 43-101 resource estimate expected by the end of the year.
  • Verdera was spun out of enCore Energy, which plans to distribute a significant portion of its shares to its own shareholders, increasing Verdera's public float.

SPOTLIGHT: enCore Energy's Growth Strategy

Host: Jimmy Connor Guest: Bill Sheriff, enCore Energy

  • The US consumes 45-50 million pounds of uranium annually but produces only about 3 million, creating a massive supply deficit.
  • A recent DOE request for 4 million pounds of US-sourced uranium annually for defense programs highlights this bottleneck and may lead to government intervention like the Defense Production Act to fast-track mines.
  • While the spot uranium market has been "comatose," the long-term contract price is at an all-time high, indicating a healthy underlying market.
  • Bill Sheriff advocates for consolidation in the uranium sector to create larger companies that can attract major institutional investors.
  • enCore Energy is positioned for significant growth, with new wellfields expected to contribute to production in 2027 and major projects like Dewey Burdo and Alta Mesa East coming online in 2028.

SPOTLIGHT: Patients Are Taking the Driver's Seat in Healthcare

Host: Erin Wright, Terrence Flynn Guest: Solo episode.

  • A structural shift is underway in healthcare, with consumers taking a more proactive role in managing their care, driven by greater choice, transparency, and digital tools.
  • In biopharma, this is creating a direct-to-consumer (DTC) parallel market, estimated to be a $26 billion peak opportunity in the US, with half driven by obesity/GLP-1 medications.
  • A recent survey showed 25% of consumers paid entirely out-of-pocket for at least one healthcare service in the past year, most commonly for behavioral and mental health.
  • The guests believe diversified and vertically integrated managed care companies are best positioned to benefit from this trend due to their multiple consumer touchpoints and technology investments.
