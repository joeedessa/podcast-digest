DOMINANT THEMES

The "Real" AI Trade: Physical Infrastructure vs. Digital Valuations (4 shows)

THESIS: The market's focus is shifting from the high valuations of AI software and chip companies to the tangible, underinvested physical assets required to power the revolution. This second-order effect is creating a new inflationary impulse in the real economy and forcing a re-evaluation of where the most durable returns will be found.

PHYSICAL ASSETS CAMP (Bob Rosbody, Jonathan Wellum, Clem Chambers) The true, long-term AI trade lies in the physical world. Bob Rosbody — Excess Returns argues that decades of underinvestment in materials like cement, aluminum, copper, and steel have created a structural bottleneck that will lead to tightening supply and inflationary pressure as AI-driven demand surges. Jonathan Wellum — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® echoes this, pointing to the immense demand for critical minerals and energy (uranium, oil) needed for data centers and the broader "production for security" reshoring trend. Clem Chambers — Wealth Building Blueprint – Vladyslav Grabarskyy frames this as a "massive capital expenditure cycle" benefiting "tap root" companies that finance and build this new infrastructure.

SELECTIVE TECH & CAUTION CAMP (Matt Caruso, Jonathan Wellum) While the physical build-out is real, caution is warranted on the digital side. Matt Caruso — Markets Unscripted observes that leadership within AI stocks is narrowing and becoming more volatile, advising investors to stick with relative strength leaders and aggressively cut laggards. Jonathan Wellum — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® is explicitly cautious on the "dear valuations" in the AI sector, warning of a potential "compute glut" similar to the dot-com fiber bust and holding a significant cash position as a buffer.

KEY TENSION: Whether the primary returns will accrue to the physical world enablers who have pricing power due to underinvestment, or if the digital leaders can sustain their premium valuations despite rising input costs and potential overcapacity.

The Everything Bubble Debate: Imminent Crash vs. Fabulous Opportunity (3 shows)

SPOTLIGHT: Six Opinions, Five Geologists

Host: Travis Ricciardo, JD Guest: Alex (Geologist)

  • Thesis: The junior mining industry faces an "existential crisis" due to a lack of investor education, leading to misallocation of capital based on marketing rather than science. Future discoveries depend on a renewed focus on scientific rigor, better funding from majors and government, and exploring "undercover" geological spaces.
  • Key Opportunity: Alex believes the next major discoveries will come from exploring geophysically "blind" areas, such as those under tertiary lava flows on the margins of known deposits. He also sees arbitrage opportunities in jurisdictions perceived as hostile to mining and potential in reprocessing old tailings storage facilities (TSFs).
  • Critique of the Industry: Alex was critical of the 2022 IPO wave, stating many companies lacked scientific dedication and simply remarketed old assets. He laments the financialization of the industry without "proper gatekeepers" and the closure of university geology departments in Australia.
  • Call to Action: He advocates for major miners like BHP and Rio Tinto to increase exploration funding tenfold and for the federal government to fund more geophysical surveys and partner with companies at the pre-feasibility stage to de-risk projects.
  • Specific Views: Alex is long DPM and WA1, bullish on gold ("looking fucking mean"), and believes nickel will be back by 2028. He is also positive on Capricorn, Regis, and the potential for consolidation in the Sandstone area of Western Australia.

SPOTLIGHT: When AI Takes Over Shopping Carts

Host: Andrew Rubin Guest: Nathan Feather, Morgan Stanley

  • Thesis: "Agentic e-commerce," where AI influences or automates purchasing, is set to become a major growth driver, potentially adding 6% to the five-year e-commerce addressable market and influencing 20% of industry volumes.
  • The Core Question: The key uncertainty for investors is who will "own the customer journey"—incumbent retailers who have existing trust and infrastructure, or external general AI agents that can shop across multiple sites for the best price. The answer will determine the future of advertising economics and profitability in the sector.
  • Profitability Challenge: For agentic transactions to be profitable, they must be incremental, as the query costs for large language models are currently higher than for traditional keyword searches. There is a real risk that external agents could divert high-margin advertising revenue away from e-commerce platforms.
  • Leading Indicators: Investors should monitor company disclosures on AI investment, consumer survey data on adoption rates (currently led by the US and China), and shifts in search behavior to identify which companies are successfully transitioning from experimentation to durable growth.
  • Winning Attributes: Companies best positioned to win will have strong retail fundamentals (broad selection, good logistics), a willingness to innovate with their own AI agents or partnerships, and complementary assets like loyalty programs and financial services.
