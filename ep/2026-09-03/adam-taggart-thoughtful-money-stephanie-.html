Stephanie Pomboy Has The Grand Game Just Changed

Host: Adam Taggart · Guest: Stephanie Pomboy, Macro Maven, Macro Mavens · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Adam Taggart and Stephanie Pomboy discuss potential "game changers" in macroeconomics and markets. Pomboy expresses skepticism about the sustainability of current market resilience to higher rates, citing the historical pattern of financial crises at successively lower interest rate levels. The conversation covers the Federal Reserve's evolving non-interventionist stance under Kevin Warsh, contrasting it with the Treasury's recent interventions, and the long-term implications of the US-Venezuela oil deal and geopolitical shifts.

Key discussion points

  • Kevin Warsh's Fed is attempting to restore market integrity by being non-interventionist, aiming to eliminate the "Fed put" that has been in place for four decades. This contrasts with previous Fed chairs who steadily burnished the put.
  • Stephanie Pomboy believes the market's ability to withstand higher rates (30-year at 5.2-5.3%) is due to investors "extend and pretend" games, anticipating Fed rate cuts that Warsh's stance now makes unlikely.
  • Pomboy highlights the increasing corporate bankruptcies and credit stresses, noting that while not high-profile, they represent the greatest wave since the Global Financial Crisis, indicating underlying fragility.
  • The current economic resilience is partly attributed to massive AI capex spending, a manufacturing boom from reshoring, and the stimulative effect of higher interest payments to bondholders, according to Adam Taggart.
  • Pomboy notes that the credit market is showing anxiety around the AI boom, with higher borrowing costs for hyperscalers and increased credit default swaps, suggesting a demand for greater risk premium.
  • Stephanie Pomboy is highly skeptical of Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's claim of having a "trillion dollars" in the Treasury General Account (TGA) to buy back Treasuries, calling it "nonsense" and a short-term gambit to scare off bond vigilantes before the midterms.
  • Pomboy suggests that if Scott Bessent genuinely wanted a trillion dollars to sustainably hold down the long end of the yield curve, he could revalue the US gold reserve from $42 an ounce to current market prices, increasing its value from $10 billion to $1 trillion overnight.
  • The rising Japanese bond yields and the potential breakdown of the yen carry trade are discussed, with Pomboy stating the impact depends on the dollar-yen relationship and the ability to stem the yen's decline.
  • Adam Taggart and Stephanie Pomboy agree that the US-Venezuela oil deal, while not immediately impactful on oil prices (5-10 years), could be a "huge long-term bonanza" for the US, potentially suppressing gasoline prices for decades and serving as a geopolitical lever against China.
  • Pomboy emphasizes that the US-Venezuela oil deal and actions in Iran should be viewed through the lens of Trump's trade negotiations and national security strategy against China, rather than solely as oil deals.
  • The long-term sustainability of US energy dominance and other policies is contingent on the political landscape, with Pomboy noting that a shift towards a "Democrat socialist movement" could swiftly end fossil fuel-friendly policies.

Notable quotes

"I'm sort of an Austrian in my economic bias. I'd like to see the economy do its own thing, complete free markets without this constant intervention by the Fed or the Treasury, which I think quite frankly is why we're in the situation we're in today."— Stephanie Pomboy

Market & macro takeaways

  • Stephanie Pomboy believes the market is underestimating the impact of higher interest rates due to a lingering expectation of a "Fed put," which Kevin Warsh's Fed is actively trying to dismantle. This suggests potential for significant market disruption if investors are forced to confront a truly non-interventionist Fed.
  • Pomboy highlights growing credit stress and corporate bankruptcies, indicating that despite headline economic resilience, underlying vulnerabilities are building, particularly for highly leveraged companies as debt rolls over at higher rates.
  • Pomboy is bearish on rates, expecting yields to continue higher, but notes a massive speculative short position in the long end of the yield curve could lead to a sizable short-covering rally if there's a catalyst for perceived fiscal discipline or reduced long-dated issuance.
  • Pomboy is an "energy bull" in the near term, expecting oil prices to remain stable or edge higher due to global reserve rebuilding and accelerating AI-driven demand, despite long-term potential from the Venezuela deal.
  • The geopolitical implications of US energy policy, particularly regarding Venezuela, Canada, and Iran, are seen as a significant long-term lever for the US against China, potentially cementing America's role as the "gas station to the world."
