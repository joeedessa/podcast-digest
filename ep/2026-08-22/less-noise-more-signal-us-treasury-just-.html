US Treasury Just Ended the Bitcoin Bear Market

Host: Pascal Hügli · Solo episode · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Pascal Hügli discusses the recent surge in Bitcoin price, attributing it to the US Treasury's announcement of increased long-term government debt buybacks. He argues that these buybacks, funded by bill issuance, constitute "QE light" and are stimulative, potentially marking the end of Bitcoin's bear market. Hügli emphasizes the shift in importance from the Federal Reserve to the Treasury in influencing market dynamics.

Key discussion points

  • Bitcoin's recent price surge above key resistance levels, including the bear market support band, short-term holder cost bases, the 200-day moving average, and the true market mean, is attributed by Pascal Hügli to the US Treasury's buyback announcement.
  • Pascal Hügli explains that the US Treasury's increase in long-term government debt buybacks, specifically 20- and 30-year bonds, from $2 billion to $4 billion, is a "QE light" measure aimed at capping long-term yields and reducing borrowing costs.
  • Pascal Hügli highlights that the Treasury's buybacks are likely funded by issuing bills, which has a stimulative effect, as argued by economists like Joseph Wang and Andy Constant, and supported by a 2024 paper co-authored by Steven Mnuchin.
  • Pascal Hügli notes that the Treasury's actions, including a prior intervention in the FX market to strengthen the Japanese Yen, signal a strategic effort to support long-end interest rates and establish the Treasury's primacy in monetary policy.
  • Pascal Hügli points out that while the $4 billion buyback amount seems small compared to the $5.5 trillion outstanding supply of 20- and 30-year bonds, its impact is significant at the margin, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has indicated the size and pace could be dramatically increased if effective.
  • Pascal Hügli suggests that the weighted average maturity of marketable Treasury debt is likely to come down from around 70 months towards the historical average of 61.4 months, indicating further duration reduction from the market.
  • Pascal Hügli observes that the US is a frontrunner in this shift towards bill issuance, with 22% of marketable debt already in T-bills, and other countries like Canada, Japan, Germany, and the UK are expected to follow suit.
  • Pascal Hügli advises that while the Shaun momentum oscillator and the end of the last bear market suggest a potential end to the current bear market, the daily RSI for Bitcoin is currently high, making it not the best time to buy, and recommends waiting for consolidation and established support levels.
