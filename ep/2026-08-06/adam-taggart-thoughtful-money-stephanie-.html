Stephanie Pomboy Rising Bond Yields Are EVERYONES Problem

Host: Adam Taggart · Guest: Stephanie Pomboy, Founder, Macro Mavens · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Stephanie Pomboy argues that a "higher for longer" interest rate environment is here to stay, driven by massive credit issuance from both the government and the private sector (especially hyperscalers), rising oil prices, and the Bank of Japan's intervention. She believes this will lead to a long-term shift where hard assets and commodities outperform financial assets, and active, fundamental investing will become crucial again after a period of cleansing bubble excesses.

Key discussion points

  • Stephanie Pomboy believes the "higher for longer" interest rate environment is here to stay, especially if one is bullish on AI and the associated capex boom, as this will feed a crowding out phenomenon putting upward pressure on Treasury yields.
  • Adam Taggart notes July saw the worst month for the NASDAQ in 25 years, the highest bond spike in July since 2005, and the highest oil price spike in July in over 30 years.
  • Stephanie Pomboy argues that current profit numbers for hyperscalers are inflated by mark-to-market gains from investments like Amazon's stake in Anthropic, which are not actual earned money and do not contribute to their ability to pay salaries or service debt.
  • Stephanie Pomboy highlights that free cash flow has gone negative for many hyperscalers, which were previously cash cows, and that these companies are issuing debt "like crazy," with increasing costs due to widening spreads.
  • Stephanie Pomboy points out that the private sector has borrowed the same amount as the federal government this year, creating a "deluge of paper" and massive demand for capital, which is driving up long-end Treasury yields.
  • Stephanie Pomboy notes that companies have stopped buying back shares and are now net issuers of stock, removing a significant tailwind for the stock market.
  • Stephanie Pomboy states that the Bank of Japan's intervention to support the yen by selling dollars (often Treasuries) is putting further upward pressure on the Treasury complex.
  • Stephanie Pomboy asserts that the Fed has little control over long-end yields and that its primary tool to affect outcomes in a credit crisis would be the balance sheet, which is currently expanding despite stated intentions.
  • Stephanie Pomboy believes that deglobalization is a secular trend that will lead to steadily higher inflation and interest rates, siphoning liquidity away from financial assets and into the real economy.
  • Stephanie Pomboy and Adam Taggart discuss the potential for a massive pension bailout in the next recession, given the estimated $4 trillion in underfunding for public and private pensions, and the significant exposure to potentially overvalued alternative assets.
  • Stephanie Pomboy suggests that active, fundamental investing will become necessary for performance in an environment of higher capital, goods, and labor costs, moving away from the "easy wins" of the past.
  • Stephanie Pomboy maintains a long-term view that commodities and hard assets will outperform financial assets due to their limited supply compared to the "hyperscale" printing of financial paper.
