Is Buying a Home Still a Good Investment

Host: Ben Carlson, Duncan Hill · Guest: Jack Reins, Author of Young Money: A Field Guide to Wealth and Purpose in Your 20s · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode of Ask the Compound features Ben Carlson and Duncan Hill interviewing Jack Reins, author of "Young Money." The discussion focuses on financial and career advice for young people, particularly those in their 20s, addressing topics such as early career spending, homeownership in high-cost-of-living cities, the impact of AI on career paths, and the risks of using margin in investing. Reins shares personal anecdotes and practical advice, emphasizing flexibility, continuous learning, and understanding the fundamental building blocks of one's profession.

Key discussion points

  • Jack Reins advises his younger self to have sold all SPACs in February 2021 after being up 6,000% and turning $6,000 into $400,000 in his Roth, instead of continuing to trade and blowing half of it.
  • Reins suggests that young people in their early to mid-20s, especially those on high-slope career paths in high-cost-of-living cities, can afford to spend a little more on experiences with people they care about, rather than solely focusing on maxing out retirement savings, provided they don't have high-interest debt.
  • Ben Carlson recommends that young people not feel guilty about investing small amounts, even $10 a week, to build a habit and do enough to get their 401k match, then have fun with the rest.
  • Jack Reins believes that for young people in high-cost-of-living cities who do not plan to raise children there, it is acceptable to rent and not stress about homeownership, maintaining flexibility to move later.
  • Regarding AI, Jack Reins advises young people to learn the fundamental building blocks of their field manually before layering AI on top, and to also tinker with AI coding tools for data collection and synthesis.
  • Ben Carlson emphasizes that people skills, such as selling, effective communication, creativity, and providing context and perspective, will become more important than ever in an AI-driven world.
  • Jack Reins, reflecting on his past SPAC trading, cautions against using margin, stating that while it feels good on the way up, investors will eventually get margin called, leading to regret and potential significant losses.
  • Ben Carlson and Jack Reins agree that increasing one's income and contributions to a portfolio is a more effective way to build wealth over time than using margin, as it compounds and provides more resilience.

"My my actual take is unless you're really really committed to living in like New York, SF, Chicago, whatever your high cost of living city is, if you don't want to raise kids there, I actually just don't think you should stress too much about whether or not you can afford a home and just like focus on your career, focus on your personal life, and once you get married and you're thinking about having kids, you might have spent all of your 20s or early 30s in Manhattan, but then you end up moving to Jersey, Long Island, Connecticut, upstate New York, where it's just cheaper."— Jack Reins
