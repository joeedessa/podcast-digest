Dont Buy Real Estate in Canada Ron Butler and Jimmy Connor

Host: Jimmy Connor · Guest: Ron Butler, Mortgage Broker · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Ron Butler discusses the severe downturn in the Canadian real estate market, particularly in Ontario and British Columbia, with prices declining and expected to continue falling into next year. He highlights the collapse of the condo construction industry and the negative cash flow for condo investors due to falling rents and rising costs. Butler also critiques Canadian economic policies, particularly the decline of the manufacturing sector and the need for deregulation and tax reform.

Key discussion points

  • Ron Butler characterizes the real estate markets in Ontario and British Columbia as being in a "severe real estate downturn," with prices in "pure decline" that is expected to continue until "some point next year," followed by prices going "flat for perhaps two or three years."
  • Butler states that condo prices in the Greater Toronto Area (GTA) are down, with some pre-construction units sold in 2021 for $1,400 a square foot now being cleared for $725 a square foot, representing a "50% hit." He believes this is "probably about the bottom in terms of price," with a potential further drop of "another 5%."
  • Butler notes a "complete collapse of the construction industry" in both Ontario and British Columbia, projecting that by "March of '28, there will be absolutely no new units under construction" for condos in Toronto.
  • Butler explains that many new cranes in downtown Toronto are building "purpose-built rentals" supported by a CMHC program, offering "lowest possible rate" construction financing and "50-year amortization at the lowest available fixed rates in Canada" upon completion.
  • Butler highlights a reversal in population growth in Ontario and British Columbia, with "fewer Canadians here than there were a year ago," combined with an influx of purpose-built rentals, leading to a "recipe for rental price disaster" and "19 straight months of lower starting rents."
  • Butler advises against buying condos as an investment in the GTA for the next "two or three years," stating that pro formas "reveal negative cash flow" due to falling rents, rising maintenance fees, and increasing property taxes.
  • Butler states that new low-rise housing construction (single family, semi's, townhomes) in the GTA is at its "lowest starts...in the last 55 years," which "does lay the groundwork for the prices to stop dropping" sooner than high-rise products, though a "real recession" could change this.
  • Butler notes that the average first-time home buyer in Canada is now age 40, up from 27 in 1987, and that a family income of "$223,000" is needed to buy a low-rise home in the GTA today.
  • Butler criticizes the decline of the manufacturing sector in Canada, particularly in Ontario, stating that manufacturing jobs are down from "27% of all jobs" in 1975 to "just a little over 10% today," and predicts "no automobiles assembled" in Southern Ontario in "10 years."
  • Ron Butler states that Prime Minister Mark Carney is "running the country as if he was a conservative prime minister," having "abandoned the entirety" of previous environmental policies and reversed "virtually everything that Pierre Poilievre advocated for."
