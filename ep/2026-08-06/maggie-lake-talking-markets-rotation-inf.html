Rotation Inflation Whats Next with Tony Greer

Host: Maggie Lake · Guest: Tony Greer, Editor of The Sailor's Edge · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Tony Greer observes a "bull market green light go" signal in equities, driven by the removal of uncertainties such as the Iran war's diminishing market impact, a significant dollar-yen intervention, and a clear stance from the Federal Reserve under Kevin Walsh. He highlights a rotation within technology, with internet stocks breaking out, while maintaining a cautious view on semiconductors. Greer also discusses the long-term bullish thesis for gold and the improving outlook for financials, despite current high valuations.

Key discussion points

  • Tony Greer notes a "big breakout day" in the S&P 500 to 76.25, attributing it to the market's removal of uncertainties, including the Iran war's reduced impact, the dollar-yen intervention, and the Fed's clear communication.
  • Greer identifies a rotation within the technology sector, with internet stocks showing a "huge breakout" and two-sigma rallies in airlines and software, while semiconductors, after a previous breakout, are now in a "little bit of a decline."
  • He expresses respect for Fed Chairman Kevin Walsh's communication style, particularly his directive to "play the ball, not the referee," which Greer interprets as letting inflation data guide market expectations and potential Fed action.
  • Greer maintains a long-term bullish view on gold, stating that its fundamental story of "lower rates, more money printing, higher M2, yield curve control" remains intact, and its price will eventually go higher, regardless of short-term fluctuations around $4,000.
  • He discusses the fragility of the bond market, particularly concerning the Bank of Japan's interventions and potential treasury sales, but notes the market is "learning to keep pace with it."
  • Greer highlights the "crossfire in 2026," describing a series of rolling macro breakouts across different sectors, including metals, oil services, semiconductors, and oil, making it a challenging environment for traders.
  • He expresses a bullish view on the financials sector (XLF), noting that stocks "look great" and are benefiting from higher interest rates and AI, but he is waiting for a better entry point.
  • Greer views Bitcoin as a "slow bleed lower," suggesting it will likely settle in a $25,000 to $75,000 range, and notes that MicroStrategy becoming a net seller is a significant bearish signal.
