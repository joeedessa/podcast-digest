A 20B Blowup A War-Sized AI Bet Was the Bottom Just a Margin Call Last Call

Host: Matt Zeigler, Jack Forehand · Guest: Jim Paulson (Chief Investment Strategist, The Leuthold Group), Ben Hunt (Chief Risk Officer, Perscient), Brent Kachuba, Cameron Dawson, Dave Nadig

Overview

This episode of Last Call discusses market turbulence despite stable indexes, driven by high-beta tech and semi volatility. Jim Paulson argues that lagged effects of restrictive economic policies will hit a vulnerable stock market, predicting a correction in "new era" stocks. Ben Hunt updates his "World War AI" thesis, positing that massive AI CapEx will lead to a command economy due to capital and energy crowding out. Brent Kachuba analyzes the "Situational Awareness" fund blowup, suggesting the subsequent bounce in AI names was a liquidation-driven event rather than a fundamental shift.

Key discussion points

  • Jim Paulson identifies two core problems for the stock market: significant vulnerabilities after a prolonged bull market (S&P 500 50-60% above post-WWII trend, record high trailing earnings and profit margins, record low unit labor costs relative to unit price, record high household equity holdings, near-record low household cash) and the delayed impact of restrictive economic policies.
  • Paulson's economic policy index (50% 10-year Treasury yield, 25% WTI crude oil price, 25% U.S. real trade-weighted dollar index, inverted scale) shows a 13-week lag before impacting the S&P 500's growth rate, currently pointing "directly south."
  • Paulson notes a 17-week lag between his policy indicator and bond market performance, where initial tightening hurts bonds, but eventual economic slowdown causes yields to fall and bond prices to rise.
  • Paulson's policy indicator suggests a one-year lag before impacting PMIs, with PMIs expected to "crater" by January next year, indicating a likely economic slowdown following financial market struggles.
  • Ben Hunt's "World War AI" thesis projects $4-5 trillion (inflation-adjusted) in AI CapEx and data center build-out in the U.S., comparable to World War II spending, with half of 2026 GDP growth coming from AI CapEx.
  • Hunt warns that financing this AI build-out, initially from cash flow and now through borrowing and equity sales (e.g., Google), is heavily reliant on private equity and private credit, creating systemic financial crisis risk if the "hamster wheel" stops.
  • Hunt highlights a "crowding out effect" on capital, leading to structurally higher interest rates, and on energy consumption, with data centers projected to increase from 4% to 25% of U.S. electrical production, necessitating rationing or higher prices.
  • Brent Kachuba details the liquidation of the "Situational Awareness" fund (estimated $20-24 billion), which held concentrated AI-type bets, leading to a "margin call" situation and subsequent acquisition of its portfolio by Citadel.
  • Kachuba observes a "massive rip" in SA's portfolio names (e.g., BE, DRAM sector names like SanDisk) on Thursday morning, with some up 20%+ in a single session, which he attributes to the forced liquidation and subsequent buying rather than fundamental shifts.
  • Kachuba expresses concern that the violent bounce in AI names is being misconstrued as a fundamental bottom, arguing it was an "artificial buy" driven by the liquidation, not a sustained bid, and that macro factors (rates, Iran war) are increasingly important.
  • Dave Nadig airs a grievance about "deregulation by de-staffing" at the SEC and CFTC, where a lack of commissioners prevents quorum and decision-making, increasing systemic risk without formal rule changes.
  • Cameron Dawson's grievance is against the misinterpretation of S&P 500 operating margins, arguing that the current expansion (15.7% in Q2, up 300 bps from Q2 2025) is due to "operating leverage boom" from semiconductors (EBIT margins up 28 percentage points to 44% in 2026 vs. 2025) rather than a sustainable "productivity boom."
  • Jack Forehand's grievance is with YouTube algorithms that push for "bearish macro videos" and extreme content, hindering efforts to cover a broad range of topics.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Jim Paulson: The S&P 500 is vulnerable due to being 50-60% above its post-WWII trend, record high trailing earnings and profit margins, and record low unit labor costs. Restrictive economic policies (rising bond yields, dollar, oil prices) have a lagged effect, suggesting a coming market downturn. He expects a "full bear market" in "new era" tech stocks (over 20% decline) but a more moderate 10-15% correction for the broader S&P 500, with "old era" sectors holding up. A leadership shift away from technology is anticipated to persist into next year.
  • Ben Hunt: The massive AI CapEx build-out (comparable to WWII spending) is a structural driver of the economy, but it's creating a "crowding out effect" on capital and energy. This will lead to structurally higher interest rates and potential energy rationing, pushing governments towards a "command economy" with backstops, direct financing, and price controls to avoid a systemic financial crisis.
  • Brent Kachuba: The recent violent bounce in AI-related stocks (e.g., BE, DRAM sector) was likely a "dead cat bounce" driven by the forced liquidation of a large fund ("Situational Awareness") and subsequent buying by entities like Citadel, rather than a fundamental shift or a true bottom. Investors should be cautious about misinterpreting such price action as a sign of sustained recovery, as macro factors (rates, geopolitical tensions) are reasserting their importance.
  • Dave Nadig: "Deregulation by de-staffing" at regulatory bodies like the SEC and CFTC is creating systemic risk and volatility by hindering their ability to function and respond to crises.
  • Cameron Dawson: Current S&P 500 operating margin expansion is primarily an "operating leverage boom" in semiconductors, not a sustainable "productivity boom." This makes margins vulnerable to a "precipitous fall" when the semiconductor cycle peaks, even if other parts of the market experience genuine AI-driven productivity gains.
