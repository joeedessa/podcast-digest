Chris Whalen Warshs Credibility Problem Golds Real Signal Why Your Annuity May N

Host: Julia La Roche · Guest: Chris Whalen, Chairman of Whalen Global Advisors, Author of The Institutional Risk Analyst Blog · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Chris Whalen discusses Federal Reserve credibility under Kevin Warsh, arguing Warsh needs to take more decisive action, including rate hikes, to confirm market signals and address the growing deficit. Whalen also highlights the importance of physical gold in Eastern markets versus Western paper markets and raises concerns about the solvency of annuities due to irresponsible reinsurance practices by some large insurance companies.

Key discussion points

  • Kevin Warsh's Fed Chairmanship: Chris Whalen views Kevin Warsh as the "anti-Volcker," lacking the necessary "drama" and power projection to be credible. Whalen believes Warsh is "overly cautious" and needs to "pick up the sword at some point" to assert the Fed's influence.
  • Fed Policy Recommendations: Whalen suggests Warsh should "take back those rate cuts from last year" and implement "two quarter point rate hikes over the next 6 to 12 months" to confirm what the bond market is already signaling. He also recommends raising margin requirements on Wall Street and continuing to shrink the balance sheet.
  • Gold as Money vs. Paper Price: Whalen, referencing Keith Weiner, explains that in the Far East (Turkey, India, China), 24-karat gold is treated as money, often held physically, whereas in the US, gold is primarily traded on price as a "paper chase."
  • Annuity Solvency Concerns: Whalen introduces Thomas Gober's work on the insurance sector, highlighting that some large insurance companies, particularly those controlled by private credit firms, may be "short funded" on annuities due to irresponsible offshore reinsurance practices, potentially putting annuity holders' retirement funds at risk.
  • Portfolio Allocation and Specific Holdings: Whalen discusses his personal portfolio, which includes mortgage REITs (Annaly, Rhythm Capital), precious metals (18-19%, including junior miners), energy stocks (Chevron, Williams), and specific bank stocks (Schwab, Flagstar, Citi preferreds).
  • Commercial Real Estate Lending: Whalen notes that while large banks are re-entering commercial real estate, Bank of Ozarks is shrinking its exposure. He supports Bank of Ozarks' model of lending for construction and development at the beginning of the asset lifecycle and then exiting.
  • SpaceX Position: Whalen maintains his position in SpaceX, bought at the IPO price, viewing it as a "long-term punt on Elon Musk" and an "option" on the company's effectiveness in space and potential future ventures like competing with bandwidth providers.
