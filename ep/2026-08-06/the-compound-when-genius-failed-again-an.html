When Genius Failed Again Animal Spirits 476

Host: Michael Batnick, Ben Carlson · Solo episode

Overview

Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson discuss the recent market volatility, including the blowup of Leopold Aschenbrenner's hedge fund, Situational Awareness, and its impact on semiconductor stocks. They analyze the resilience of the broader market despite these events and persistent macro concerns, with Ben Carlson highlighting the "behavior gap" in retail investing and Michael Batnick questioning the Fed's influence outside of crises. The hosts also touch on the ongoing bull market in nostalgia and the societal implications of AI.

Key discussion points

  • Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson discuss the hedge fund Situational Awareness, run by Leopold Aschenbrenner, which blew up due to excessive leverage despite correctly identifying the AI boom. They note the fund's assets surged from $9.3 billion in March 2026 to $45 billion by July 2026 before its public levered book was sold to Citadel at a 10% discount.
  • Ben Carlson highlights the "behavior gap" in retail investing, citing the DRAM ETF, which saw $26 billion in net inflows but resulted in investors losing $1.6 billion on average because they invested after significant gains and before a crash.
  • Michael Batnick argues that the Fed's influence is often overstated, stating, "I think it's basically astrology for middle-aged men that talk about the Fed's forecasts. I think the Fed really honestly besides in a crisis doesn't do much." He believes the Fed only truly matters as a lender of last resort during crises.
  • The hosts observe that despite two wars, gas price spikes, sticky inflation, rising long bond yields, hedge fund blowups, and crashes in software and semiconductors, the S&P 500 is at all-time highs, indicating the market's resilience.
  • Ben Carlson notes the increasing weight of technology equities in the market, now above tech bubble levels, but argues this is logical given technology's growing role in the economy and the superior quality of current tech companies compared to past bubbles.
  • Michael Batnick points out that "degenerate behavior" in the market, such as meme stock speculation and leveraged semiconductor trades, is being punished, while the "legitimate bull market is reaching new all-time highs."
  • The discussion covers the collapse of the South Korean KOSPI index, which dropped 40% in five weeks due to semiconductor-related liquidations, affecting 3.5% of the population with margin calls, while foreign investors bought the dip.
  • Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson discuss the "bull market in nostalgia," citing Google Trends data for live events, vinyl, boomboxes, and 1990s culture, and the success of movies like "The Odyssey" and the potential for a "Home Alone" reboot with Macaulay Culkin.
