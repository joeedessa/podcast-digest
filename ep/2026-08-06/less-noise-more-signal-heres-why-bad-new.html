Heres Why Bad New No Longer Tanks Bitcoin

Host: Pascal Hügli · Guest: Johan Burkman, (no title stated), The Bitcoin Layer (TBL)

Overview

Pascal Hügli and Johan Burkman discuss the recent Coldcard exploit and its implications for Bitcoin self-custody, emphasizing the need for improved security practices and the potential for multi-signature solutions. They also explore the current state of the Bitcoin market, debating whether a bottom has been reached, considering factors like miner capitulation, on-chain metrics, and the influence of AI capital rotation. Burkman expresses a re-adjusted bullish outlook on Bitcoin, while Hügli remains cautiously optimistic.

Key discussion points

  • The Coldcard exploit is philosophically devastating for Bitcoin, challenging the core tenet of self-custody and leading to feelings of betrayal among Bitcoiners, as stated by Johan Burkman.
  • Pascal Hügli notes that the Coldcard incident, unlike FTX, affects those who followed best practices for self-custody, highlighting human fallibility even in trustless systems.
  • Johan Burkman suggests that the Coldcard exploit could lead to improvements in self-custody practices, emphasizing the "don't trust, verify" ethos and the need for better code review and peer collaboration.
  • Pascal Hügli argues that self-custody is not for everyone, and institutional-grade custody solutions are a valid option, especially for large sums, citing his experience with hardcore Bitcoiners using bank custody.
  • Johan Burkman differentiates Bitcoin's utility based on user problems: IBIT for debasement, Lightning Network for remittances, and self-custody for "freedom money" and borderless movement.
  • Pascal Hügli expresses concern about the long-term network security of Bitcoin if a critical mass (e.g., 50%) holds their Bitcoin with custodians, potentially centralizing the asset like gold.
  • Johan Burkman views the market's reaction to custody issues as a pendulum swing, with the community constantly seeking the "right percentage" of self-custody given evolving technology and AI threats.
  • Johan Burkman believes that strong encryption at Bitcoin's base layer is resistant to AI brute-forcing, but second-layer applications built on Bitcoin may introduce more attack vectors.
  • Pascal Hügli and Johan Burkman discuss the absence of a major miner capitulation in the current bear market, suggesting that miners' diversification into AI revenue streams might make them more robust.
  • Pascal Hügli tracks the realized cap in a "bottoming range" of $53k to $73k, noting that 25% of the realized cap now sits in this zone, which historically signals a bottom.
  • Johan Burkman's base case since late February was a Bitcoin bottom in the $60k range in 2026, equating it to the $20k range in 2022, with a possibility of $48k-$50k in a black swan event.
  • Johan Burkman observes a bullish divergence in the 30-day 25-delta options skew, where price is lower but the skew is higher (less negative), indicating reduced demand for puts relative to calls.
  • Pascal Hügli notes that the Bitcoin funding rate has turned positive across exchanges, suggesting that longs are paying shorts, which he interprets as a sign of disbelief towards the end of a bear market.
  • Pascal Hügli tracks Bitcoin's beta, downside beta, and correlation to an AI basket, observing an improvement (lower sensitivity, less downside, falling correlation) over the last 60 days, suggesting potential capital rotation.
