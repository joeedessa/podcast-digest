The Growth Strategy Trapping The Fed Darius Dale

Host: Felix Jauvin · Guest: Darius Dale, Founder of 42 Macro · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Darius Dale discusses the current macro regime, which he terms "Paradigm C" (running the economy hot) with a "sprinkling of Paradigm D" (printing money to address bond market issues). This environment is leading to bubble-like conditions in equities and challenges in the bond market. Dale argues that while top-down models suggest things are "fine," the market experiences significant dispersion and volatility, making it a harder environment for beta strategies compared to alpha strategies. He also details the implications of a rising R* and the Federal Reserve's policy choices under Chair Kevin Warsh, particularly regarding the bond market and the dollar's stability.

Key discussion points

  • Darius Dale identifies the current macro regime as "Paradigm C" (running the economy hot) since April 2025, with a "sprinkling of Paradigm D" (printing to counteract bond market issues) since December 2025, leading to bubble-like equity conditions and bond market challenges.
  • Darius Dale's global macro risk matrix indicates a "risk-on reflation market regime" with a 71% strength of signal, suggesting a favorable short-to-medium-term outlook for stocks, gold, Bitcoin, and commodities, but not for the dollar and bonds.
  • Darius Dale notes that R* (the real neutral policy rate) has risen by 50-75 basis points over the past 3-4 months, now ranging from 1.47% to 1.78% in the US, narrowly above the effective real Fed funds rate of 1.21%. This implies a deteriorating supply-demand balance for global capital, favoring capital providers.
  • Darius Dale explains that the market pricing R* above the real effective Fed funds rate signals that the Federal Reserve has not caught up to the economy, suggesting investors should allocate capital to assets yielding more than Treasury bonds.
  • Darius Dale's Fed decision tree model suggests the Fed should remain on hold, despite some signals (federal deficit spending, public debt monetization, Taylor rule deviation, NU relative to unemployment) that would typically suggest tightening.
  • Darius Dale believes there is a "surprisingly dovish outcome heading for financial markets at some point in the next call three to six months" from the Fed's five task forces, which is not fully priced into the forward rates curve.
  • Darius Dale's model indicates a fair value for the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.8% and the 30-year Treasury yield "well north of 6%," primarily by adding a normal level of term premium (long-run mean prior to GFC was 1.88%).
  • Darius Dale attributes Kevin Warsh's appointment as Fed Chair to the need for a "credible dove in Hawk's clothing" to stabilize the US dollar and the bond market, given the significant rise in gold as a share of global FX reserves and the dollar's decline.
  • Darius Dale highlights that Federal Reserve policy since Volcker has become progressively easier from both a policy rate and balance sheet standpoint, leading to a bond market that is now competing with AI capex and infrastructure build-out for capital.
  • Darius Dale defines success for the US economy as "staying in paradigm C," which is the "grow phase" of dealing with debt, as cutting (Paradigm B) risks political instability and printing too much (Paradigm D) risks civil war.
  • Darius Dale argues that the removal of forward guidance by the Fed, while initially disliked by the market, is ultimately beneficial as it injects volatility into the interest rate curve, reduces capital misallocation, and narrows the distribution of probable economic outcomes.
  • Darius Dale emphasizes that 42 Macro's systematic investment approach, using the KISS model portfolio and Dr. Mo risk management overlay, aims to create a "positively skewed return distribution" to compound wealth faster by managing risk and avoiding large drawdowns.
