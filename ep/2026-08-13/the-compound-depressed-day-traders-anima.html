Host: Michael Batnick, Ben Carlson · Solo episode · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson discuss the current market environment, noting the S&P 500's numerous all-time highs despite various global concerns, largely attributing this resilience to the AI trade and significant capital expenditure by hyperscalers. They also delve into the changing landscape of emerging markets, the psychology of retail trading and gambling, and the ongoing housing market dynamics.

Key discussion points

  • The S&P 500 has hit 26 new all-time highs this year, exceeding the average of 21 since 1990, which Michael Batnick finds surprising given global headlines like war and higher gas prices.
  • Michael Batnick attributes the market's "Teflon" nature and continued ascent primarily to the AI trade and the massive capital expenditure by hyperscalers (Amazon, Google, Meta, Oracle, Microsoft), which has surpassed $3 trillion on a rolling three-year forward basis.
  • Ben Carlson highlights that despite the S&P 500's strong performance, only 45% of companies are outperforming the index year-to-date, suggesting a challenging environment for stock pickers, though sectors like energy, materials, and industrials are seeing high outperformance rates.
  • Emerging markets are trading at a record discount to the S&P 500 (9.9x vs. 20x forward earnings), with Taiwan and South Korea now making up 45-46% of the EM index, and technology comprising 41%, making EM a "pure AI play" according to Ben Carlson.
  • Retail participation in South Korean equity markets has more than doubled to 28% since 2020, but 77% of household assets are in real estate, 9% in cash, and only 3.6% in equities and bonds, indicating a significant runway for stock market interest from younger generations in other countries.
  • Michael Burry's recent crash call, predicting a possible 1987-type fall, is discussed, with Ben Carlson agreeing that an "air pocket situation" (down 7-12% day) is more probable due to market speed, but not a 1987-style crash.
  • A study found that one-quarter of men aged 18-29 trade stocks daily, with two-thirds reporting feeling like failures, which Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson attribute to the negative-sum nature of daily trading and gambling.
  • The US housing recession, which began in Q1 2022, is believed to be over by Connor Sen, despite the industry experiencing brutal conditions, as the Case-Shiller National Home Price Index is up almost 12% since Q1 2022.
  • Elon Musk's ambitious predictions for SpaceX, such as scaling the economy of the Earth a thousand or even a million times through lunar mass accelerators, are noted for their blend of visionary thinking and frequent non-fulfillment.
  • Disney's Toy Story franchise has generated $16 billion in income, with $1 billion in annual global retail sales, yet Michael Batnick notes the stock has "stunk" despite the company's strong brand reach and accelerating theme park revenue growth.
  • The labor force participation rate for those 55 and older is dropping significantly, which Bank of America interprets as a positive sign of early retirement due to increased wealth from housing and stock portfolios, rather than people giving up on the workforce.
  • A Columbia School of Business professor estimates AI infrastructure investment at 2.8% of GDP, suggesting the US economy would be in a recession without AI, a view Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson largely agree with, though they note the money would have gone elsewhere.
