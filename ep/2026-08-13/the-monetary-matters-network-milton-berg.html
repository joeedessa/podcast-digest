Overview

Milton Berg discusses his current 100% long market positioning, initiated on July 29th and 30th, following a period of being short. He highlights numerous buy signals from late March and April, projecting significant upside for the S&P 500, with median projections between 8,000 and over 10,000. While bullish on the broader market, Berg expresses caution regarding the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) and the KOSPI, anticipating a potential retest of their July 29th lows within the next month or two, and remains long with caveats. He also reiterates his long-term bearish view on gold and silver, having sold his personal holdings on the day of their January peak.

Key discussion points

  • Current Market Positioning: Milton Berg is 100% long, having covered shorts and gone long on July 29th and 30th. His portfolio includes 5% long KOSPI (Korea), nearly 10% long Russell 2000, 5% long S&P mid-caps, 10% long NASDAQ 100, 20% long SOX, and nearly 50% long S&P 500 (via SPY).
  • July 29th Low and Positive Divergence: Berg identifies July 29th as a panic low, noting the S&P 500 did not make a new low, creating a positive divergence against the June 9th low. This, combined with a "Montgomery cycle date" on July 29th, supported his bullish turn.
  • Short-Term Caution for SOX and KOSPI: Despite being long, Berg is cautious about the SOX and KOSPI, which experienced significant declines (28.73% and 43.93% respectively). He expects these indices to rally, but anticipates a retest of their July 29th lows within the next one to two months, citing historical precedents like the 1987 crash.
  • Exhaustive Gaps and Negative Divergences: Berg points to "exhaustive gaps" in the NASDAQ 100 and KOSPI, which did not lead to sustained upside, and negative divergences where the S&P 500 made new highs but other broad indices did not. These suggest a potential short-term top or a retest of lows.
  • April Buy Signals and S&P Projections: Berg details multiple buy signals generated from March 31st through April 27th. One signal on March 31st, which occurred only once before (at the COVID low on March 24th, 2020), projects a median S&P 500 target of 8565.42 within a year, representing a 10.42% gain from current levels (adjusted conservatively by Berg). Other signals project median gains of 12.27% to 32.08% for the S&P 500.
  • Distinction Between Market Tops and Bottoms: Berg emphasizes that calling market bottoms is significantly easier and more reliable than calling market tops. He states that market bottoms are often V-shaped and characterized by panic selling, while tops are typically rounded and harder to pinpoint.
  • Gold and Silver Bearish View: Berg maintains a long-term bearish view on gold and silver, believing they made a multi-year top in January. He sold his personal precious metals on January 29th, the day of the high. He notes that gold relative to crude, housing, and CPI was at extreme levels.
  • Long Bond Outlook: Berg views long bonds as "way cheap" and expects yields to rise to 6-8% as a "normal fluctuation." He believes the Federal Reserve allowed yields to reach "crazy" lows (e.g., 0.69% for the 30-year bond) and that the market is now normalizing.
  • Retail vs. Institutional Models: Berg's retail product, Milton Berg Edge, aims for simplicity, with investors going long on the first buy signal and exiting if the market declines 8%. This model has historically generated 18.4% per annum. Institutional models are more sophisticated, seeking "perfect signals" with no real drawdowns.
  • Fed Policy and Inflation: Berg believes the Fed will be forced to raise rates and potentially engage in quantitative tightening to combat inflation, which he views as a monetary phenomenon, not driven by oil prices.
