Roads Power Lines and a Permitted Mill Phoenix Metals Brownfield Advantage

Host: Trevor Hall · Guest: Nick Campbell, CEO, Phoenix Metals · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Nick Campbell, CEO of Phoenix Metals, discusses the company's strategy to revitalize British Columbia's Boundary Region through brownfield copper-gold exploration. Phoenix Metals has consolidated a historically fractured district, acquired assets out of receivership, and secured significant backing and First Nation support. Campbell emphasizes the potential for large-scale open-pit targets and the strategic advantage of existing infrastructure and a permitted mill for a potential near-term restart of underground operations.

Key discussion points

  • Phoenix Metals' unique formation involved Chris Loader (President of Asisko Gold), Jeff Stibbard (founder of JDS Energy and Mining), and Chief Clarence Louie of the Osoyoos Indian Band, providing geological, mine-building, and crucial First Nation permitting support.
  • The company acquired its core assets, including a 200-ton-per-day permitted mill and tailings facility, out of the receivership of Golden Dawn Canex, which had previously consolidated the district but faced mismanagement issues.
  • Phoenix Metals has raised $46 million and holds $50 million in the bank, planning an extensive 55,285-meter drill program focused on defining open-pittable, near-surface bulk tonnage targets, particularly at the Phoenix and Lexington target areas.
  • Nick Campbell believes there is potential for "a billion tons of very good grade copper gold mineralization at a strip ratio of less than three to one" within the district.
  • The Lexington mine target has 170 historical holes and is targeted for 150 to 200 million tons of potential open-pittable material, grading 0.3 to 0.5 g per ton gold and 0.25 to 0.5% copper.
  • The Phoenix target area, an extension of a historic pit, is a 1.5 km long, 750 m wide copper-gold skarn system, with the historic pit running 0.9% copper and 1.14 g per ton gold. The JD Golden Crown zone within Phoenix shows high-grade potential, with historic drilling returning 3.5 m of 429 g per ton gold and 2.6 m of 196 g per ton gold.
  • Phoenix Metals is considering a restart of the Lexington underground mine, which has a 2026 43-101 compliant indicated resource of 424,000 tons grading 6 g gold and 1% copper. This restart is estimated to cost around $12 million Canadian and could generate $3 to $4 million per year in free cash flow, funded by non-dilutive sources.
  • The project benefits from exceptional infrastructure, including multiple power lines, a natural gas pipeline, and rail access, and can operate year-round due to mild winter weather conditions, avoiding typical Canadian winter shutdowns.
  • Metallurgical test work on the Lexington and Selskaren areas indicates good recoveries (around 90% for copper, 75-85% for gold) and a clean concentrate (over 25% copper concentrate grade) with no deleterious elements, historically purchased by Sumitomo.
