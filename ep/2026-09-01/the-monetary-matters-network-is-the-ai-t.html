Is the AI Trade Over Why July Wasnt a Blowoff Top Jeff Keller

Host: Max Wiethe · Guest: Jeff Keller, Founder and Portfolio Manager of Capeite Partners · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Jeff Keller, founder of Capeite Partners, discusses the current state of the AI trade, arguing that the recent volatility in Q3 2026 is not a blowoff top but rather normal market behavior within an S-curve of AI adoption. He highlights a divergence in tech sector performance, with software and services up 20% in Q3, while semiconductors and semiconductor equipment are down 7.5%. Keller suggests that while retail involvement signals caution, the fundamental, open-ended growth story of AI differentiates it from past speculative bubbles.

Key discussion points

  • AI Adoption and Market Volatility: Jeff Keller believes AI adoption is still moving upwards on an S-curve, and the volatility seen in Q3 2026 is normal, not indicative of a blowoff top for the mega-trend. He notes that the "bulls would say, hey, Apple services is still growing double digits today. Google search is still growing double digits today. Meta is growing in the 20s."
  • Software Sector Divergence: Keller distinguishes between application software and infrastructure/cyber security software. He views the bounce in application software as a recalibration from previous premium valuations, while infrastructure and cyber security have seen significant rallies due to short-term certainty and perceived AI threats.
  • Valuation and Time Horizon: Keller emphasizes the danger of relying solely on multiples for highly cyclical businesses in tech. He looks for "three-year visibility into into stocks and valuation" and prioritizes "a two to three-year, you know, through cycle earnings multiple with decent downside protection."
  • Dampened Feedback Loop for AI Announcements: Max Wiethe observes that market reactions to AI-related partnership deals and capital raises have become significantly dampened. Jeff Keller attributes this to "a lot more sobriety in the trade" and increased scrutiny on whether announcements will generate "sustainable long-term profits."
  • Retail Involvement as a Cautionary Signal: Jeff Keller states that "whenever you get heavy retail involvement in any trade, uh that usually in my experience has meant that the clock is ticking for the end of that trade." However, he differentiates AI due to its "open-ended growth story."
  • Hyperscaler Capex and ROI: Keller views hyperscaler spending on AI as an "existential" and "defensive option" rather than solely a return on investment calculation. He believes they will "keep spending because the risks to stopping, I think, are much greater than the optionality you buy by at least continuing to stay in the game."
  • Meta's AI Strategy: Jeff Keller interprets Meta's messaging about potentially renting out excess compute capacity as a signal to investors that "we get it and we will stop if need be. We will release our capacity if need be," rather than a fundamental shift in their long-term AI commitment.
  • Data Center Buildout and Politics: Keller views concerns about the politics around data centers and potential moratoriums as "overstated." He believes that "they're I think they're going to get built" despite short-term political headwinds.
  • Fund Management Philosophy: Jeff Keller describes his fund as "long biased," with "more than 50% net exposure." He favors an "old school stock picking" approach with a longer duration, not constrained by being perfectly factor-balanced, believing it offers an advantage in current markets.
  • Conditions for a Bearish Tech Stance: Keller states it's "unlikely I would ever get net short." He identifies three key factors to watch for a potential crack in the AI trade: "the lab ARS," "the capex," and "the price of compute." He does not see these signals yet.
  • AI's Impact on Market Efficiency: Jeff Keller disagrees with the idea that AI will remove fear and greed from markets, arguing instead that "AI probably enables a lot of shortcuts. It enables a lot more confidence. I think that it's going to create a lot more behavioral inefficiency."
  • Post-Earnings Focus: Looking ahead to the end of 2026, Keller will focus on the ARR of AI labs and open source adoption, capex trends (especially for 2027 and 2028), and forward prices of compute as indicators for the AI trade.
