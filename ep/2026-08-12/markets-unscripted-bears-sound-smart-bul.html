Bears Sound Smart Bulls Make Money

Host: Matt Caruso, Jason Shapiro · Solo episode · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Matt Caruso and Jason Shapiro discuss the current bull market, emphasizing that it extends beyond just AI stocks to broader asset classes and earnings. They caution against a perpetually bearish mindset, arguing that the market's upward drift over time makes a long bias more advantageous. Jason Shapiro highlights specific crowded long positioning in the Dow and a potential false breakout in the S&P 500 as the only current bearish arguments, while Matt Caruso points to strong underlying liquidity and investment, particularly AI-backed, as drivers for continued market strength.

Key discussion points

  • Jason Shapiro notes that the current market is a bull market "everywhere for the most part," including gold, silver, earnings, and M2, making it difficult to fade.
  • Jason Shapiro identifies the Dow as showing "maximum crowded long" positioning, based on large speculators' activity, but stresses that this is a warning signal, not a guarantee of a downturn.
  • Jason Shapiro views the recent S&P 500 breakout as potentially a "false breakout" due to excessive bullish sentiment from "people that I don't trust" and a subsequent reversal day.
  • Matt Caruso argues that AI is the "ultimate productivity tool" driving a "productivity boom" that will filter into the broader economy, similar to the internet in the 1990s, and is a key factor in current strong earnings.
  • Matt Caruso suggests that the trillions of dollars flowing into the US for AI-backed investment act as a "massive stimulus," which is less transparent than traditional monetary policy but significantly impacts liquidity and the credit cycle.
  • Jason Shapiro criticizes the "AI bubble" narrative, pointing out that stocks like Nvidia and Taiwan Semi are trading at a "22 forward PE" after recent pullbacks, making a bubble argument difficult.
  • Jason Shapiro highlights that the AI profit boom is broadening, citing "garbage truck margin boost shows the AI profit boom has begun" as an example of AI's impact beyond tech giants.
  • Jason Shapiro and Matt Caruso discuss the psychological bias towards bearishness, noting that "bears sound smart, bulls make money," and that a consistent short bias often leads to missed opportunities over time.
  • Jason Shapiro states that the market will signal when a bull market is at risk, specifically when it "no longer going up on good news" or "doesn't go down a lot on bad news."
  • Matt Caruso emphasizes that deeper market corrections are "almost always credit driven," and with credit conditions currently strong, a deep correction beyond recent pullbacks is less likely.
  • Jason Shapiro notes that if the bearish story about money printing and debt is true, "it'll show up" in market action, such as a failed bond issuance or a change in character of leading stocks.
  • Matt Caruso highlights that gold stocks, despite a long-term bullish narrative around money printing, did "nothing for 15 years" before a new leg higher, illustrating the importance of actionable real-time trading over long-term narratives.
