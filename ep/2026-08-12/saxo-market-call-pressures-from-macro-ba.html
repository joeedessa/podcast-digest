Pressures from macro backdrop building ahead of US CPI

Overview

John J. Hardy and Ole Hansen discuss the building macro pressures ahead of the US CPI release, focusing on the impact of El Niño on commodities, the geopolitical situation in Iran on crude oil prices, and the implications of rising US Treasury yields on gold, the Japanese yen, and equities. Hansen highlights the complexity of El Niño's impact on commodity production and the ongoing supply limitations in the oil market due to geopolitical tensions. Hardy notes the resilience of gold despite higher yields and the vulnerability of the Japanese yen to rising US rates and oil prices.

  • Ole Hansen states that demands from the US and Iran are "so far apart in terms of finding a solution," leading to continued limited oil supplies. He highlights that the market is currently borrowing oil from strategic reserves, but eventually, higher prices will be needed to kill demand.
  • Ole Hansen observes that the refined product market is experiencing the most distress due to locked-in refinery capacity in the Middle East and recent attacks on Russian refineries, leading to higher margins for oil majors with refinery assets.
  • John J. Hardy notes that the 30-year US Treasury yield is poking at highs since 2007, reaching 5.28% today, and expresses concern that rising yields may not support the dollar and could impact broader risk sentiment, especially in equity markets.
  • Ole Hansen explains that gold has traded sideways despite aggressive rate hikes in 2022-2023 due to robust central bank buying and continuous strong imports into China, which provides an underlying level of demand. He notes that gold has broken out above $4,000 and is consolidating at a higher level.
  • John J. Hardy highlights that the Japanese yen is suffering further weakness, with Dalian trading well above 159, and suggests that Japan needs a lower US CPI number, lower treasury yields, and lower oil prices to calm the situation.
  • John J. Hardy discusses the underperformance of AI hardware stocks like Coherent (down 14%) and Lumentum (down 8.6%) yesterday, noting their extreme volatility and upcoming earnings reports, while software-as-a-service names like Adobe are recovering.
  • John J. Hardy mentions NVIDIA's consortium with six companies, including Apollo, Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, Brookfield, and KKR, for a half-a-trillion-dollar financing deal for AI build-out, raising concerns about "hidden leverage" in the system.
