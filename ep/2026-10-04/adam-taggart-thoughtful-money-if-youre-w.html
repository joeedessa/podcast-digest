If Youre Worried About A Bear Market Buy Bonds Now Lance Roberts

Host: Adam Taggart · Guest: Lance Roberts, Portfolio Manager, RIA Advisors · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Adam Taggart and Lance Roberts discuss the current economic landscape, focusing on the implications of recent payroll and PCE data, the impact of higher interest rates on various market sectors, and the strategic role of bonds in a portfolio. Roberts argues that while major indices appear strong due to tech performance, underlying market sectors are showing stress from higher rates, and bonds currently offer a compelling investment opportunity for income and capital preservation, especially given current yields and disinflationary trends.

Key discussion points

  • Dr. Thomas Hoenig's perspective on fiscal discipline: Adam Taggart reports that Dr. Hoenig, former Kansas City Fed CEO, believes fiscal discipline in Washington is essential to address debt issues and that the Fed's role is to refuse to monetize debt, forcing Congress to control spending.
  • Lance Roberts' view on fiscal discipline and economic growth: Roberts counters that calls for fiscal discipline are perennial and often overlook that much economic growth stems from government spending. He argues that the Fed's rate hikes primarily impact consumer borrowing, pushing the economy towards recession to curb demand-side inflation.
  • Productive vs. non-productive debt: Roberts suggests that if government spending continues, it should focus on productive investments like upgrading the electrical grid to support AI data centers, which would generate future revenue and strengthen economic growth.
  • AI's return on investment: Taggart highlights the uncertainty surrounding AI's return on investment, questioning whether current spending is justified in the near term or if significant benefits are 15-20 years away, requiring a re-evaluation of today's pricing.
  • Underweighting international markets: Roberts explains that RIA Advisors remains underweight international and emerging markets due to their dependence on US consumption, slower economic growth rates, and lack of investment in future-oriented sectors like AI, particularly in countries like Germany and France.
  • PCE revisions and Fed policy: Roberts notes that recent PCE revisions brought the average inflation rate down to 1.6%, suggesting the Fed may be making a mistake by hiking rates, as inflation outside of oil price spikes is not significantly increasing.
  • Labor hoarding and disinflation: Roberts points to weak employment reports and labor hoarding as disinflationary signals, indicating that companies are retaining existing employees but not hiring new ones, which will weigh on consumer spending over time.
  • Market dispersion and sector performance: Roberts illustrates that while market-cap-weighted indices like the S&P 500 and NASDAQ are near all-time highs, equal-weighted indices, small caps, mid-caps, international stocks, and sectors like real estate, utilities, and staples are significantly underperforming due to higher interest rates.
  • Q4 market outlook: Roberts predicts a move higher in markets through year-end, driven by the return of corporate stock buybacks, strong earnings, and potential gridlock in midterm elections, which Wall Street typically favors. He expects a rotation from growth to value, with small caps and mid-caps potentially outperforming the broad market.
  • Bonds as a "gift": Roberts views current bond yields (e.g., 5% on 5-year bonds) as a "gift" that investors should take advantage of, arguing that bonds provide capital preservation and predictable income, covering a significant portion of portfolio return goals with less equity risk.
  • Distinction between individual bonds and bond funds/ETFs: Roberts emphasizes that buying individual bonds allows investors to hold to maturity and receive face value, regardless of price fluctuations, whereas bond funds and ETFs are trades on interest rate direction and do not mature.
  • Municipal bonds: Taggart and Roberts discuss the attractiveness of municipal bonds, particularly for tax-exempt income, noting that state-specific munis offer exemption from state income taxes in addition to federal.
