Hormuz Hyperscalers the Market

Host: Steve Duttenhofer, Jeff deGraaf, Steve Pavlik · Guest: Michael Allen, Managing Director at Beacon Global Strategies, Host of the NATS Matters podcast · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode discusses global geopolitical tensions, particularly the situation in the Strait of Hormuz and US-China relations, with Michael Allen. The hosts also cover domestic economic issues, including the impact of data centers on local communities and the upcoming US midterm elections. Jeff deGraaf provides an update on the market cycle clock and factor momentum, while Steve Pavlik outlines key policy events for the upcoming week.

Key discussion points

  • Michael Allen believes that for the remainder of President Trump's term, there will be "muddling through" regarding the Strait of Hormuz, with no major outcome expected until after the midterm election.
  • Michael Allen notes that the Iranians believe Trump is more likely to compromise on the economic blockade before the election, as they perceive him as sensitive to losing both houses of Congress.
  • Michael Allen expresses surprise that oil prices are not higher given the conflict in the Strait of Hormuz, attributing it to China's use of reserve oil and demand destruction, as well as expanded pipeline capacity bypassing the Strait.
  • Michael Allen suggests that the current Iranian leadership is "more hardcore and more willing to do things that they would never have done in the past" following the killing of the Supreme Leader, viewing the conflict as an existential threat.
  • Michael Allen states that expectations for the upcoming Trump-Xi meeting are low, with success defined as merely having a "meeting that looks good," partly due to US vulnerability in critical minerals.
  • Michael Allen discusses the political backlash against data centers, noting 250-500 anti-data center ordinances or laws passed since May, driven by concerns about power, water, and job displacement, with foreign adversaries potentially stirring the pot.
  • Michael Allen is concerned about the US's military capabilities, highlighting the need to dramatically rebuild capacity to be a player in at least three theaters (Middle East, Ukraine, Taiwan) given the high level of global threats.
  • Michael Allen observes that China is keeping Russia afloat and helping Iran, with these countries, including North Korea, working together against the US to challenge its global dominance.
  • Jeff deGraaf explains that the RenMac market cycle clock indicates an "elevated inflation zone with a middling growth trajectory," which historically correlates with annualized S&P 500 performance of around 56 basis points, compared to a historical average of 93 basis points.
  • Jeff deGraaf notes that while the market cycle clock suggests caution, corporate credit markets are "still very very well bid," indicating that concerns are not yet hitting aggregate demand.
  • Jeff deGraaf states that factor momentum, specifically the difference between high and low rate-of-change stocks, which was in the 100th percentile in June and May, has "crashed," going from an 84% return to a negative 54% return in a 65-day period.
  • Jeff deGraaf predicts that momentum as a factor "probably isn't that important for the next six months" due to expected sloppiness and a longer grinding environment for sentiment to reset.
  • Steve Pavlik highlights that Tuesday is the effective date for Canadian reciprocal tariffs, with potential last-minute negotiations, and suggests watching the Quebec election on October 5th as a factor in Canadian negotiating tactics.
  • Steve Pavlik notes that the first-ever GOP midterm retreat in Dallas, Texas, with President Trump as keynote speaker, will offer Trump a national platform to draw policy contrasts, which may also benefit Democrats by making the election a referendum on Trump.
  • Steve Pavlik estimates Democrats have an 85-95% chance of taking the House in the midterms, but expects a narrow majority due to redistricting, making governance difficult. He gives Republicans a 55-60% chance of holding the Senate, with Michigan and Maine being key states to watch for Democrats.
