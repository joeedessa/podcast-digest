Overview

This episode is a co-hosted discussion between Kevin Muir and Patrick Ceresna, focusing on recent market events and their implications. Patrick Ceresna highlights the Treasury's bond intervention as a significant signal that the administration is concerned about rising yields, leading to a decisive bullish pivot in gold and Bitcoin. Kevin Muir, however, views the bond market's backup as a natural response to increased supply and a return to normal interest rates, expressing skepticism about the Treasury's intervention and its long-term efficacy. They also discuss the outlook for the S&P 500, the semiconductor sector, and various commodities including copper, natural gas, uranium, and grains.

Key discussion points

  • Patrick Ceresna interprets the Treasury's bond intervention as a "Treasury driven twist" that, despite its small size, signaled the administration's discomfort with high yields, leading to significant moves in gold and Bitcoin.
  • Kevin Muir argues that the bond market's rise in yields is a natural consequence of record bond issuance across corporate, government, and non-US government sectors, and that current term premiums are merely a "return to normal."
  • Kevin Muir believes that the market's reaction to the Treasury's intervention, particularly in gold, indicates a concern about the government potentially monetizing debt, and that the Treasury Secretary should not be "playing games that traders play."
  • Patrick Ceresna notes that the dollar's breakdown following the bond intervention neutralized its prior bull breakout, potentially trapping it in a 15-month trade range for months to come.
  • Kevin Muir, citing the "currency whisperer," states that the Bessant announcement was a clear signal to sell US dollars, reinforcing a US dollar bear view.
  • Kevin Muir asserts that gold is in a secular bull market, and the Treasury's intervention provided the necessary catalyst for its recent decisive bullish move.
  • Patrick Ceresna observes that while Nvidia's earnings were strong, the lack of a strong follow-through in other semiconductor stocks and hyperscalers (Mag 7s) is concerning for the broader market's ability to reach new highs like S&P 8,000.
  • Kevin Muir suggests that the muted response in other tech stocks indicates that "everyone's already long" and there are no new buyers, making further upside difficult.
  • Patrick Ceresna highlights that copper is at the 99th percentile of large speculator positioning, driven by decisive long buying, indicating a crowded but fundamentally supported bullish consensus.
  • Patrick Ceresna points out that natural gas is at the 0 percentile of positioning, with gross shorts at five-year highs, making it a "hated market" and potentially setting up for a "widowmaker" short squeeze, especially in winter gas contracts.
  • Patrick Ceresna notes that uranium, after a five-month flatline, broke out in sync with gold, mirroring its parabolic blowoff and correction, raising the question of a new uranium bull market.
  • Patrick Ceresna reports an "epic bull breakout" in grains (wheat, corn, soybean) over the last two weeks, with egg stocks also starting to wake up, suggesting a potential surprise bull market in food commodities.
  • Kevin Muir and Patrick Ceresna agree that crude oil is unlikely to go lower than its current "higher shelf" of $80-90, making energy stocks a strong bullish play with potential for a 30% run this year, while oil itself remains more neutral.
