AI Trade Dead Money For Now - But Macro Overall Is Pretty Risk-On For Stocks Tia

Host: Jack Farley · Guest: Tian Yang, Co-founder and Head of Research at Variant Perception · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Tian Yang of Variant Perception discusses the current state of the stock market, particularly the AI trade, which he believes is "dead money for a while" despite a generally "risk on" macro environment. He explains that while AI is real, the narrative has run its course, and the market is broadening out into other sectors. Yang also delves into Variant Perception's proprietary models, geopolitical themes, and specific investment preferences across global markets.

Key discussion points

  • Tian Yang believes the AI trade, particularly in semiconductors, is "dead money for a while" due to excessive money inflow and a narrative that has run its course, comparing it to Bitcoin since 2025 or gold this year after its crash.
  • Yang states that Variant Perception's macro risk indicators, which look forward 3 to 6 months, suggest a "risk on" macro regime, with growth, inflation, policy, and liquidity broadly supportive of risk assets, leading to a broadening out of the equity market.
  • Yang highlights the "primacy of sovereignty" as a key investment theme for the next 5 to 10 years, driven by US-China competition across energy, manufacturing, and technology, suggesting government involvement will sustain capex longer than expected.
  • Yang expresses skepticism about Europe due to energy and regulatory issues, and about India due to its geopolitical vulnerability between the US and China, preferring Latin America and seeing potential in Japan after a necessary currency shock.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Tian Yang believes the macro environment is "risk on" for equities, supported by growth, inflation, policy, and liquidity indicators, with the equity market broadening out.
  • Yang's firm uses a log periodic power law (LPPL) bubble and crash detection system, which triggered sell signals for semi-AI related stocks in early June and buy signals for laggards like China and Indonesia.
  • Yang notes that while intraday downside exhaustion signals exist for semis and NASDAQ, daily frequency or medium-term signals are not yet present.
  • Yang suggests that the "agentic AI" narrative is "over as a growth factor of the semiconductor AI trade," and a new narrative, possibly involving "real world modeling," is needed for new highs.
  • Yang's macro risk models indicate US real growth is likely between 1.5% and 2%, closer to 2%, and inflation is high but not overly so, unlike in 2022.
  • Yang highlights two top-down factors for generational market tops: monetary policy tightening for 6-9 months and narrowing equity breadth; neither is currently met.
  • Yang sees the "primacy of sovereignty" as a long-term investment theme, driven by US-China competition, which will likely keep capex going longer than expected due to government involvement.
  • Yang is skeptical of a Chinese consumer comeback, arguing that China's export-oriented, sovereignty-focused strategy crowds out the household sector.
  • Yang believes Chinese AI will accelerate price compression and destroy profit pools in the industry, similar to China's impact on manufacturing.
  • Yang views Japan's macro situation as "ridiculously untenable" due to high inflation, wage growth, and real estate prices, coupled with an unresponsive BOJ and weak currency, suggesting a "Plaza Accord type event" is needed.
  • Yang believes gold has found a floor and will trade sideways, serving as a stagflation hedge, but requires central bank rate cuts to truly rally. He prefers gold over nominal bonds.
  • Yang is bullish on other metals, expecting more geopolitical shocks to drive upside exposure.
