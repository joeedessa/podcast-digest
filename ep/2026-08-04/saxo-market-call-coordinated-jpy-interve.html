Overview

This episode of Saxo Market Call focuses on the coordinated intervention in the Japanese yen by Japanese and U.S. authorities, aiming to strengthen the JPY. John J. Hardy also discusses the "clearing of the boards" in the AI hardware space following the transfer of Leopold Aschenbrenner's hedge fund portfolio to Citadel, and previews upcoming earnings reports from key AI-adjacent companies. He explores contrasting views on AI's future, from "endless growth" to "reverse centaur" frameworks.

Key discussion points

  • Coordinated JPY Intervention: The Ministry of Finance in Japan, in coordination with U.S. authorities, intervened to strengthen the Japanese yen. The U.S. supported this by selling euros versus the yen, signaling support for Japan's currency stabilization efforts rather than a statement on the dollar's valuation.
  • "Situational Awareness" Hedge Fund Blow-up: The transfer of Leopold Aschenbrenner's large, leveraged portfolio to Citadel is seen as a "clearing of the boards" for the market, particularly in Korean memory names and AI-linked stocks, after a "traumatic deleveraging" event.
  • Contrasting AI Visions: John J. Hardy discusses Aschenbrenner's view of AI leading to "endless growth" and super intelligence, versus Cory Doctorow's "reverse centaur framework" which posits AI will concentrate power and devalue human labor, and extreme views like "If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies."
  • Upcoming Earnings and Market Dynamics: Key earnings reports are expected from Palantir, AMD, Western Digital, and SanDisk. John J. Hardy is watching for a potential slowdown in negative correlation among SaaS names and a recovery in Mag7 and hyperscalers, as well as the AI hardware space's reaction post-volatility.
  • NVIDIA's "Circular Financing Trade" and CDS: John J. Hardy notes a theme of "circular financing" centered on NVIDIA, with significant capacity going to pre-IPO startups like Anthropic and OpenAI. NVIDIA's CDS prices have doubled from 40 to over 80, an indicator to track despite still implying low default odds.
