The Bond Market Is Pricing In Something Worse Than Inflation Weekly Roundup

Host: Felix Jauvin, Quinn · Solo episode · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode of Forward Guidance, a "Weekly Roundup" format, features hosts Felix Jauvin and Quinn discussing recent market volatility, inflation data, and the Federal Reserve's policy stance. Quinn notes the market's "crosscurrents" including inflation data, the AI trade, and positioning. Both hosts agree that while the long end of the bond market may have seen its peak in yields, a significant growth slowdown or equity crash would be required for yields to go much lower. They also discuss the implications of the "AI trade" showing "dents" and its impact on broader economic growth and financing conditions.

Key discussion points

  • Quinn observed "weeks of record bond shorts all across the market" prior to the recent inflation data, indicating stretched positioning.
  • Felix Jauvin noted that despite hot CPI and PPI data, bond yields rallied, and the probability of an October Fed hike remained low (20-30%), suggesting Fed speak successfully talked the market off a hike.
  • Quinn believes the Fed's actions have made an October hike "not really live," which has "important ramifications for path on risk" as it removes a "biggest growth risk."
  • Felix Jauvin highlighted that the "AI thing" is the primary driver of top-line economic growth, and "dents" in this trade, such as slowing Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) for Anthropic, have significant "trickle-down effects" on data centers, semis, and memory.
  • Quinn explained that the AI trade's demand for capital financing has pushed up rates, and when it cracks, access to capital is inhibited, reducing forward supply expectations of AI-related debt, which can lead to lower yields.
  • Felix Jauvin and Quinn discussed the "aggressive rotation between small cap, large cap," with Quinn noting the Russell index being down for "eight weeks in a row" while tech selling for "three or four days" causes widespread concern.
  • Quinn views the recent bond market move as a "shot across the bow, the warning sign," indicating de-grossing and de-risking ahead of well-telegraphed events.
  • Felix Jauvin referenced a take from "Efficient Market Hyp" comparing the recent bond rally to the stock rally after the hot CPI print in October 2022, suggesting a "vibe shift" but not necessarily a sustained trend.
  • Quinn stated that the debt of companies like CoreWeave is "probably money good" because hyperscalers like Nvidia could easily backstop them, but equity holders would likely face dilution.
  • Felix Jauvin and Quinn discussed "debasement trades" like gold and Bitcoin as potentially "cleaner bets" in an environment of government backstops and artificial demand, but acknowledged the risk of a "correlation to one shock."
  • Quinn believes there's a "window" for real economy stocks (housing, equal weight) to perform well in the short term if the AI trade blows up and rates fall, but questions their long-term viability given underlying economic issues.
  • Felix Jauvin cited a chart from "Ahon from Prometheus" showing that the "bite that a rate hike has is not nearly as strong anymore" due to the indebted economy and fixed long-term debt, suggesting rate hikes might even be "stimulative to the economy" in terms of net incomes.
  • Quinn disagreed with the notion that rate hikes are not net tightening, arguing that their effect is "obfuscated by all the other bajillion intervention measures" and that they contribute to the "imbalancing of the K and the imbalancing of the economy."
  • Quinn predicted "absolutely going to be re-escalation" in the oil and Iran situation, but suggested that the timing of such events might be manipulated in the "lead up in the 2 weeks to the midterms."
