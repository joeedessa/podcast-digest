Its a Bubble So Why Is Citis Dirk Willer Buying

Host: Meb Faber · Guest: Dirk Willer, Head of Global Macro Asset Allocation Emerging Market Strategy at Citi · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Dirk Willer discusses Citi's definition of a market bubble, concluding that US markets entered bubble territory in mid-2025. Despite this, Citi's strategy is to "buy it" while acknowledging an eventual "payback time." Willer outlines the factors supporting continued equity strength, particularly in the US, and identifies potential hedges and long-term opportunities in fixed income and select emerging markets.

Key discussion points

  • Dirk Willer defines a bubble as an asset going up more than two standard deviations against its long-term trend in real terms, a price-based definition borrowed from GMO.
  • Citi's view is that US markets entered bubble territory around mid-2025, but the strategy is to "buy it" because bubbles can continue for extended periods, though they eventually "end badly."
  • Willer notes that the "last Fed hike is a problem" for bubbles, but the current Fed hiking cycle is unlikely to be far enough along to impact the bubble, as the first and last hikes rarely coincide.
  • Sentiment, as measured by the American Association of Individual Investors, is "quite bearish," which is unusual for a bubble and suggests it may not be near its peak.
  • Willer expects a strong year-end rally in equities, particularly in November and December, due to strong seasonal patterns that are amplified in good years.
  • The S&P 500 has shown impressive resilience, largely ignoring rising interest rates, with Willer noting that "the level of rates almost doesn't matter for equities," but rather the volatility of the sell-off (MOVE index).
  • Citi's "War Shadow Rate" indicates a hawkish bias from the Fed, suggesting they are not close to overtightening, which implies it's too early to buy bonds.
  • The Treasury Secretary has "a fair amount of firepower" to influence the back end of the curve, potentially by issuing fewer coupons and more T-bills, which could force the Fed to buy the front end.
  • Long-term investors might find real yields on 30-year bonds (around 3.3% real) interesting, especially given the expectation of a "meaningful setback" in equities over the next five years.
  • Willer believes that when the equity bubble bursts, the Fed will cut rates "very aggressively," potentially back to zero, and would likely buy the back end of the curve in a deflationary bust.
  • Citi's dollar model, with components including relative equity performance, rates, terms of trade, and macro data momentum, currently shows a "three to one favoring the dollar."
  • Willer is positive on Brazil due to its idiosyncratic nature, expected market-friendly government, and potential for aggressive rate cuts from high real rates (9-10%).
  • Emerging Asia, specifically Taiwan and Korea, are viewed positively as part of the AI story, though Korea's recent retail-driven ramp-up and subsequent fall suggest caution.
  • Gold has a "clear bull case" in the "very big picture" due to global central bank diversification away from the dollar, but in the short term, rising US real rates make it unattractive.
  • Willer's "generals indicator" for exiting bubbles involves selling when four of the top seven S&P names (identified annually) break below their 200-day moving average, aiming for an earlier exit than fundamental shifts.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Dirk Willer (Citi) believes US markets are in a price-based bubble but advocates buying it, expecting continued performance for another year, with an eventual severe "payback time."
  • Willer expects a strong year-end equity rally in November and December 2026, driven by seasonal patterns.
  • Willer suggests that the S&P 500 can continue to ignore rising interest rates, as equity performance is more sensitive to treasury market volatility (MOVE index) than rate levels.
  • Willer views the Fed as having a hawkish bias, implying it's too early to buy bonds, but anticipates aggressive rate cuts and Fed buying of the back end of the curve when the equity bubble bursts.
  • Willer sees long-term value in real yields on bonds, particularly for investors with a 5-year horizon, as a hedge against an eventual equity market setback.
  • Willer maintains a dollar bullish bias, supported by relative equity performance, widening US rates, and terms of trade.
  • Willer is positive on Brazil equities due to idiosyncratic factors, a market-friendly government, and potential for significant rate cuts.
  • Willer is positive on Emerging Asia (Taiwan, Korea) as part of the AI story but advises caution on Korea due to recent retail-driven volatility.
  • Willer is cautious on European equities due to lack of AI exposure, energy shock sensitivity, potential ECB overtightening, and political headwinds.
  • Willer sees a long-term bull case for gold driven by central bank diversification but advises against it in the short term due to a hawkish Fed and rising real rates.
  • Willer likes copper due to strong global data momentum and structural demand from AI and energy transition.
  • Willer suggests credit as a good hedge against an equity bubble burst and recession, given tight spreads and endless supply.
