Bearish Into November Room to Run After Why Dan Niles Is Watching Hyperscaler Cr

Host: Justin Carbonneau, Jack Forehand · Guest: Dan Niles, Founder and Portfolio Manager at Niles Investment Management · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Dan Niles discusses the current state of the AI revolution, comparing it to past industrial revolutions and the internet bubble. He believes AI represents a significant overinvestment but that the current valuations, particularly in semiconductors, are not as stratospheric as during the dot-com era, suggesting a potential 30-50% pullback rather than a complete meltdown. Niles expresses short-term negativity on the market leading up to the November 3rd midterms due to political risks, September seasonality, and potential Fed rate hikes, but sees another year of strong growth for AI stocks.

Key discussion points

  • Dan Niles believes AI is a transformative technology, leading to overinvestment, but current valuations (e.g., Nvidia at 15x P/E) are not as extreme as the dot-com bubble (e.g., Cisco at over 100x P/E). He anticipates any semiconductor meltdown to be limited to 30-50%.
  • Niles monitors token production and cost, noting a 50% drop in token costs since May, offset by a 2.5x increase in tokens produced. He also watches the accelerating revenue growth and expanding operating margins of the big three public cloud vendors (Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud Platforms) as indicators of AI's profitability.
  • The liquidation of "situational awareness" fund due to 4x leverage and simultaneous losses on long (semiconductors) and short (software) positions created a "cataclysmic bottom" in the market, which Niles identified as a short-term bottom on July 29th.
  • Niles highlights political risk as a short-term concern for AI, specifically local opposition to data center construction, which could prolong the buildout. He is "pretty negative" between now and the November 3rd midterms due to this and other factors.
  • He predicts that value in the AI stack will shift from model providers (like OpenAI and Anthropic) to infrastructure providers and ultimately to companies building great businesses on top of cheap tokens, similar to how the internet infrastructure enabled companies like Google and Amazon.
  • Niles expresses concern about China's state-sponsored entry into the semiconductor industry, particularly DRAM and NAND, citing CXMT and YMTC's aggressive expansion plans. He believes this could disrupt the "semiconductors are no longer cyclical" narrative.
  • He identifies three "safe spaces" within software that should be less disrupted by AI: security, system of record companies, and entertainment/video game companies. Recent positive results from Atlassian, Workday, and Salesforce suggest a potential bottom in the broader software group.
  • Niles is closely watching credit default swaps for hyperscalers (Microsoft, Coreweave, Nvidia) as an indicator of market stress, noting that some are trading at premiums to the North American investment grade credit default swaps, indicating concerns about capital markets' ability to absorb AI capex debt.
  • He believes the Fed will raise rates on September 16th, followed by a pause on October 28th, due to sustained elevated inflation (65 months above 2% target) and Kevin Warsh's recent comments.
  • Niles emphasizes the importance of downside protection in investing, arguing against a "buy and hold" strategy due to the high risk of companies failing, citing examples like AOL, Yahoo, and Nokia. He advocates for strong conviction but loosely held beliefs, adapting as facts change.

Market & macro takeaways

  • He monitors AI's fundamental health by tracking token costs (down 50% since May) against token production (up 2.5x) and the accelerating revenue growth and expanding operating margins of major cloud providers (Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud Platforms).
  • Niles is short-term bearish on the market until the November 3rd midterms, citing September seasonality (historically the only down month for S&P 500 since 1957), political uncertainty, and the likelihood of a Fed rate hike on September 16th. He notes average peak-to-trough losses of 10% from July 31st to November 9th in midterm election years since 1990.

Recent positive earnings from Atlassian, Workday, and Salesforce suggest a potential bottom for the underperforming software sector (up 4% YTD vs. semis up 60% YTD).
  • He highlights rising credit default swaps for hyperscalers (e.g., Nvidia's CDS higher than average North American investment grade) as a warning sign about the capital markets' ability to absorb the increasing debt funding for AI capex.
