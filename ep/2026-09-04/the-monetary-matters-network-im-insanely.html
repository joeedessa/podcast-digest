Im Insanely Bullish on Bonds Jared Dillian on Copper Bonds Semis and The Awesome

Host: Jack Farley · Guest: Jared Dillian, Author of The Awesome Portfolio and Editor of The Daily Dirt Nap · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Jared Dillian expresses extreme bullishness on bonds, arguing that current high yields represent an "incredible deal" and that market sentiment regarding deficits and inflation is misguided. He also discusses his bearish outlook on semiconductors, healthcare, and financials, believing many charts in these sectors are topping. Dillian introduces his new book, "The Awesome Portfolio," which advocates for a diversified approach to retirement investing across stocks, bonds, gold, cash, and real estate to reduce volatility and drawdowns.

Key discussion points

  • Jared Dillian believes current market sentiment on bonds is a "mind virus," with fears of out-of-control deficits and inflation being overblown. He points to declining inflation, weak economic data (payroll reports, Chicago PMI, Jolts, ISM), and historical deficit levels (6% of GDP now vs. 12% in 2010) as evidence.
  • Dillian is "insanely bullish" on bonds, seeing 5.2-5.3% on 30-year Treasuries and 4.7% on 10-year Treasuries as "incredible deals." He has personally moved a large portion of his money into bonds for a 3-5 year long-term trade.
  • Dillian observes that charts for semiconductors, healthcare, and financials appear to be topping. He specifically mentions JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Wells Fargo, Johnson & Johnson, NVIDIA, and AMD as looking like they are topping.
  • Dillian views AI as a bubble, noting that tech is being financed with debt at high interest rates for assets with short lifespans, unlike the dot-com bubble which was equity-financed. He suggests that if AI unwinds, private credit will also be affected.
  • Dillian is "cautiously bullish" on gold, despite its recent break below the 200-day moving average. He believes a "super ugly jobs print" (e.g., negative 50,000 or 100,000) could reverse the current trade and make Kevin Warsh's recent hawkish comments appear dovish.
  • Dillian maintains his bearish stance on private credit and alternative investments, stating that the unwinding will take a long time due to illiquidity in private markets and that the bottom has not yet been found.
  • Dillian introduces "The Awesome Portfolio," a retirement investment strategy diversified equally across stocks, bonds, gold, cash, and real estate, which he claims offers significantly lower volatility and drawdowns (max 12% vs. 40% for S&P 500) with only a 1-2 percentage point reduction in performance.
