Fed To Punch The Economy In The Gut This Fall Darius Dale

Host: Adam Taggart · Guest: Darius Dale, CEO and Head Pumbah, 42 Macro · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Darius Dale argues that the Federal Reserve will be forced to "punch the economy in the gut" by tightening monetary policy, likely via balance sheet reduction and rate hikes, to regain bond market credibility and address rising neutral rates. This action, while potentially causing a 1998-style market correction, is necessary to set up future structural dovishness. John Llodra and Mike Preston of New Harbor Financial discuss current market breadth, sector rotation, and the potential for a blow-off top, while also highlighting a potential bottoming in precious metals.

Key discussion points

  • Darius Dale believes the Fed will be forced to tighten monetary policy, potentially with 1-2 rate hikes and balance sheet reduction, to address a rising market-implied neutral rate and regain bond market credibility.
  • The current economic growth, driven by consumer spending and AI CapEx, is largely in interest-rate insensitive sectors, making traditional rate hikes less effective and suggesting balance sheet tightening as a more targeted approach.
  • A "geopolitically driven supply and demand imbalance" in the Treasury bond market, characterized by increased government deficits and AI CapEx demand against dwindling global savings, is pushing up neutral rates globally.
  • Darius Dale forecasts a 1998-style market correction (15-19% drawdown) in the next 2-4 months if the Fed tightens, but expects the uptrend to resume afterward, provided the Fed doesn't "kill the business cycle."
  • The U.S. federal government's Q3 and Q4 2026 quarterly refunding announcements show a significant increase in bill issuance and Treasury buybacks, which Darius Dale suggests could "bail out the financial markets" and potentially avert a significant fall correction.
  • John Llodra notes that New Harbor Financial's indicators are "strongly bullish" and that market breadth is broadening beyond mega-cap tech, with small caps showing improved fund scores since mid-January.
  • New Harbor Financial sees the current market as potentially in a "blow-off top" scenario, and while they are underweight equities (50% in their core tactical model), they would aim to capture some of the upside while using options for hedging.
  • Mike Preston observes early indications of a trend change in precious metals, noting silver's recent 4.5% daily gain and gold's $170/ounce jump, suggesting a potential "bottom fish" entry for traders.
  • Mike Preston believes that gold mining stocks (GDX) could see "another double" from current levels in the next 1-2 years, following a significant correction.
  • John Llodra advises investors to use the summer "doldrums" to evaluate financial goals and planning, particularly considering Roth conversions and potential Medicare surcharges (IRMA).
