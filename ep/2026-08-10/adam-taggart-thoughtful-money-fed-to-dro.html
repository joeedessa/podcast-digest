Fed To Drop Market By 20 Darius Dale

Host: Adam Taggart, John Llodra, Mike Preston · Guest: Darius Dale, CEO, 42 Macro · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Darius Dale of 42 Macro discusses the rising neutral policy rate (R*) and the geopolitical supply/demand imbalance in the Treasury bond market, predicting the Fed will be forced to tighten monetary policy. He anticipates a short-term market correction similar to 1998, followed by a resumption of the uptrend. John Llodra and Mike Preston of New Harbor Financial provide their market outlook, noting a broadening of market breadth and early signs of a potential trend change in precious metals.

Key discussion points

  • Darius Dale believes the Federal Reserve will be forced to tighten monetary policy, potentially hiking the Fed funds rate once or twice and contracting its balance sheet, to regain credibility with the bond market and address the rising market-implied neutral policy rate (R*).
  • The current economic environment is characterized by a geopolitically driven supply and demand imbalance in the Treasury bond market, with increasing government deficits globally and massive AI capex demand competing for a dwindling growth rate of global savings.
  • 42 Macro's models (KISS and Dr. Mo) are signaling a defensive posture, indicating that the fundamental storm clouds Darius Dale observes are starting to impact institutional flows, suggesting a potential short-term market correction.
  • John Llodra notes a broadening of market breadth beyond mega-cap technology stocks, with small caps, healthcare, and financials showing improved "fund scores," which he considers a healthy development for the market.
  • Mike Preston observes early indications of a potential trend change in precious metals, specifically silver and gold, with recent strong price action suggesting a bottom-fish entry opportunity for traders, despite a brutal decline since late January.
  • Darius Dale highlights a potential subversion of the expected market correction through dovish net financing policy by the Treasury, with projected borrowing of $739 billion in Q3 2026 and $628 billion in Q4 2026, including significant Treasury buybacks.
  • The long-term sustainability of the current economic trajectory is questioned by Darius Dale, who argues it is sustainable in economic terms by crowding out the private sector but may not be sustainable in political terms due to growing discontent among younger generations regarding the "American dream."
  • New Harbor Financial's indicators are "strongly bullish," and they are largely invested, but they would look for a degradation in market breadth and weakening underneath the surface as signals to lighten up, even if indices are making new highs.
