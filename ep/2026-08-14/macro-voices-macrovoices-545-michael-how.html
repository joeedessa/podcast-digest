MacroVoices 545 Michael Howell Warsh vs The Markets

Host: Erik Townsend, Patrick Ceresna, Masil Bagnan · Guest: Michael Howell, Founder of Cross Border Capital and publisher of Capital Wars Substack, Global Liquidity Indices · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Michael Howell discusses the global liquidity cycle, which peaked in late 2025 and is now declining, driven by strong real economies crowding out financial markets. He argues that this "everything bubble" will eventually bust, leading to higher interest rates and a defensive stance in financial assets. Howell also highlights China's unique liquidity expansion and internal yuan devaluation as a key driver for gold prices, predicting a significant rally in gold and commodities, particularly energy.

Key discussion points

  • Global Liquidity Cycle: Michael Howell states that the global liquidity cycle, particularly in advanced economies, peaked at the end of 2025 and is now in a noticeable downturn. He expects the cycle to bottom around mid-to-late 2027, driven by strong real economies drawing liquidity away from financial markets, not central bank tightening yet.
  • Warsh vs. Markets: Michael Howell believes that if Kevin Warsh, as Fed Chair, attempts to resist market-driven higher interest rates, it will be an "unhappy relationship." He argues that the market, specifically the long end of the curve, controls interest rates, and rising bond yields due to strong nominal GDP growth will force the Fed to tighten policy.
  • Asset Bubbles and Busts: Michael Howell identifies the current market as an "everything bubble" fueled by endogenous liquidity creation within the financial system, where rising asset prices increase collateral values, leading to more borrowing and liquidity. He warns that "after every bubble comes a bust."
  • China's Liquidity Expansion and Gold: Michael Howell explains that China is embarking on a significant liquidity expansion to devalue the yuan internally and address its debt problem, which is driving up gold prices. He notes that the Shanghai Gold Exchange is now the marginal pricer of gold worldwide, and Chinese demand is fueling the rally.
  • Gold Price Outlook: Michael Howell is "100%" in agreement that gold has bottomed and is likely to rally, driven by China's historic debts and the West's future debts. He suggests that if the gold-oil ratio mean reverts to its historical average of 20x and gold is underpinned at $4,000/ounce, oil could reach $200/barrel, or $135/barrel at 30x.
  • Commodity Markets: Michael Howell sees commodity markets, especially energy, as attractive. He believes oil prices will go up significantly, contrary to the view that they will crash after the Strait of Hormuz crisis resolution. He is "very invested in energy stocks."
  • US Bond Market and Debt: Michael Howell predicts the 10-year US Treasury bond could test 6% yields due to accelerating nominal GDP growth (6-8% range) driven by fiscal spending, AI, and deglobalization. He suggests the Treasury may fund deficits by issuing more short-dated bills, which would be "really bad for the dollar" and cause gold to "shoot up."
  • Japanese Yen and Monetary Policy: Michael Howell notes that Japanese bond yields are rising, catching up with nominal GDP growth (over 4%). He attributes the yen sell-off to the Bank of Japan's reluctance to raise short-term interest rates, a situation he compares to the US Fed's potential dilemma.
  • US Fed Hiking Cycle: Michael Howell is "100%" certain of a Fed rate hike, citing the two-year Treasury note yield as a reliable predictor. He believes the Fed has "at least another six months of intensified tightening" before any inflection.
  • S&P 500 Outlook: Masil Bagnan notes the S&P 500's recent explosive move to new all-time highs, with bulls maintaining elevated levels. He suggests a short-term catalyst could push it to 8,000 within a week or two, with significant technical damage only occurring after a 400-point decline.
  • Nasdaq Lagging: Masil Bagnan highlights that the Nasdaq is lagging the S&P and Dow, with speculators still net short 35,000 contracts, indicating a potential long-short pairing against overextended semiconductor and MAG-7 exposures.
  • US Dollar Outlook: Patrick Ceresna observes the US dollar mean reverting its prior bullish trend, driven by yen intervention. He sees an "interesting inflection point" where the yen's continued rally or the dollar's breakdown will determine the next move.
  • Uranium Market: Patrick Ceresna notes that uranium has been "completely dead" for three months but is now upticking, with uranium stocks (URA) rallying almost 25% trough-to-peak, potentially signaling a new accumulation cycle.
  • Wheat Market: Masil Bagnan points out that large speculators went net short wheat by 15,000 contracts in one week, with 11,000 fresh new shorts, before the Black Sea escalation. He suggests that if the situation worsens, these traders may have to buy back their shorts.
