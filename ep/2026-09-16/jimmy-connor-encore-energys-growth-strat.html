enCore Energys Growth Strategy Bill Sheriff

Host: Jimmy Connor · Guest: Bill Sheriff, enCore Energy · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Bill Sheriff discusses enCore Energy's current operational status, including permitting delays and upcoming production from new wellfields. He outlines the company's growth strategy, focusing on major projects like Dewey Burdo and Alta Mesa East, and emphasizes the need for consolidation within the uranium sector to attract larger institutional investors. Sheriff also provides insights into the US domestic uranium market, particularly the implications of the DOE's request for information for defense programs.

Key discussion points

  • EnCore Energy experienced a permitting "snafu" that delayed production for three to four months, leading to an idle period in Q2. However, Bill Sheriff is optimistic that the timelines expressed were pessimistic and that 2027 will be a good year, with the trough of issues likely passed.
  • The US is the largest consumer of uranium, consuming 45 to 50 million pounds annually, but only produces approximately 3 million pounds. Bill Sheriff notes that this 3 million pound figure is a new high, with roughly 2 to 2.5 million pounds coming from ISR producers.
  • The "Fast 41" program, bolstered by the Trump administration, has significantly streamlined federal permitting for projects like enCore Energy's Dewey Burdo, which was fully permitted in under a year after being mired for 15 years.
  • Bill Sheriff advocates for mergers in the uranium sector, stating that companies need to be larger (e.g., 4-5 million pounds per year production) to attract major hedge funds and generalist investors who want to write $50 million checks without becoming insiders.
  • EnCore Energy's sales strategy aims to be no more than 50% contracted, with most contracts featuring floors and ceilings, providing a guaranteed minimum price and upside potential, along with annual inflation adjustments.
  • The DOE's request for information for 4 million pounds of US-sourced uranium annually from 2030 for defense programs highlights a significant bottleneck, as current US production is barely 3 million pounds and fully contracted. Bill Sheriff speculates this could lead to the use of the Defense Production Act to streamline mine development in less energy-focused states.
  • Bill Sheriff notes that while the spot uranium market has been "comatose" and "flat and totally unremarkable" for the first time in five or six years, the long-term contract price is at an all-time high, which is less transparent to investors.
  • EnCore Energy has over $70 million in liquidity and does not currently "need the money" from its recently announced $750 million shelf and $250 million ATM, but views it as preparation for potential mergers and acquisitions.
