Its a bull market and nobody drinks anymore TCAF 255

Host: Josh Brown, Michael Batnick, Ben Carlson · Guest: Todd Sohn, Chief ETF Strategist, Strategus and Strategus Asset Management · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode features Todd Sohn discussing the booming ETF industry, with a record number of new launches and issuers. The conversation covers the evolving landscape of ETFs, including thematic, leveraged, buffer, and option income products, and the impact of new entrants like Corgi on fee structures. The hosts and guest also delve into the valuation of sports franchises, the stickiness of cash in money market funds, and the future of sector investing and new asset classes like "compute."

Key discussion points

  • Todd Sohn notes that 2026 is the biggest launch year by ticker for ETFs, with 900 funds year-to-date, and the cost of launching and maintaining funds is much lower, making it easier for new issuers.
  • Josh Brown and Michael Batnick discuss the soaring valuations of professional sports franchises, with the Lakers valued at $12.4 billion and the Knicks at $9.9 billion, and the potential for MSGS stock to trade 30-50% higher after the Rangers spin-off.
  • Todd Sohn highlights the emergence of new ETF issuers like Corgi, a Y Combinator-backed company, which is launching numerous thematic and leveraged ETFs at significantly lower fees (35-50 basis points) compared to incumbents.
  • Michael Batnick predicts that prediction market ETFs, which bet on outcomes across various events, will become a significant category, with Subversive already filing for such products, though the SEC has currently paused them for comment.
  • Todd Sohn and Josh Brown identify buffer ETFs and option income ETFs as breakout categories, with Goldman Sachs acquiring Innovator and Neos, two major players in these spaces, to cater to aging demographics seeking yield and downside protection.
  • Josh Brown expresses skepticism about "ecosystem" ETFs, such as those targeting the "SpaceX AI lab ecosystem" or companies doing business with Nvidia, believing that investor interest in such niche themes will wane as market fascination with AI evolves.
  • Todd Sohn discusses Korea's regulatory tightening on leveraged single-stock funds, requiring a week of simulated trading for investors, contrasting it with the less restrictive US market where demand for leverage is quickly returning.
  • Michael Batnick and Todd Sohn observe that $3 trillion in retail money market funds remains "stuck," not flowing into the stock market despite its boom, and predict it would only unstick if Fed funds rates drop below 3% or in a massive stock market correction.
  • Josh Brown and Todd Sohn note the declining weight of consumer discretionary and staples in the S&P 500, with staples at 4.5%, reflecting a bull market where investors are less focused on defensive sectors.
  • Todd Sohn mentions that money is leaving crypto ETFs, with investors potentially shifting to AI stocks, and questions the proliferation of niche crypto products like 2x Dogecoin ETFs.
  • Todd Sohn explains that commodity ETFs, excluding gold, have historically struggled due to futures roll problems (contango) and tax issues (K1s), with active management like VanEck's RAX being an exception.
  • Josh Brown and Todd Sohn anticipate the launch of "compute" ETFs, which will track the price of computing power, following recent announcements of major partnerships in the AI compute space.
  • Todd Sohn predicts that some thematic funds will "fall on their face" due to market saturation and insufficient assets, but notes that unlike IPOs, a failed ETF doesn't necessarily hurt investors if it accurately tracks its underlying asset.
