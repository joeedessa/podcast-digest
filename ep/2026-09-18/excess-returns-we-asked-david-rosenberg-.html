We Asked David Rosenberg Why He Wants the Bonds Everyone Hates and Where Hes Hid

Host: Matt Zeigler · Guest: David Rosenberg, President and Chief Economist, Rosenberg Research · Listen at source ↗

Overview

David Rosenberg argues that the market has overreacted to inflation concerns, particularly regarding oil prices, and has excessively priced in Fed rate hikes. He believes the Fed will not ratify current market expectations, leading to a potential bull steepener in the Treasury curve. Rosenberg advocates for a defensive, diversified portfolio with exposure to bonds, specific equity sectors, and international markets, while being bearish on the US dollar and bullish on gold.

Key discussion points

  • David Rosenberg believes the market has gone "completely manic" in pricing in almost two Fed rate hikes by year-end and a 4.5% funds rate by the end of next year, contrasting with the Fed's March dot plots that suggested a rate cut. He expects the Fed's upcoming dot plots will not ratify these market expectations, leading to a "nice bull steepener" in the Treasury curve.
  • Rosenberg dismisses current inflation as primarily an "exogenous tax on the economy" from oil prices, noting a lack of inflation from the labor market (average hourly earnings trending lower at 3.1% year-over-year, a five-year low), housing (rents deflating, new home prices negative 1% year-over-year), and monetary factors (M2 growth alongside money velocity peaked in March).
  • He highlights that 90% of the increase in the 10-year Treasury note yield from its February 27th low (below 4%) has come from the real interest rate, with only 10% from market-based inflation expectations, indicating that the backup in yields is driven by "general elevated and rising level of policy uncertainty" rather than broad-based inflation.
  • Rosenberg anticipates a shift to fiscal gridlock after the November 3rd midterm elections, with a 90% probability of Democrats taking the House, which he views as "good for bonds" by ending "six years of unrelenting fiscal stimulus and deficits."
  • He expresses concern about the concentration risk in the S&P 500, where the top 10 stocks (mega-cap tech) account for over 40% of the index, and other sectors have become correlated with the generative AI trade. He suggests hiding in consumer staples and healthcare, which have the lowest correlations.
  • Rosenberg is fundamentally bearish on the US dollar, citing central banks reducing dollar reserves and increasing gold allocations, and a disconnect between dollar reserves and global trade. He notes his model portfolio is 70% inversely correlated to the US dollar.
  • He is bullish on gold as a long-term secular story, driven by a 1 percentage point gap between demand growth (2.5%) and supply growth (1-1.5%), and its inverse correlation with the US dollar and real interest rates. He views gold as a balance on the portfolio with no counterparty, coupon, reinvestment, or earnings revision risk.
  • Rosenberg learned "discipline" at Gluskin Chef, emphasizing the importance of cutting losses and admitting mistakes in portfolio management, rather than sticking with bad calls.

Market & macro takeaways

  • David Rosenberg believes the market has overshot in pricing Fed rate hikes, creating an opportunity in the Treasury market for a bull steepener. He sees current inflation as primarily an oil-driven supply shock, not broad-based demand-side inflation, with core goods CPI running at -0.6% year-over-year.
  • He anticipates fiscal gridlock post-midterm elections (November 3rd), which he views as positive for bonds by curbing fiscal stimulus.
  • Rosenberg is concerned about the S&P 500's concentration risk, with mega-cap tech accounting for over 40% of the index and high correlations across sectors due to the AI trade. He suggests defensive sectors like healthcare and consumer staples for US equity exposure.
  • He advocates for diversification into international markets, specifically Japan and Europe, due to better valuations, dividend yields, and less concentration risk compared to the US.
  • Rosenberg holds a bearish view on the US dollar, expecting a long-term downtrend driven by central bank reserve diversification into gold and a disconnect between dollar reserves and global trade.
  • He is bullish on gold as a long-term secular play, supported by central bank demand and its inverse correlation with the dollar and real interest rates.
  • His portfolio strategy emphasizes risk management, diversification, and minimizing volatility, rather than making binary bets on recession/no recession. He is positioned for slower nominal GDP growth and falling inflation.
