We Wont Die Wondering Alkanes Nic Earner

Host: Travis Ricciardo, JD · Guest: Nic Earner, Managing Director, Alkane · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode features Nic Earner of Alkane, discussing the company's diverse strategies for creating shareholder value, including de-mergers, acquisitions, strategic equity stakes, and exploration success across various commodities. Earner details Alkane's journey from a subscale miner to an ASX 200 company, highlighting key projects like Tomingley Gold Mine and the Boda discovery, and explaining the rationale behind its M&A and exploration approach. The discussion emphasizes the challenges and opportunities in the mining sector, particularly for mid-tier companies.

Key discussion points

  • Travis Ricciardo notes that Alkane has created shareholder value in many different ways, including de-mergers, acquisitions, strategic equity stakes, and exploration success across different commodities and strategies, leading to its inclusion in the ASX 200.
  • Nic Earner recounts his career before Alkane, including his time at Straits Resources starting up the Hillgrove mine and nearly nine years at Olympic Dam under WMC and BHP.
  • Earner explains that Alkane's early years involved running the Peak Hill gold mine, using cash for initial extraction at the Dubbo project and exploration, leading to discoveries that formed part of Tomingley Gold Mine and the rare earths at Dubbo.
  • Earner details the discovery of Boda in 2019, noting it was identified from tenements picked up from other companies that had drilled "sniffs" but found grades insufficient for their larger copper targets or commodity prices at the time.
  • Earner discusses the strategic decision to drill out Boda to an indicated resource, despite the $40 million cost, because "if you don't have an indicator resource you cannot progress approvals and if you don't progress because you have to do a study and justify you know and so if you don't so if you haven't done that you're going nowhere and if you don't have an approved project then in reality you don't really have anything eventually."
  • Earner explains the rationale behind the ASM de-merger in 2020, stating that investors were increasingly bifurcated, with some only interested in gold and others in rare earths, making it difficult to value the combined entity.
  • Earner describes Alkane's strategy of taking strategic stakes in developers like Kadus, Genesis, and Medallion, aiming to technically assist them, understand their projects, and potentially make an offer later, though this approach is now being reconsidered.
  • Earner reflects on the decision to hedge gold at Tomingley in March 2023 at a forward curve price of "2,800 bucks," acknowledging the massive opportunity cost given subsequent price movements but defending the pragmatic decision not to "bet it all on black."
  • Earner discusses the Mandandalay deal, initiated in late 2023, as a response to Alkane's share price stagnation and the need for another transaction to gain market recognition and improve liquidity, leading to a significant re-rate catalyst for Alkane.
  • Earner describes the Costfield mine, acquired through the Mandandalay deal, as a "narrow vein truly narrow vein mine" that processes about 150,000 tons of ore per year at an average grade of "8 to 12 grams a ton."
  • Earner highlights the advantages of the Swedish asset, including a corporate tax rate of "21%," a royalty rate of "2%," and power costs of "just a few cents a kilowatt hour," calling it "Mine is heaven, mate."
  • Earner emphasizes Alkane's exploration culture, inherited from Ian Charas and maintained by a long-serving team, which prioritizes geology and continuous allocation of funds to exploration, even during lean times.
  • Earner outlines Alkane's future strategy, acknowledging that outside of Boda, the company is "post growth" at its current 170,000 ounce level across three mines, and future growth in the "three-year odd gap" before Boda's FID (late 2029) must come from M&A, targeting construction-ready development assets or mergers.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Nic Earner notes that in the 2017-2019 period, developers struggled to secure funding, describing it as "a brutal, brutal time."
  • Nic Earner observes that the market often struggles to value companies with multiple commodities, especially if they are disparate, leading to "bifurcated" share registers.
  • Nic Earner states that the gold price in March 2023 on the forward curve was "like 2,800 bucks."
  • Nic Earner highlights that the Mandandalay deal was a "tremendous re-rate catalyst" for Alkane, radically improving its cost of capital and increasing its share price due to index inclusion and increased liquidity.
  • Nic Earner believes that bringing together companies of similar size and valuations, creating greater balance sheet flexibility and cash flow growth, is crucial for mining companies to consistently pay dividends, entertain buybacks, and address their perpetually declining asset base.
  • Nic Earner points out that Sweden offers a favorable mining environment with a corporate tax rate of "21%," a royalty rate of "2%," and power costs of "just a few cents a kilowatt hour."
  • Nic Earner notes a phenomenon in Canada where some people are "really freaking tired" and "happy to hand accountability over to people that are happy to have it," which can facilitate M&A.
  • Nic Earner identifies Australia, New Zealand, US, Canada, and Scandinavia as target regions for M&A.
