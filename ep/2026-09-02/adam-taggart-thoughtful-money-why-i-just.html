Overview

Adam Taggart, at age 55, discusses his first home purchase, detailing his personal journey, financial preparation, and the specific factors that led him to buy in South Reno, Nevada, despite his long-held skepticism about the housing market. Melody Wright, a housing analyst, interviews Taggart and provides expert commentary on the personal nature of homeownership decisions, the importance of due diligence, and navigating the new build process. The discussion emphasizes that Taggart's situation is unique and not a general recommendation for others to buy.

Key discussion points

  • Adam Taggart's long-term housing market skepticism stemmed from his experience in the early 2000s in Palo Alto/Menlo Park, where he found homes overpriced and was outbid, leading him to research and identify a housing bubble before the 2008 GFC.
  • Taggart intentionally sat out the housing market for years, saving diligently into a "home fund," which allowed him to be financially prepared to buy a home outright or with a very low mortgage in his mid-50s.
  • The decision to buy was driven by a desire to establish roots in Nevada after becoming empty nesters and a "forcing function" when their rental home was sold, necessitating a move.
  • Taggart's specific location in South Reno, Nevada, was chosen due to its perceived resistance to major price corrections, low inventory, influx of affluent Californians, and desirable amenities like proximity to Mount Rose ski area and Lake Tahoe.
  • Taggart sought expert advice from housing analysts, including Melody Wright and Nick Jurley, who indicated that his specific zip code in South Reno was likely to be relatively resistant to price declines and potentially increase in value.
  • The property Taggart purchased was a new build by Toll Brothers, chosen for its "fantastic view" which he considers a scarce, valued asset that will appreciate long-term. The specific unit became available due to another buyer moving to a different lot, offering a slightly lower price due to its pre-permitted, fixed layout.
  • Melody Wright emphasizes that homeownership is a personal decision, and individuals must conduct their own risk-reward assessment, ensuring they do not overextend themselves financially and are willing to accept potential losses over a given time period.
  • Taggart and Wright discuss the complexities of new builds, including the pressure of rapid decision-making, the value of hiring a design firm, and leveraging a builder-friend to identify cost savings and better options outside the developer's design center.
  • A key learning from the new build process is that developers like Toll Brothers often mark up design elements significantly, and using outside contractors for certain finishes can reduce the overall home price at delivery, thereby lowering property taxes permanently.
  • Taggart highlights the importance of understanding HOA regulations and the need for a realtor to be present at the first meeting with a new build developer to ensure commission is paid, noting that developers often base commissions on the base house price, excluding lot premiums and design upgrades.
  • Taggart plans to pay down his mortgage quickly, aiming to be mortgage-free within a couple of years, and is considering an adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) because he is confident he will pay it off before the rate adjusts.
  • The discussion covers the importance of title insurance, specifically an ALTA homeowners policy (enhanced or expanded policy), which offers broader protection than a standard lender's policy, covering risks that extend after home purchase.
  • Taggart notes the value of pre-wiring and other practical upgrades, even if not immediately used, as they can increase the resale value of the house and are often more difficult and costly to install after construction.
  • Both Taggart and Wright stress the importance of local involvement for new residents to influence the future development and character of their chosen area.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Adam Taggart views specific local housing markets, particularly in desirable areas experiencing an influx of high-net-worth individuals (e.g., Reno, Nevada, from California), as potentially resistant to broader national housing corrections.
  • Taggart highlights that scarce assets, such as properties with unique, desirable views, are likely to appreciate over the long term due to limited supply and increasing demand.
  • Melody Wright suggests that Adjustable Rate Mortgages (ARMs) are often misunderstood due to historical context and can be a suitable product for individuals who understand the terms and plan to pay off the loan before rate adjustments. She criticizes the "Henry Ford credit" approach to mortgages in the US.
  • Taggart emphasizes that reducing the official purchase price of a new build by sourcing design elements from outside contractors can lead to permanent savings on property taxes.
  • The importance of water resources is noted as a long-term consideration for "forever homes," with Reno's proximity to the Sierra Nevada mountains providing a predictable water supply.
