Overview

This episode discusses the recent market correction, focusing on Nvidia's role as a "systematically important semiconductor" and the impact of large IPOs. Noah Weisberger and Jason Glazer from Bank Credit Analysts analyze the confluence of factors driving market volatility, including interest rates, IPO supply, and specific company news, while also offering their outlook on the durability of the business cycle and the Fed's policy path.

Key discussion points

  • Noah Weisberger's prior research on bond-stock correlation suggested owning less stocks when correlations became positive, a view he notes was "unheated" by the market.
  • BCA's quantitative analysis on CEO departures shows cumulative abnormal returns are negative for weeks/months, especially if the departure is due to legal/ethical lapses (e.g., -6% in one week, -12% in one month) versus medical reasons (+1.9% in one month after initial decline).
  • Jason Glazer notes that clients were overweight Nvidia due to its key role in the AI supply chain but started worrying about circular financing and the CFO's performance as interim CEO.
  • Noah Weisberger compares the current market concerns about Nvidia's financing to the 2007-2008 period, noting that while risks are disclosed, the ultimate revenue flow down the tech stack is still uncertain.
  • Jason Glazer observes that the equity market is now realizing what the bond market recognized months ago regarding widening credit spreads, citing Mark Rowan's comment on companies borrowing at 16%.
  • Noah Weisberger highlights that strong equity market conditions tend to precede an uptick in IPO activity, which then tends to precede a worse market environment, evidenced by a "beautiful sign curve" in correlations.
  • Kevin Muir argues that the tech bull market's reliance on buybacks and low-cost borrowing has been exhausted, leading to massive equity issuance (e.g., SpaceX, Anthropic IPOs) that is now "killing the bull market arguments."
  • Noah Weisberger acknowledges that current IPO issuance levels (SpaceX + Anthropic) are in the 99th percentile of S&P 500 market cap, where forward S&P returns are below average and the range of outcomes is much wider, with a 30-35% chance of negative returns.
  • Jason Glazer notes that clients are chasing "defensive stocks" like Staples and Healthcare, which have become very expensive (e.g., Costco at 45x P/E), creating a "confused message" given strong economic data and falling oil/10-year rates.
  • Jason Glazer suggests the market's "thermometer is kind of broken" due to changes in market structure, particularly the size of passive investments acting as momentum funds, which are now unwinding.
  • Noah Weisberger observes a "pretty big divergence" between economic growth (PMIs, industrial production, consumer getting better) and the performance of cyclical versus defensive stocks, making him pay attention.
  • Kevin Muir argues that the Fed's hawkish stance, particularly after the election, was a larger portion of the selloff than realized, and he interprets their actions as intent on achieving their target and pushing the economy into recession.
  • Noah Weisberger agrees that for liability-driven investors, locking in a 10-year coupon that solves three-quarters of their return target problem is attractive, and he would put incremental risk dollars into fixed income.
  • Jason Glazer believes the Fed will have to look at the markets and may not go 50 basis points in December, potentially finishing the hiking cycle with just four hikes, which could be favorable for tech and cyclicals.
  • Noah Weisberger thinks the market is "overly concerned about a slowdown in earnings" that may be two years away, bringing that concern forward too quickly.
  • Jason Glazer believes people are "overly negative and overprojecting" the impact of the Democratic blue wave in the midterms, arguing that both parties need a decent economy and will behave more rationally.

He sees the current market decline as a drawdown, not the end of the bull market, given a favorable macroeconomic backdrop and good earnings outlook for the next two years. He would put incremental risk dollars into fixed income. He is increasingly less optimistic about margins than bottom-up analysts.
  • Jason Glazer: Clients were overweight Nvidia but are now concerned about circular financing and the CFO's performance. He notes a "confused message" in the market with defensive stocks becoming expensive while consumer discretionary and retail perform well, despite hawkish Fed rhetoric. He believes the market is overreacting to the Democratic blue wave and that the Fed may not hike as aggressively as currently feared, which could favor tech and cyclicals.
  • Kevin Muir: Argues that the tech bull market's arguments (buybacks, low-cost borrowing) are exhausted due to massive equity issuance.
