Felix Zulauf The Bottom Is In One Last Rally Then a Crash and the Mother of All

Host: Julia La Roche · Guest: Felix Zulauf, President of Zulauf Consulting · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Felix Zulauf believes the market has bottomed, with a significant rally expected into spring 2027, followed by a major decline in the second half of 2027. This decline will lead to the "mother of all stimulus" from central banks and fiscal authorities, setting the stage for another large market run-up. He also discusses the unique global economic imbalances and the unprecedented AI investment boom.

Key discussion points

  • Felix Zulauf asserts that global stock markets, gold, and silver are bottoming, while bond yields are topping. The dollar has limited upside, primarily in time rather than price.
  • He highlights chronic global current account imbalances, with the US running a nearly 5% of GDP deficit and Asian current accounts growing, which he views as unsustainable.
  • Zulauf describes the current AI investment boom as the largest in US history, exceeding the railroad and dot-com booms, but warns that its earnings growth is unsustainable beyond the first half of 2027.
  • He forecasts a rally to new S&P highs (around 8,500) by spring 2027, concluding the bull cycle from the 2022 low, followed by a significant decline to 5,000 in the S&P and a 50% drop in the NASDAQ 100 in the second half of 2027 due to earnings problems.
  • Zulauf distinguishes the current market setup from 1987, noting that in 1987 there was constant capital outflow, a declining dollar, and relentlessly rising bond yields, whereas now bond yields have peaked and the dollar is strong.
  • He expects oil prices to decline to about $60 due to a slowing global economy and reduced risk of major Middle East escalation, which will help bring inflation down.
  • Zulauf advises investors to go maximum cash by spring 2027 to prepare for the expected decline and subsequent "mother of all stimulus," which he believes will lead to a 100% run-up in the S&P from 5,000 to 10,000 into the late 2020s or early 2030s.
  • He expresses concern about systemic risks later this decade, potentially similar to 1929, if bond yields explode to 8% for 10-year treasuries, which he doubts the system can sustain.
