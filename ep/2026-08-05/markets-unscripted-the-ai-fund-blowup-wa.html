The AI Fund Blowup Was This the Perfect Bottom

Host: Matt Caruso, Jason Shapiro · Solo episode

Overview

Matt Caruso and Jason Shapiro discuss the recent "AI fund blowup," comparing it to historical liquidity events like LTCM and the 1987 crash for specific stocks. Jason Shapiro argues that the liquidation of a highly leveraged AI fund created a "perfect bottom" for AI stocks, while Matt Caruso emphasizes the importance of relative strength and risk management in the aftermath. They also explore contrarian opportunities outside of AI and the critical role of financing in the future of AI development.

Key discussion points

  • Jason Shapiro identifies three factors for trades: positioning, sentiment, and market action. He notes that while NASDAQ positioning wasn't crowded, sentiment shifted with news failures from MU, Samsung, and Hynix, indicating a "bad tape."
  • The "AI fund blowup" is compared to LTCM, where a highly leveraged position was correct in theory but failed due to illiquidity and forced selling. Jason Shapiro suggests this event created a "perfect bottom" for AI stocks, as the market gapped up 20-25% the next day, preventing easy entry.
  • Matt Caruso highlights the importance of relative strength in a market downturn, advising investors to focus on resilient stocks rather than those that fell the most. He points to Cisco's recovery in 1998 as an example of a strong stock rebounding quickly after a liquidity event.
  • Jason Shapiro proposes a contrarian view: looking for opportunities in non-AI related stocks, as the AI sector is currently over-analyzed and potentially over-mined for alpha. He notes that consumer stocks like Brinker International and Cheesecake Factory have shown strength.
  • Both hosts agree that the long-term viability of AI hinges on its ability to be financed. Jason Shapiro emphasizes watching credit spreads on AI-related companies and general interest rates as key indicators, rather than questioning the fundamental utility of AI.
