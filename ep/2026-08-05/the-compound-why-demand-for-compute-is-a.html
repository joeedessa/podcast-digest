Why Demand for Compute Is About to Explode TCAF 253

Host: Josh Brown, Michael Batnick, Ben Carlson · Guest: Alex Kantrowitz, Founder, Big Technology Newsletter and Podcast · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode features Alex Kantrowitz discussing the rapid advancements and economic implications of AI. He highlights the explosive growth in AI model usage and revenue, the evolving competitive landscape among foundational model providers, and the shift in value capture towards AI applications and cloud infrastructure. Kantrowitz also addresses the potential for AI agents to replace existing software tools and the ongoing debate about the cost and availability of compute.

Key discussion points

  • Alex Kantrowitz notes the explosive growth of OpenAI, with ChatGPT users increasing from under 200 million in June 2024 to over 900 million today (over 1 billion according to Sensor Tower). OpenAI and Anthropic are expected to generate close to $80 billion in revenue this year, potentially exceeding $100 billion.
  • Kantrowitz explains that the blueprint for building AI models is widely available, making compute, data, and model size the primary differentiators. China, constrained by access to cutting-edge Nvidia chips, has specialized in areas like reasoning (Deep Seek) and coding (Kimmy K2) to achieve parity with frontier models in specific domains.
  • The value in AI is shifting from foundational models to applications and products. OpenAI is cutting prices for two of its latest models by 20% and 80% per token, respectively, in response to competition from open-weight models and the use of AI routers like Open Router.
  • Alex Karp of Palantir and Satya Nadella of Microsoft have raised concerns about foundational AI models using customer data to train themselves and potentially build competing products, as seen with Anthropic's Claude Design product competing with Figma.
  • Microsoft's Azure cloud growth accelerated to 43% year-over-year, the highest since 2022, with $30 billion in quarterly revenue. Google Cloud was up 82%, and Amazon Web Services (AWS) grew 36.7% year-over-year, its fastest growth in 18 quarters, exceeding a $25 billion annual revenue run rate for its AI business.
  • Meta's free cash flow dropped from $12 billion a quarter ago to $784 million, a 90% decline, due to massive CapEx spending on AI without clear revenue generation or product success in AI. Analysts are "begging Zuckerberg to lease out the compute."
  • Josh Brown argues that Apple could become the "toll booth for consumer AI use" by leveraging its ecosystem power to mandate interoperability for Agentic Siri with third-party apps, without incurring significant AI development costs.
