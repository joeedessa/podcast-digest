The Week Ahead Will the Great Labour Market Freeze Begin to Melt

Host: Ben Ford · Guest: Dominique Dwor-Frecaut, Macro Hive

Overview

Dominique Dwor-Frecaut discusses the recent FOMC meeting, highlighting that market reactions were more surprising than the meeting itself, which aligned with her expectations for no change and a hold. She analyzes the dissents from Hammack, Kashkari, and Logan as different interpretations of risk, primarily concerning policy being too loose. Dwor-Frecaut also provides her outlook on the Fed's policy for the rest of the year, aligning with the "doves" due to low wage growth and high corporate profits.

Key discussion points

  • FOMC Dissents: Dominique Dwor-Frecaut explains that the dissents from Hammack, Kashkari, and Logan reflect concerns that current policy is too loose to achieve the inflation target, with Hammack worried about demand pressures and Kashkari about a higher inflation regime. Logan is suspected to share the view that policy is too loose and sees the labor market as a potential inflation source.
  • AI's Impact on Inflation: Dominique Dwor-Frecaut disagrees with the view that AI investment is adding to demand-side inflation, arguing instead that its impact is primarily through cost. She notes a widening AI-related trade deficit due to limited US manufacturing capacity, and a significant increase in global chip prices, which act as a crucial input cost.
  • Fed's "No Forward Guidance" Approach: Dominique Dwor-Frecaut criticizes Bullard's approach to communication, stating that "no forward guidance on policy does not mean no information on the Fed assessment of the economy and the Fed reaction function." She believes that withholding this information impedes good market functioning and creates confusion.
  • PCE Data and Real Income: Dominique Dwor-Frecaut notes that the recent PCE price index showed strong increases in energy-impacted categories offset by deflation in discretionary categories, consistent with supply shocks without second-round effects. She highlights a second month of positive real income growth, tentatively indicating a break from the trend of declining real income.
  • Labor Market Outlook: Dominique Dwor-Frecaut is looking for stabilization and recovery in labor market flows, particularly in JOLTS data (hires, fires, and quits), as a sign of a stronger, more resilient labor market. She notes tentative hints from low claims and a decrease in the University of Michigan survey question on future unemployment, suggesting the labor market "could be about to unfreeze."
