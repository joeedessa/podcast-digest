Who Killed The Random Walk Victor Haghani on Momentum Passive Investing and LTCM

Host: Jack Farley · Guest: Victor Haghani, Founding Partner of Long-Term Capital Management and Elm Wealth · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Victor Haghani discusses his paper "Who Killed The Random Walk," which challenges the random walk theory of asset prices by introducing a multi-agent model with different investor types, including "extrapolators" and "static asset allocators." He argues that these investor behaviors, combined with corporate supply and demand, explain market puzzles like excess volatility, trends, and booms and busts, rather than solely fundamental earnings. Haghani also outlines Elm Wealth's dynamic asset allocation strategy, which combines value and momentum principles.

Key discussion points

  • Haghani's paper "Who Killed The Random Walk" proposes a multi-agent model where investor types (value investors, static investors, extrapolators/return chasers) interact to explain stock market puzzles such as excess volatility, stochastic volatility, trends, and booms and busts.
  • He distinguishes between "passive investing" (market cap-weighted index funds for stock picking, which he views as generally positive) and "passive asset allocation" (static allocation like 60/40, which he views as problematic and a cause of market booms and busts).
  • Haghani argues that the overall level of the market is currently "ridiculous" and not primarily driven by fundamental earnings, but rather by extrapolators, static asset allocators, and corporate supply/demand (e.g., buybacks, IPOs).
  • He differentiates between "momentum investors" (who make binary, chunky allocation changes based on momentum signals) and "extrapolators" or "return chasers" (who make continuous, slow-moving allocation changes based on past returns), noting that momentum strategies have historically performed well, while return chasing has performed poorly.
  • Elm Wealth's investment approach uses a "five-star screen" for risky assets, focusing on systematic risk, estimable risk premiums, broad diversification, liquidity, transparency, low fees, and tax efficiency, leading to a dynamic asset allocation based on long-term expected risk premiums and risk metrics (using momentum as a proxy).
