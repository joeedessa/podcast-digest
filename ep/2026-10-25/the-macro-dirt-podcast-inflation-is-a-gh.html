Inflation is a Ghost Tax

Host: Tony Greer, Jared Dillian, Matt Ziegler · Guest: Dylan Ratigan, Journalist, Author, TV Host, Entrepreneur · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode discusses the recent bond rally and its implications for equities, with Jared Dillian attributing it to short covering and a Demark 9-13-9 setup, while Dylan Ratigan views it as a risk-off maneuver from AI names. A central theme is Dylan Ratigan's theory that inflation is a "ghost tax" and a hidden default on the currency's purchasing power, particularly impacting the poor. The hosts and guest also engage in an extended discussion about music and its personal significance.

Key discussion points

  • Jared Dillian noted a 19 basis point move lower in 10-year Treasuries, the biggest since the Silicon Valley Bank bankruptcy in 2023, driven by massive short covering in the CTA world and pod shops.
  • Jared Dillian observed that going into Thursday, three and a half rate hikes were priced out of the curve, which reduced to two and a half by the end of yesterday, with the probability of a December rate hike falling to 77%.
  • Dylan Ratigan interpreted the bond rally and simultaneous 2-2.5% Nasdaq sell-off as a "fairly violent and classical risk-off maneuver," with a flight from AI names and widening credit default swaps on big AI names.
  • Tony Greer highlighted that the market move was an aggressive rotation, not an all-asset class sell-off, with consumer staples, home construction, home builders, and transports rallying, while semis, oil services, industrial miners, gold miners, uranium miners, and rare earth miners were "shellacked."
  • Jared Dillian mentioned that JC Parets, a chart guy, called gold a "rock," which Jared views as a sentiment signal often preceding a bottom, referencing 2013 when similar comments preceded gold bottoming at $1050.
  • Dylan Ratigan presented his theory that the inflation experienced is a "hidden default" or "ghost tax," arguing that while the dollar remains strong relative to other currencies, its purchasing power has collapsed domestically, effectively inflating away the national debt.
  • Dylan Ratigan posited that this "ghost tax" disproportionately burdens poor people, as rich people can use inflation to reduce debt servicing costs, and middle-income people experience it as an inconvenience, while the poor face existential challenges.
  • Jared Dillian discussed the "affordability crisis" being addressed by Democrats in South Carolina, suggesting that solutions require either a massive recession or significant supply-side deregulation, citing Carol Sokoloff's 1980 book on deregulation's role in a 40-year bond run.
  • Dylan Ratigan proposed that costs in education, healthcare, and national defense could be reduced by 50-70% by reorienting their "North Star" to health, learning, and security, respectively, rather than perpetuating existing institutional structures.
  • Tony Greer shared a personal anecdote about college bills, stating that the cost per child per year "only goes up," reinforcing Dylan Ratigan's "ghost tax" theory as a "lightbulb aha moment."
  • Dylan Ratigan shared a personal connection to Fleetwood Mac's "Dreams," associating it with a period of "dream life" in New York City while hosting Fast Money, characterized by "soft mornings" and playing "offense" in life.
  • Matt Ziegler revealed the backstory of King Harvest's "Dancing in the Moonlight," stating it was written in a hospital bed after the singer and his girlfriend were assaulted on a beach, seeking peace and serenity.
  • Dylan Ratigan explained AC/DC's "Thunderstruck" is on his playlist as "the ultimate stoke for the drive," a consequence of his high school football locker room experience.
  • Dylan Ratigan described Persian jazz lounge music as the soundtrack he would play if tasked with running a tanker through the Straits of Hormuz, as an "homage to the pirates and the madmen."
