4 Inflation Stretched Valuations Why Is the Market Still Risk-On

Host: Jack Forehand · Guest: Chan Yang, Head of Research and Portfolio Manager of the VPX ETF at Varied Perception

Overview

Chan Yang discusses Varied Perception's quantitative and qualitative framework, emphasizing causal relationships and adaptive leading indicators. He explains their macro risk indicator, which currently signals a risk-on environment due to resilient growth, neutral policy, contained inflation, and good liquidity. Yang also addresses the impact of AI capex, the dynamics of market tops, and his out-of-consensus view on the Fed's policy under Chair Walsh.

Key discussion points

  • Chan Yang emphasizes thinking from first principles to identify causal relationships in data, rather than relying on black-box models, to ensure statistical meaningfulness and persistence.
  • Varied Perception's approach to leading indicators involves a multi-stage causal discovery process, using algorithms to adapt and overweight indicators that have been predictive at recent turning points, contrasting with static traditional models.
  • The Macro Risk Indicator, a score between 0 and 100, is constructed from four dimensions: growth, inflation, policy, and liquidity, using decision trees to determine a final risk-on/risk-off score.
  • The Macro Risk Indicator has been in a "risk-on" regime, indicating resilient growth, overstated policy risk, contained inflation, and a good liquidity environment, which aligns with the broadening out of equity markets.
  • Yang suggests that markets are efficient at pricing first-order impacts, but the key is to identify second-order impacts, particularly shifts in policy, which are not currently leaning against the equity bull market.
  • AI capex is deemed very important, contributing to corporate dissaving and overall economic resilience, with the time to worry being when hyperscalers dial back capex.
  • Yang believes the current AI cycle is in a phase where AI adopters are starting to benefit, with the profit pool broadening out beyond bottleneck hardware, until the next major technological breakthrough.
  • Recessions are typically preceded by a rising savings rate, not a falling one, as precautionary savings pick up, leading to a drawdown in income for others and a negative feedback loop.
  • Varied Perception's market tops checklist includes behavioral signs (circular financing, retail hype, guru investors) and economic signs (slowing growth, tightening policy, tightening liquidity), with the latter not fully present currently.
  • Yang views the current inflation as mechanically above target due to energy prices, but underlying core inflation drivers (housing, labor market, small business, low-income consumers) remain subdued, suggesting policymakers should look through the supply shock.
  • Yang holds an out-of-consensus view that the Fed will not hike interest rates this year, attributing this to Chair Walsh's focus on long-term reforms and political considerations, particularly President Trump's stance.
  • The US labor market is bifurcated, with many finding jobs but struggling with personal finances due to price level resets and stagnant real incomes, as shown by the divergence between "jobs hard to get" and "personal finances" indicators.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Chan Yang's Macro Risk Indicator suggests a continued risk-on environment for equities, driven by resilient growth, neutral policy, and good liquidity, despite elevated valuations.
  • He identifies AI capex as a significant tailwind for the economy, with the current phase benefiting AI adopters as the profit pool broadens.
  • Yang's analysis of market tops indicates that while behavioral excesses and high valuations are present, the economic conditions (slowing growth, tightening liquidity) and cash settlement mechanisms are not yet fully signaling a major top.
  • He holds an out-of-consensus view that the Fed, under Chair Walsh, will prioritize long-term reforms over short-term rate hikes, even with inflation above target, viewing current inflation as a supply shock to be looked through.
  • Varied Perception's VPX ETF aims to adapt to changing market dynamics by actively tilting sectors based on capital cycle models and selecting stocks based on quality and crowding, currently showing overweight in energy and semis.
