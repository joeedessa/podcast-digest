Why Macro is Pretty Risk-On for Equities Tian Yang of Variant Perception

Host: Jack Farley · Guest: Tian Yang, Co-founder and Head of Research at Variant Perception

Overview

Tian Yang discusses the current "risk-on" macro regime for equities, driven by supportive growth, inflation, policy, and liquidity indicators, despite signs of excess in AI-related stocks. He highlights a rotation from high-growth tech to value laggards like energy and financials, and emphasizes the role of sovereign competition in sustaining CapEx and influencing global markets. Yang also provides tactical and medium-term views on various asset classes and geographies, including a preference for Latin American equities and skepticism on India and European assets.

Key discussion points

  • Tian Yang believes the macro environment is "broadly still supportive risk assets" for the next three to six months, with equities broadening out as money rotates to laggards.
  • Yang notes that the "agentic AI" and "bottlenecks" narratives that fueled the semiconductor rally from April are "clearly done," and a new "wow moment" narrative is needed for semis to regain highs.
  • Variant Perception's LPPL (log periodic power law) bubble and crash detection system triggered "bubble exhaustion" signals in early June for semis/AI, indicating a tactical fade, and "buy signals" for China and Indonesia in June.
  • Yang states that the "primacy of sovereignty" is a five-to-ten-year investment theme, where existential US-China competition in energy, manufacturing, and technology means governments will backstop CapEx even if the private sector exhausts.
  • Yang identifies two consistent top-down signs of generational market tops: monetary policy tightening for six to nine months, and equity breadth narrowing for six to nine months, neither of which are currently present.
  • Yang attributes the US economy's resilience to corporate dissaving (high CapEx), a positive fiscal impulse (tariff refunds, OBBA front-loading), and low household savings rates, which keep money flowing.
  • Yang suggests that the demand for "cash settlement" in financial transactions, potentially triggered by mega IPOs like SpaceX, Anthropic, or OpenAI, could signal downside risks as the "hierarchy of money" shifts.
  • Yang is "personally pretty skeptical of India as an allocation" due to its geopolitical setup, being "caught between China and the US," and its high food weight in the inflation basket limiting RBI policy.
  • Yang views Japan's macro situation as "ridiculously untenable" with high inflation and a weak currency, requiring a "Plaza Accord type thing" or significant BOJ action to resolve the currency issue and make Japanese assets more interesting.
  • Yang believes Fed Chair Walsh is prioritizing "restoring the functioning of money markets" and reforming the Fed over immediate 25 bps rate hikes, which requires time and political capital.
  • Yang sees gold as having "found the floor" and playing a portfolio role as a "stagflation" hedge, preferring it over nominal bonds in a multi-asset portfolio.
  • Yang's VPX ETF is a long-only systematic US equity strategy that combines capital cycle models for sector tilts, quality and crowding for stock picking, and macro/LPPL overlays for risk management, aiming to maximize forecast return.
