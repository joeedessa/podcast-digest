A War-Sized AI Bet Private Credit Went All In Will the Government End Up Owning

Host: Jack Forehand, Justin Carbonneau · Guest: Ben Hunt, Perseid · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode introduces "Why Am I Reading This Now," a new show examining financial media narratives. Ben Hunt discusses his "World War AI" thesis, arguing that the U.S. will spend trillions on AI CapEx, comparable to WWII spending, which is driving markets and creating a systemic financial crisis risk. Hunt predicts government intervention, including backstops, bailouts, and price controls, as the only way to avoid a full-blown crisis.

Key discussion points

  • Ben Hunt's "World War AI" thesis posits that the U.S. will spend $4-5 trillion on AI CapEx and data center build-out, an amount equivalent to WWII spending in inflation-adjusted dollars. This spending is the marginal driver of markets and the economy, with half of 2026's projected 2% GDP growth expected from AI CapEx.
  • Hunt argues that the initial stage of AI CapEx is being financed by "shadow banks," specifically private equity and private credit markets, because companies cannot fund it from cash flow. If this "hamster wheel" of financing stops, it would lead to a "full blown systemic i.e. financial system crisis" due to private markets being "all in" on AI CapEx.
  • Hunt identifies a "crowding out effect" due to simultaneous global government borrowing and AI CapEx, leading to a "real dearth of capital" and structurally higher interest rates. He also highlights raw input constraints like energy, water, and electricity, noting that data centers' projected energy consumption will rise from 4% to 25% of American electrical production, which "the math does not check out" for.
  • Hunt predicts that if data center projects fail, leading to trillions in losses, the government will intervene with loan backstops, direct financing, potential ownership, and a command economy with rationing and price controls, framing it as a "war for national survival" to avoid a systemic crisis.
  • Hunt observes a shift in the narrative around oil prices from a temporary shortage to a long-term supply reduction, meaning the floor for oil prices has structurally jumped up. He also notes a resurgence of negative narratives around AI CapEx and increasing negative political narratives concerning AI and data centers, particularly heading into midterm elections.
  • Hunt points to a "horseshoe effect" where both the left and right show impetus towards price controls and government ownership of large economic sectors, with hyperscalers willing to accept government equity stakes for backstops. This trend is expected to integrate into a strategic competition with China, further justifying government intervention and control of the AI industry.
