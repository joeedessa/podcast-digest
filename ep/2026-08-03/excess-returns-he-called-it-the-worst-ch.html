Host: Jack Forehand, Justin Carbonneau · Guest: Rupert Mitchell, Blind Squirrel Macro

Overview

Rupert Mitchell discusses his macro portfolio, which aims to outperform a 60-40 portfolio with less volatility. He details his current positions, including energy equities, physical commodities, and precious metals, and explains his "aggressively disinterested" stance on long-duration fixed income. Mitchell also shares his views on the "chart of truth" comparing the S&P to the rest of the world, the impact of equity issuance, and specific opportunities in equal-weighted S&P, UK investment trusts, Uzbekistan, and Turkey.

Key discussion points

  • Rupert Mitchell's portfolio is designed as an antidote to the 60-40, outperforming the S&P with less than 50% of the volatility since the beginning of last year. He is "aggressively disinterested in long duration fixed income" due to a lack of perceived diversification benefits and widespread inflation.
  • Mitchell is underweight U.S. equities, seeing "better value elsewhere." He recommends a "big pair trade long R.S.P. short QQQ" (S&P equal weight vs. Nasdaq 100), which has shown a "10 percent move since since since since the third week of June."
  • Mitchell believes China's role as a monopsony in energy markets has created a "collar" on crude prices, effectively writing a put at the high $60s per barrel and cutting off the right tail. This creates a "very friendly environment for energy equities," making the entire sector "much more investable" even without sustained triple-digit crude prices.
  • Mitchell highlights UK investment trusts as a preferred method for investing in "very cheap equities" in the UK, noting they are trading at a "high single digit discount to NAV." He also sees Uzbekistan as a rare "set and forget" frontier market opportunity through GDRs in the Uzbek National Privatization Fund, which are up 30% since IPO in May.
  • Mitchell suggests ignoring inflation noise in Turkey, as its economy is only 2% reliant on the US consumer and its equities are "crazy cheap," with the top 20 companies trading at just under 10 times forward earnings. He believes a "decent brush fire" is needed in risk assets before further advancement and is adding vol positions to his hedge book.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Rupert Mitchell believes the "chart of truth" (S&P vs. rest of world) has been flat since November, despite a "punchy" dollar (DXY above 100), indicating rest-of-world equities are "chugging away."
  • Mitchell observes inflation everywhere and believes bonds lack diversification benefits in a world where stock and bond total returns appear positively correlated.
  • Mitchell notes that equity issuance, particularly from large tech companies, is a significant accelerant, turning a "massive tailwind" (buybacks, 401k flows) into a "huge headwind" for U.S. equities.
  • Mitchell argues that equal-weighted indices outperform cap-weighted indices over time, and individuals should invest in them.
  • Mitchell views China as a monopsony in energy markets, creating a "collar" on crude prices that mitigates risk for energy equities and makes the sector "much more investable."
  • Mitchell believes UK equities are "more universally disliked" than almost any other asset class, trading at the "widest gap since the 70s" compared to US equities, presenting an attractive risk-reward setup.
  • Mitchell sees Uzbekistan as a rare opportunity to invest in a frontier market emerging onto the mainstream, with core franchise infrastructure assets trading for low single-digit earnings multiples.
  • Mitchell suggests that Turkish equities, despite inflation noise, are "crazy cheap" (top 20 companies at just under 10 times forward earnings) and represent "survivor portfolios" that have learned to operate in a tough environment.
  • Mitchell is concerned about a rerun of the leverage unwind seen two summers ago, noting "an awful lot of dry brush on the forest floor" in risk, including leverage in equities and record low dispersion and implied correlation.
