Overview

Steve Eisman, Glenn Schorr, and Ken Worthington discuss current market dynamics, focusing on the health of private credit and private equity, the business models of retail brokers, and the evolving cryptocurrency ecosystem. Eisman expresses skepticism about private credit valuations and the long-term viability of business models dependent on customer "laziness," while Schorr and Worthington offer more nuanced views on these sectors and the potential for AI and blockchain technology.

Key discussion points

  • Ken Worthington covers brokers, asset managers, exchanges, and crypto, specifically Coinbase, Bullish, and Circle.
  • Glenn Schorr covers big banks, big brokers, traditional asset managers, trust banks, and alternative asset managers.
  • Retail brokers like Charles Schwab and LPL generate significant profits from the "free float of customer cash," which is uninvested cash on which the broker earns a spread.
  • The potential for agentic AI to optimize customer cash could reduce profitability for retail brokers, though Ken Worthington believes firms like Schwab will adapt.
  • Glenn Schorr prefers large, diversified banks and wealth managers (e.g., JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley) as more flexible models against cash optimization, due to their broad service offerings.
  • Private credit demand in the wealth channel has significantly dried up, with inflows being "infinitesimal," and redemption requests still exceeding the 5% per quarter limit in most places.
  • Private equity performance has underperformed public markets for the first time since 2008 or 2009, and monetizations are mixed, with some firms like KKR reporting record monetizations while others struggle to sell assets.
  • Steve Eisman predicts that the private credit software situation will reach fruition in early 2027, with a larger impact in 2028, as loans need refinancing and valuations are significantly lower.
  • Ken Worthington views the cryptocurrency ecosystem as an asset class driven by blockchain technology and use cases, rather than solely a hedge against fiat currency debasement.
  • Ken Worthington believes Bitcoin is a "single-use case technology" as a store of value, and is less interesting than other chains like Ethereum and Solana that support more use cases.
  • Steve Eisman suggests Circle should sell due to the difficulty of breaking into the payment system against incumbents like Visa and Mastercard, who are actively developing their own stablecoin solutions.
  • Glenn Schorr reports "stunning" Q2 results for big banks, with investment banking up 38%, trading up 47%, and asset and wealth management showing 7% organic growth, though deposit competition is increasing.
