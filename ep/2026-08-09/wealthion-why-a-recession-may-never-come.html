Why a Recession May Never Come and Why Gold Still Wins

Host: Maggie Lake · Guest: Andrew Sarna, Portfolio Manager at Fourth Lane Partners · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Andrew Sarna discusses the current economic landscape, characterized by a large U.S. deficit making a recession unlikely, despite underlying economic weaknesses. He highlights the implicit cap on Treasury yields due to government insolvency concerns and the concentration of the S&P 500 in a few AI-related names. Sarna advocates for a core holding in real assets like energy and precious metals, particularly gold, as a hedge against money printing and geopolitical tensions.

Key discussion points

  • Andrew Sarna views a U.S. recession as unlikely given the 6% to 7% deficit, despite bearish signs like negative leading economic indicators and rising delinquency rates among lower-end consumers.
  • Sarna believes there is an implicit cap on Treasury yields, even without formal yield curve control, because higher yields would render the U.S. government budget insolvent.
  • Sarna notes that 40% of the U.S. S&P 500 is concentrated in 10 names, mostly indexed to the AI trade, which he sees as potentially rolling over, leading to anemic economic growth driven by government spending rather than private sector activity.
  • Sarna suggests that the U.S. stock market might benefit from a slowdown in AI CapEx, as evidenced by Google's stock price falling after announcing increased CapEx, indicating the market is asking large companies to pull back.
  • Sarna maintains a long-term constructive view on gold, citing persistent money printing and fracturing global order, despite short-term pressures from rising real rates and initial selling during conflicts.
