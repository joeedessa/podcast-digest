David Rosenberg and Rich Bernstein on What Ends the AI Trade And What They Own I

Host: Matt Zeigler · Guest: David Rosenberg, Founder and President, Rosenberg Research

Overview

This episode features a reunion of former Merrill Lynch colleagues David Rosenberg and Rich Bernstein, discussing the current market environment, particularly the AI trade, inflation, and asset allocation. Rosenberg argues that the US economy is more fragile than it appears, with disinflationary forces building, and views the stock market as a bubble. Bernstein agrees on the speculative nature of the market and the misallocation of capital due to the AI boom, advocating for diversification outside the US and into sectors like energy.

Key discussion points

  • Rich Bernstein notes that all 30 versions of the Atlanta Fed's Taylor rule suggest the Fed should be hiking rates, which he personally believes is appropriate given other economic data, but he uses this point to highlight the inconsistency of "conservative economists" who previously championed the rule.
  • David Rosenberg disagrees with the Fed hiking rates, citing a four-quarter average real GDP growth of 1.4% (below the Fed's 2.0% potential GDP growth estimate) and a decelerating Dallas Fed trim mean inflation metric at 2.2% (down from 2.7% a year ago and at a 5-year low).
  • Bernstein argues that the AI bubble is inherently inflationary due to misallocation of capital, drawing parallels to the dot-com bubble where capital flowed into tech instead of energy, leading to underinvestment in critical infrastructure like housing and energy.
  • Rosenberg highlights that approximately 50% of business capex is AI-related, growing at 18% in real terms, while non-AI capex is negative year-over-year, indicating a sapping of momentum from the rest of the economy.
  • Rosenberg views gold as a secular bull market driven by central bank buying since 2010, with global central banks reallocating reserves into gold, and sees the recent downdraft as a buying opportunity.
  • Bernstein uses the analogy of gold as a "spare tire" in a portfolio, a hedge against unforeseen uncertainty, and notes that the recent interest in silver (a high-beta version of gold) was a warning sign of excessive bullish sentiment.
  • Bernstein observes that non-US stocks have outperformed venture capital over the last five years, a significant shift that few acknowledge, and RBA currently holds its biggest non-US overweight in the firm's history.
  • Rosenberg and Bernstein both agree that the US market is in a bubble-like state, citing high valuations (Shiller CAPE multiple above 41, a three-sigma event), extreme sentiment, and a record 73% of household asset mix in equities.
  • Rosenberg references Bob Farrell's Rule #9 ("When all the forecasts and experts agree, something else is going to happen") to describe the current market, where mutual fund managers are at 1% cash and speculative long positions are at historical highs.
  • Bernstein suggests that Bob Farrell and Dick McCabe would be "scouring looking for charts that are improving outside the tech sector."

Market & macro takeaways

  • Rich Bernstein believes the Fed should be hiking rates, contrary to current policy, due to economic data and the Taylor rule. He sees the AI boom as a misallocation of capital, creating inflationary pressures in the real economy. He is significantly overweight non-US stocks, believing opportunities outside the US are better due to converging growth stories, lower valuations, and higher dividend yields.
  • David Rosenberg argues against Fed rate hikes, citing slowing GDP growth and decelerating underlying inflation. He views the US stock market as a bubble, with extreme valuations and sentiment, and expects the credit market to lead the eventual rollover of the AI trade. He is long gold, seeing it as a secular bull market driven by central bank demand, and considers the recent dip a buying opportunity with a $6,000 price target. He advocates for international diversification, particularly in India, Southeast Asia (ex-Korea/Taiwan), Japan, Europe, and Latin America, to hedge against the US bubble.
