The AI Unwind And Warshs Long-End Gamble Weekly Roundup

Host: Felix Jauvin, Quinn · Solo episode

Overview

Felix Jauvin and Quinn discuss the unwind of the AI trade, specifically the forced liquidation of Leopold Ashburner's firm and Ken Griffin's subsequent purchase of those assets. They also analyze the recent Federal Reserve meeting led by Kevin Warsh, interpreting his communication as a deliberate attempt to tighten financial conditions via the long end of the yield curve without explicitly hiking short-term rates, aiming to manage inflation and growth ahead of the midterms.

Key discussion points

  • Quinn notes that the selling in AI-related assets had become "high volume and pretty aggressive" before Leopold Ashburner's firm's forced liquidation, which he views as a meaningful signal of a market washout.
  • Felix Jauvin highlights that the fundamental story of the AI trade began to be questioned with SK Hynix's earnings miss, coinciding with peak leverage in the system.
  • Quinn suggests that the market's reaction to Kevin Warsh's Fed press conference, particularly the rise in long-end yields, was a direct result of Warsh's stated intention to remove balance sheet accommodation from the long end of the curve.
  • Felix Jauvin points out that Warsh's communication style, which avoids explicit forward guidance while still signaling policy direction, creates uncertainty but is likely a strategy to achieve committee consensus.
  • Quinn believes that if the Fed allows the long end to reprice without intervention, it could lead to another "50 to 75, maybe a hundred basis points higher in the long end of the bond market," causing a stock market correction, lower growth, and lower inflation.
  • Felix Jauvin observes that the market's initial rally after the Fed's pause was followed by significant rate volatility during Warsh's press conference, particularly in the 30-year bond.
  • Quinn argues that the front end of the curve is "not that far from where it should be," with the long end being "way too low" due to Fed intervention.
  • Felix Jauvin notes that the latest GDP data, while missing consensus at 1.5% (vs. 2.1%), showed strong personal consumption expenditure, suggesting the core economy was robust before recent tightening.
  • Quinn predicts that growth estimates and earnings estimates will likely "come down from the recent peaks" over the next two to three quarters due to rising real rates and tightening financial conditions.
  • Felix Jauvin suggests that the Fed's strategy of tightening the long end without hiking short rates is a "bet" that financial conditions will tighten sufficiently by September to avoid a hike close to the midterms.
  • Quinn views the coordination between different government branches and the Fed as a "manufacture the outcome they need" strategy, especially given the background of hedge fund managers running the Treasury and Fed.
  • Quinn expects the Iran war situation to "float to the background" due to sufficient market problems, noting that the back end of the oil curve has not reacted as strongly as the front months.
