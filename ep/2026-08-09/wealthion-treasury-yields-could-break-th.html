Treasury Yields Could Break the AI Boom

Host: Maggie Lake · Guest: Jesse Felder, Founder and Author of The Felder Report · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Jesse Felder believes the market is witnessing a "slow motion debt crisis unfold," driven by high nominal GDP growth, the Fed being behind the curve, and massive sovereign debt levels globally. He argues that the AI bubble is at its "beginning of the end" due to impaired leveraged capital and deteriorating earnings quality among hyperscalers. Felder suggests a shift towards value stocks and the energy sector, particularly oil, as protective measures against impending financial tightening and inflation.

Key discussion points

  • Jesse Felder identifies the 10-year Treasury yield as "the most important chart in the world" because it is the risk-free rate everything is based on. He notes that the Taylor rule suggests the Fed is behind the curve by as much as 300 basis points, leading to a lack of confidence in the Fed's ability to control inflation.
  • Felder states that the strong move in gold prices over the last 18-24 months has been a "really good leading indicator for the rest of the commodity space," which in turn leads interest rates. He believes commodities are following gold's lead, and interest rates are following commodities.
  • Felder argues that the Fed, under Kevin Warsh, is making a mistake by not clarifying its reaction function to inflation and nominal GDP growth, which is causing markets to lose confidence in its willingness to address inflation.
  • Felder highlights a "supply demand issue" in the bond market, with a US fiscal deficit of 6% of GDP during an economic expansion and $2 trillion a year of new supply. This, combined with hyperscalers needing hundreds of billions in borrowing, is inundating debt markets.
  • Felder believes the AI trade's "final blow off" occurred with the impairment of leveraged capital, citing the crash in the South Korean stock market and the blow-up of the hedge fund "situational awareness." He notes that "last in, first out" (LIFO) dynamics are at play, with the last speculative money now impaired.
  • Felder observes a shift in market dynamics, with momentum breaking and value stocks starting to perform well again. He points to consumer staples versus consumer discretionary, software versus semiconductors, and defensive versus cyclicals as indicators of this shift towards risk-off and value.
  • Felder identifies oil prices as "the most attractive sector" within commodities, noting that oil is "as cheap as it's ever been" relative to gold or silver. He predicts oil prices will move "steadily higher" and believes they are "well over a hundred dollars a barrel" intrinsically.
  • Felder is closely watching whether Anthropic and OpenAI can go public, as their ability to sell public equity is crucial for them to pay the "hundreds of billions of dollars" in compute commitments to hyperscalers. He sees doubt about their IPOs as a "huge warning sign" for the AI bubble's sustainability.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Jesse Felder believes a "slow motion debt crisis" is unfolding, driven by high nominal GDP growth, the Fed being behind the curve, and massive sovereign debt levels globally, particularly in Japan and the US.
  • Felder argues that the Fed's current policy, especially Kevin Warsh's unwillingness to clarify the reaction function, is eroding market confidence and contributing to rising long-term yields.
  • Felder predicts that to control inflation, the Fed will need to create a "significant tightening of financial conditions," including lower stock prices and wider high-yield spreads, resembling a typical recession.
  • Felder views the AI bubble as having reached its "beginning of the end," marked by the impairment of leveraged capital (e.g., South Korean traders, "situational awareness" hedge fund) and deteriorating earnings quality among hyperscalers.
  • Felder suggests a strategic shift from momentum to value investing, noting that value stocks are starting to perform well. He specifically sees value in certain software names that have been "destroyed" by the momentum pairs trade.
  • Felder is bullish on commodities, particularly the energy sector and oil prices, which he believes are significantly undervalued relative to other commodities and their intrinsic value (well over $100/barrel). He sees this as being in the "middle innings of this commodity bull market."
  • Felder highlights the critical importance of Anthropic and OpenAI's ability to go public to finance their compute commitments to hyperscalers, viewing any doubt about their IPOs as a major warning sign for the AI bubble.
