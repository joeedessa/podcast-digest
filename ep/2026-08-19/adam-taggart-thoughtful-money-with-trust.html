With Trust Breaking The World Over Quality Collateral Is Now Key Jonathan Wellum

Host: Adam Taggart · Guest: Jonathan Wellum, Founder and CEO of Rocklink Investment Partners · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Jonathan Wellum discusses his firm's macro view, focusing on "six Ds": digitization, debt, debasement, deglobalization, demographics, and dear valuations. He expresses caution regarding current market valuations, particularly in the AI sector, and highlights opportunities in hard assets and specific industrial and insurance companies that benefit from deglobalization and the need for productivity enhancements. Wellum emphasizes a disciplined, value-oriented investment approach.

Key discussion points

  • Jonathan Wellum's "six Ds" framework for macro analysis includes digitization (AI, data centers, EVs, robotics), debt, currency debasement, deglobalization (reshoring, US-China tension), demographics (aging population), and dear valuations.
  • Wellum expresses concern about current market valuations, particularly in the AI sector, noting that the market has not had a substantial pullback and that premium valuations on AI companies carry significant risk due to uncertainties in returns, pricing, commodification, and obsolescence.
  • Rocklink Investment Partners maintains approximately 20-23% cash in their portfolio as an optionality to invest during market weakness and to dampen volatility.
  • Wellum believes a potential "compute glut" in AI, similar to the dot-com fiber glut, would have a broader market impact than the 2000 tech bubble, affecting financial services and demand for energy and metals.
  • AI is expected to be transformative for the mining industry by improving the efficiency of mineral identification and extraction through technologies like remote sensing and potentially robotics, thereby reducing costs and increasing output.
  • Wellum highlights the potential for AI to enhance productivity, which is crucial given aging populations and high debt levels, and to improve healthcare through better diagnostics and drug discovery.
  • Wellum acknowledges AI's potential to combat fraud, waste, and abuse in government spending and enhance cybersecurity, but cautions that political will is necessary for effective implementation.
  • Deglobalization and the "production for security" (PROSEC) trade are significant trends, leading to reshoring and increased investment in critical minerals and resources within the Western Hemisphere, benefiting industrial companies and those involved in resource extraction.
  • Wellum sees the Western Hemisphere, including Canada, the US, Mexico, and South American countries, as a self-sufficient region for resources, reducing dependency on volatile areas like China and the Middle East.
  • Rocklink maintains a heavy weighting in precious metals and minerals due to irreversible currency debasement trends and increasing demand from the AI revolution, noting structural deficits in silver and copper.
  • Wellum identifies specific investment opportunities in software companies that were oversold due to AI fears (e.g., Roper Technologies), commercial property and casualty insurance companies, industrial businesses (e.g., Carlile, Schneider Electric), and Sprott Inc. (SPR) as a leveraged play on commodity ETFs.
  • Wellum believes the long-term fundamentals for precious metals and minerals are incredibly strong, with structural deficits and increasing demand providing a strong price floor, despite potential volatility.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Jonathan Wellum views current market valuations as "dear" and expresses caution, particularly regarding the AI sector, where he believes premium valuations are difficult to justify given uncertainties in returns and potential commodification.
  • He maintains a significant cash position (20-23%) to capitalize on market pullbacks and reduce volatility.
  • Wellum predicts that a significant correction in AI-related stocks would have a broad market impact, extending beyond the tech sector to financial services and commodity demand.
  • He sees strong long-term opportunities in hard assets, including precious metals (gold, silver) and industrial metals (copper), driven by currency debasement, deglobalization, and the material demands of the AI revolution.
  • Wellum advocates for investing in companies that benefit from deglobalization and reshoring trends, such as industrial companies and those involved in critical mineral extraction within the Western Hemisphere.
  • He identifies specific investment areas: oversold software companies (e.g., Roper Technologies), commercial property and casualty insurance companies, and Sprott Inc. (SPR) as a leveraged play on commodity ETFs.
  • Wellum emphasizes the importance of dollar-cost averaging and disciplined investing in volatile sectors like precious metals, focusing on well-managed companies with strong fundamentals.
  • He highlights the potential for micro-reactors to transform the mining industry by providing cheaper and more efficient energy in remote locations, significantly reducing operating costs.
