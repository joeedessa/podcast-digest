What Wall Street Looked Like Before Buybacks

Overview

This episode features Meb Faber interviewing a guest about the historical context and evolution of share repurchases. The guest, who co-authored one of the earliest books on the topic, discusses how buybacks emerged as a corporate finance tool in the post-WWII era to rebalance over-equitized balance sheets and has since become a trillion-dollar global phenomenon. They also touch on the common misconceptions about the legality and history of buybacks.

Key discussion points

  • The guest co-authored "The Repurchase of Common Stock" approximately 50 years ago, advocating for companies to borrow money and repurchase shares to optimize their balance sheets, which were under-leveraged after post-WWII debt paydowns.
  • Share repurchases were considered groundbreaking at the time of the book's publication but have since become a normal and accepted part of sensible corporate management, now exceeding a trillion dollars annually.
  • Meb Faber notes that politicians and gurus often incorrectly claim share repurchases were illegal or non-existent before 20 years ago, despite historical evidence and the guest's early work.
  • The guest believes that company management is generally sharp, thoughtful, and focused on the long term, with most corporate cash management decisions being sensible, disciplined, and driven by boards of directors in shareholders' interests.
  • The guest prefers share buybacks over dividends as an investor, though acknowledges that many individual investors and some institutions still value dividends more.
