AI Trade Dead Money For Now - But Macro Overall Is Pretty Risk-On For Stocks Tia

Host: Jack Farley · Guest: Tian Yang, Co-founder and Head of Research at Variant Perception · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Tian Yang argues that while the overall macro environment remains "risk-on" for equities, the AI trade, particularly in semiconductors, is likely to be "dead money for a while" due to narrative exhaustion and excessive crowding. He suggests rotating into value sectors like energy and financials, and highlights specific tactical opportunities such as buying Sonia futures. Yang also discusses the geopolitical landscape's impact on supply chains and the unique challenges and opportunities in various global equity markets.

Key discussion points

  • Tian Yang believes the macro risk indicators, looking 3 to 6 months out, suggest a "risk on" regime for risk assets, with growth, inflation, policy, and liquidity broadly supportive.
  • Yang states that the "agenic AI" narrative that fueled the semiconductor rally from April is "clearly done," and a new narrative is needed for the sector to regain highs, leading to a period of "dead money."
  • Variant Perception's LPPL (log periodic power law) bubble and crash detection system signaled "bubble exhaustion" in early June for semis and buy signals for China in June after a final leg down.
  • Yang identifies the UK's Sonia futures as the "cleanest thing live today" for a trade, betting on the Bank of England to be less hawkish due to growth and inflation downside risks.
  • Variant Perception's VPX ETF is running a barbell strategy of long energy and long financials, while starting to buy back into tech and semi names after their crash.
  • Yang explains that the economy has held up due to corporate dissaving (AI capex), fiscal impulses (tariff refunds, OBBA frontloading), and low household savings rates, which keep money flowing.
  • Yang suggests that the "primacy of sovereignty" theme, driven by US-China competition in energy, manufacturing, and technology, will lead governments to step in and facilitate financing even if the private sector exhausts, extending capex cycles.
  • Yang views the demand for "cash settlement" in transactions, as opposed to non-cash forms of financing, as a key signpost for a market downturn, citing mega IPOs like SpaceX as potential catalysts.
  • Yang is skeptical of India as an allocation due to its geopolitical position "caught between China and the US" and high food inflation limiting the RBI's policy flexibility.
  • Yang believes Japan's macro situation is "ridiculously untenable" with high inflation on the ground despite low headline CPI, and that a "Plaza Accord type thing" or a significant BOJ policy shift is needed to resolve currency issues and make Japanese assets more interesting.
  • Yang argues that the Fed's focus under Kevin Warsh is on reforming money markets and restoring their signaling function, which is "existentially important" and outweighs a 25 bps hike.
  • Yang sees gold as having found a floor and likely to trade sideways, needing central bank rate cuts to rally significantly, but still serving a portfolio role as a "stagflation offset" against equity risk.
