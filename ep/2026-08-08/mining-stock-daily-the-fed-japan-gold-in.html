The Fed Japan Gold Intervention Changes Everything

Host: Trevor Hall · Guest: Peter Boockvar, Chief Investment Officer, 1. BFG, Author of The Book Report · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Trevor Hall hosts Peter Boockvar to discuss the unprecedented yen intervention by the US Treasury and Federal Reserve, its implications for the Treasury market, and the broader US economy. Frank Cappelleri then provides technical analysis on market breadth, the S&P 500, semiconductors, gold, silver, and the US dollar, offering specific price targets and trade ideas.

Key discussion points

  • Peter Boockvar believes the recent gold rally could be a reaction to the US Treasury's intervention with Japan to defend the yen, noting the Fed facility allowing Japan to borrow against Treasuries suggests Treasuries are no longer just a "buy and hold thing" for foreigners.
  • Boockvar argues that the Bank of Japan (BOJ) "has to follow through with a rate hike in September" and "in December" to make the yen intervention sustainable, otherwise it will be "all for naught."
  • Boockvar highlights that the ISM manufacturing index rebound is happening globally and is partly driven by inventory restocking and pre-ordering to get ahead of price increases, questioning its sustainability without improved end demand.
  • Boockvar notes that cost pressures are "rampant" for companies, with PPI running "about 200 basis points above CPI," indicating that inflation is higher than consumer prices suggest.
  • Frank Cappelleri observes that the S&P 500's recent strong 4-day move was supported by technology, and that "pristine breadth" has allowed the S&P to break out to new all-time highs despite semiconductors being "off 20% basically from the highs."
  • Cappelleri identifies a bullish flag pattern in the S&P 500, projecting an upside target of 7,680, and a bullish cup and handle pattern targeting 7,925, with an overall trading box projection just above 8,000.
  • Cappelleri views the semiconductor (SMH) chart as a "classic bearish head and shoulders pattern" targeting 460, and has recommended a short position to clients, noting it's "the first short that we recommended at all of them since mid-April."
  • Cappelleri points out that the cumulative S&P advanced decline line and the RSP equal weight S&P 500 ETF have been moving higher, indicating broad market participation even when the S&P itself was struggling.
  • Cappelleri states that gold's recent 4% move, breaking through a triangle pattern and aggressively above its 50-day moving average, is the "only time we can really actually ask that question so far really going back to March" about whether the move is real.
  • Cappelleri suggests that a pullback in gold to retest the 50-day moving average, followed by a close above it, could be a "potential buying opportunity."
  • Cappelleri notes that gold miners (GDX) have shown a "very good first step," pushing through their 50-day moving average and above a long-term downtrend line, with lower lows in price but higher lows in RSI, indicating decelerated selling.
  • Cappelleri describes silver's long-term quarterly chart as a "big multi-decade cup and handle pattern" still in play, with a pullback occurring above 50, and suggests a potential inverse head and shoulders pattern could lead to a rally to 70.
  • Cappelleri highlights the US dollar's (Dixie) significance at the 100 level, noting that its rally in June/July coincided with the S&P losing footing, and that holding below 100 is "very important" for equities.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Peter Boockvar believes the US Treasury's intervention in the yen market, using a Fed facility, signals fragility in the US Treasury market, as foreign entities may view Treasuries as a source of funds rather than just a long-term hold. He suggests this could lead to a global aversion to long-term paper outside of institutional buyers.
  • Boockvar highlights that global manufacturing is rebounding, but questions its sustainability due to potential inventory restocking and the commoditization of AI/data center products, where the US may become a "price taker." He also notes rampant cost pressures for companies, with PPI significantly outpacing CPI.
  • Frank Cappelleri indicates that the S&P 500's recent rally is confirmed by strong market breadth, with financials and industrials breaking out to new highs. He warns that the semiconductor sector (SMH) is showing a bearish head and shoulders pattern, and its continued underperformance or decline could eventually pull the broader market down.
  • Cappelleri sees gold's recent breakout above its 50-day moving average and a triangle pattern as a significant positive shift, suggesting a potential buying opportunity on a retest of the 50-day. He notes similar constructive signs in gold miners (GDX).
  • Cappelleri views silver as still "pretty ugly" but with potential for a bullish formation (inverse head and shoulders) if it can stabilize and break above its 50-day moving average.
  • Cappelleri emphasizes the critical importance of the US dollar (Dixie) holding below the 100 level for equity market stability, noting that a significant dollar rally could negatively impact the S&P 500.
