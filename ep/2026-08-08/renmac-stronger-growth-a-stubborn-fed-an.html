Stronger Growth A Stubborn Fed and Tariff Redux

Host: Steve Duttenhofer, Jeff deGraaf, Neil Dutta, Steve Pavlik · Guest: Dr. Steve Myin, Former Chairman of the White House Council of Economic Advisors and a key architect of the administration's tariff framework, former Federal Reserve member, and Senior Strategist at Hudson Bay Capital.

Overview

Dr. Steve Myin argues that US economic growth has solid underlying fundamentals driven by supply-side improvements, including tax policy, deregulation, and technology, pushing potential growth closer to 3%. He contends that current elevated inflation measures are largely due to measurement error in categories like portfolio management services and software, which the Fed should look through. Myin believes the Fed should be less data-dependent and more forecast-dependent, focusing on monetary policy lags that affect the economy 12-18 months out. Jeff deGraaf notes a significant rotation in markets away from tech and AI momentum, with new leadership emerging in other sectors.

Key discussion points

  • Dr. Steve Myin believes US potential economic growth is closer to 3% (around 2.8%) due to improved tax policy, deregulation, and technological advancements, particularly in AI, with upside risk as AI adoption matures.
  • Myin attributes elevated PCE inflation to measurement errors, specifically in portfolio management services (contributing 40 basis points to core PCE) and software (contributing 20 basis points), arguing these categories should show deflation or quality adjustments not currently captured.
  • Myin asserts that the Fed should set policy based on forecasts for inflation 12-18 months out, not on recent data prints, due to monetary policy lags. He finds it confusing that the FOMC voted to hold in June but hiked in July despite intervening data showing lower inflation and a disappointing jobs report.
  • Myin views the recent rise in interest rates as primarily driven by increases in real rates, reflecting changes in long-run growth expectations and corporate saving/borrowing behavior due to AI investment, rather than a loss of Fed credibility, as inflation expectations (CPI swaps) remain well-behaved.
  • Jeff deGraaf observes a "massive rotation" in the market, with momentum in tech and AI cracking. He expects a bounce in these names but anticipates they will drift and become more challenging, with new leadership emerging in sectors like healthcare (e.g., Amgen, Illiam, Bristol Myers).
  • Steve Pavlik highlights the upcoming August 19th deadline for Trump's 50% tariff threat on Canada, suggesting it's a tactic to force concessions and negotiations on USMCA.
  • Myin justifies tariffs on multiple grounds: national security (domestic defense production), financial stability (reducing the explosively negative net international investment position), and tax policy (shifting tax burden to foreign exporters and reducing domestic income tax distortions).
  • Neil Dutta is watching the jobs number and next week's inflation data (CPI and PPI), particularly the wedge between CPI and PCE, to gauge the likelihood of a September FOMC hike.
