Misunderstood Stocks in Data Center Power Cybersecurity and Payments Dean Deiya

Host: Jack Farley · Guest: Deya Pernas, Co-Founder, Pernas Research · Listen at source ↗

Overview

Jack Farley interviews Deya and Dean Pernas of Pernas Research about their investment philosophy and current market views. The Pernas brothers discuss their approach to identifying misunderstood stocks in themes such as data center power, cybersecurity, and payments, emphasizing fundamental analysis and a contrarian perspective. They highlight the impact of AI on various sectors, particularly the energy demands of data centers and the evolving landscape of cybersecurity threats.

Key discussion points

  • Dean Pernas believes open-weight AI models are bullish for the broader AI ecosystem (chips, infrastructure, application layers) because they commoditize frontier models, increasing demand for other layers as enterprise AI use becomes cheaper.
  • Deya Pernas argues that hyperscalers are existentially forced to make massive capital outlays for AI with uncertain ROI, creating vulnerability in the top-weighted S&P 500 and opportunities for active managers in companies that benefit from this spend without similar CapEx.
  • Dean Pernas identifies "bring your own power" as the largest theme in data centers for the next three to five years, driven by an antiquated US energy grid and AI's projected 20% share of US energy demand by 2030.
  • Dean Pernas also highlights increasing data bandwidth needs between data centers as a key theme, due to hyperscalers building smaller, geographically dispersed data centers that require extensive communication.
  • Deya Pernas explains that cybersecurity is entering a new chapter where AI enables bad actors to find and exploit code vulnerabilities more easily, shifting the focus from identity-based attacks to exposure management.
  • Deya Pernas views the market's sell-off in software stocks as partially warranted due to decelerating growth rates and high stock-based compensation, but sees opportunities in companies with unique data, strong security/compliance features, deep integrations, or real-world components.
  • Deya Pernas is bullish on cross-border money movement companies like Wise and Remitly, citing their ability to drive down costs compared to traditional correspondent banking and benefit from continuous M2 growth and the electronification of cash.
  • Dean Pernas expresses concern about the semiconductor industry due to China's increasing competitiveness and ability to produce components at lower costs, leading Pernas Research to largely avoid the sector.
  • Deya Pernas is skeptical of stablecoins disrupting cross-border payments for consumers and small businesses, noting their primary use in crypto functions and lack of adoption in local economies for goods and services.
  • Dean Pernas notes that Meta's AI strategy has become "murky," moving from an open-source approach to potentially becoming a cloud provider, and expresses a negative outlook on the stock due to perceived floundering and ineffective capital allocation in AI.
  • Deya Pernas emphasizes that Pernas Research's investment process involves turning over "a ton of rocks," with a hit rate of roughly one in a hundred for companies that involve significant research, which also informs their cash weighting.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Deya Pernas: The S&P 500 is vulnerable due to hyperscalers' massive, uncertain CapEx spending on AI, creating opportunities for active managers to find companies benefiting from this spend at reasonable valuations.
  • Dean Pernas: The US energy grid is antiquated and cannot meet the projected 20% of US energy demand by AI by 2030, leading to a sustained "bring your own power" theme for data centers for the next 3-5 years.
  • Deya Pernas: The cybersecurity landscape is undergoing a structural shift, with AI enabling more sophisticated attacks on code vulnerabilities, making exposure management a critical and growing area.
  • Deya Pernas: M2 growth is expected to continue at 5-6% annually, making payment companies that can defend their take rates and benefit from this growth very powerful.
  • Dean Pernas: China's increasing competitiveness in semiconductors (EVs, drones, memory, lithography machines) poses a significant threat to the industry, making it a difficult sector to play.
