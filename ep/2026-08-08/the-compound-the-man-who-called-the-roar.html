The Man Who Called the Roaring 2020s TCAF 254

Host: Josh Brown, Michael Batnick, Ben Carlson · Guest: Ed Yardeni, President of Yardeni Research

Overview

Ed Yardeni discusses his "Roaring 2020s" thesis, initiated in August 2020, arguing that the economy's resilience and technological innovation, particularly AI, will prevent a recession through the end of the decade. He emphasizes the importance of earnings growth and profit margins as key drivers for the S&P 500, projecting significant upside despite market concentration concerns. Yardeni also shares his views on the bond market, the Federal Reserve's policy, and the impact of demographic shifts.

Key discussion points

  • Ed Yardeni initiated his "Roaring 2020s" call in August 2020, drawing parallels to the 1920s post-Spanish Flu and World War I, predicting economic resilience despite initial pandemic anxieties.
  • Yardeni views AI as an "evolutionary, not revolutionary" part of the digital revolution that began in the mid-1960s, emphasizing its role in processing information and leveraging data as a "fourth factor of production."
  • Yardeni believes the US economy will not experience a recession through the end of the decade, citing its resilience against the pandemic, supply disruptions, Fed tightening, and geopolitical events.
  • Yardeni argues that current earnings growth, particularly the "fabulous earnings momentum" (FIMO) seen in analyst consensus forecasts, is grounded in strong revenues and expanding profit margins, not a bubble.
  • Yardeni attributes rising profit margins, especially in Information Technology and Communication Services (45% of S&P 500), to their high productivity and "creative destruction" through continuous innovation.
  • Yardeni dismisses concerns about an "earnings bubble" driven by hyperscaler capex, noting that demand for compute still outstrips supply and that the market for these components is liquid and competitive.
  • Yardeni challenges the "K-shaped economy" thesis, arguing that while the rich are getting richer, the "G-shaped economy" (generational wealth of baby boomers) is a significant, overlooked positive factor supporting consumption and intergenerational transfers.
  • Yardeni views market "shock absorbers," such as distressed asset funds and the Fed's liquidity provisions during crises, as crucial in preventing systemic blowups like Long-Term Capital Management or the 2008 banking crisis.
  • Yardeni believes the current market concentration, while high, is not indicative of a 1999-style bubble, pointing to broader market participation and strong earnings growth across many sectors beyond the "Magnificent Seven."
  • Yardeni suggests that the "dot bubble" experience acts as a "governor" on the current rally, making investors more cautious and preventing excessive multiple expansion.
  • Yardeni, as a fundamental analyst, respects technical analysis, particularly for commodities like oil and gold, viewing charts as indicators of "the madness of crowds" and market psychology.
  • Yardeni, who coined the term "bond vigilantes" in 1983, believes that 4-5% bond yields are a "healthy" and "bullish" level, representing a return to the "old normal" before the Great Financial Crisis.

Market & macro takeaways

  • Ed Yardeni believes the US economy is exceptionally resilient, capable of avoiding a recession through 2029, driven by technological innovation and strong corporate earnings.
  • Ed Yardeni sees AI as a significant, ongoing productivity enhancer for companies, leading to sustained high profit margins, particularly in tech and communication services.
  • Ed Yardeni argues that the current S&P 500 rally is earnings-led, not driven by speculative multiple expansion like the dot-com bubble, and that market breadth is improving.
  • Ed Yardeni highlights the "G-shaped economy" (generational wealth of baby boomers) as a powerful, underappreciated force supporting consumption and the overall economy.
  • Ed Yardeni suggests that the bond market's "old normal" of 4-5% yields is healthy and allows for efficient capital allocation, contrasting with the "rigged" market post-GFC.
  • Ed Yardeni advocates for the Fed to use the 2-year Treasury yield as a direct guide for monetary policy, potentially leading to more frequent, market-driven rate adjustments.
  • Ed Yardeni views increased corporate reporting (quarterly earnings) and more market information as beneficial for investors, opposing proposals for less frequent reporting.
