Host: Patrick Ceresna · Guest: Chase Taylor, Pine Cone Macro · Listen at source ↗

Overview

This episode features Patrick Ceresna and guest Chase Taylor discussing the current market landscape, with a particular focus on the re-acceleration of crude oil prices and its knock-on effects on inflation, bond markets, and risk assets. Taylor emphasizes the binary nature of the oil market, tied directly to the ongoing war, and offers his views on the implications for various asset classes, including fixed income, equities, and commodities.

Key discussion points

  • Chase Taylor views the recent drop in crude oil prices to the $60s as "one of the craziest things I've ever seen in markets" and "a gift to anyone paying attention," given the subsequent rise back to $100 without full Chinese demand.
  • Taylor believes the war's continuation is the primary driver for higher oil prices, stating, "as long as this war is going on, oil's going to keep going higher. Once the war is off, it's going to go a lot lower. It's it's it's truly that binary."
  • Regarding inflation, Taylor notes that "super core" CPI was 0.5% month-over-month, annualizing above 5%, and argues that "if nominal GDP is eight, diesel six bucks, like yeah, you're going to have a bunch of inflation."
  • Taylor suggests that the front end of the yield curve, specifically the 2-year Treasury, is becoming attractive from the long side, despite his "hardcore inflationist" stance, as the market has priced in 3.6 hikes, which he believes might be overdone.
  • Patrick Ceresna highlights the collapse in market breadth, with only the "MAG Sevens" and energy stocks holding up the S&P 500, questioning where future leadership will come from if the broader market deteriorates.
  • Taylor believes the "MAG Sevens" could become a drag if the war continues, driving up the cost of capital and impacting their earnings, which he feels are currently "flattering earnings way more than than it probably should."
  • Taylor places a 65% probability on a surprise ending to the war this year, leading to a normalization of straight flows, which he expects would cause inflation expectations to drop and the Fed to ease its hawkish stance, fueling a stock market rally.
  • Taylor notes that copper is a "crowded decisively bullish" trade, with large speculators holding near five-year highs in net positioning, and cautions that "if everyone loves copper, it's about to have a bad time."
  • Taylor views natural gas as having further downside, potentially to $3, due to ample North American supply and the expectation of a long, mild shoulder season, but acknowledges that a "big cold shot east of the Rockies in November" could reverse the trend.
  • Regarding grains, Taylor sees the current rally as "middle" innings, with tropical commodities like rice, sugar, and cocoa being the "best bet" due to more concentrated production issues and weather impacts.
  • Taylor introduces the KROP ETF (Crop) as his "favorite chart pattern in the world," a "Bubba chart" (beat up big base alpha), suggesting it has potential for a nice run if the agriculture space continues its bullish trend.
