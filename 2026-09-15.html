AI: Peak Hype or New Inflationary Regime? (4 shows)

THESIS: The narrative around Artificial Intelligence is fracturing. The debate is no longer just about which companies will win, but about the fundamental macroeconomic impact of the technology, with camps diverging on whether it is an inflationary force or a growth bubble that is starting to pop.

PEAK CAPEX / SLOWDOWN CAMP (Ben Brey, John J. Hardy, Steve Eisman's guests) This camp believes the initial investment boom is unsustainable. Ben Brey — Benny & The Squirrel argues that AI capex, a key GDP driver, peaked in Q2 and is now rolling over, which will become a drag on growth. John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call discusses the "AI slowdown" narrative being pushed by executives at firms like Anthropic, which he and others like Michael Burry see as potentially "self-serving" to cover for slowing growth or to erect regulatory moats. The team on Steve Eisman — Steve Eisman adds to this by questioning the financial viability of key players like OpenAI, which is reportedly "bleeding cash every single day" despite massive revenue growth.

INFLATIONARY BOOM CAMP (Mike Kopoulos) In direct opposition, Mike Kopoulos — Excess Returns argues AI is currently a powerful inflationary force. He cites the massive capital spending on infrastructure, the corresponding surge in energy demand, the scarcity of specialized labor, and the wealth effect from AI stocks as four distinct channels driving prices higher right now. He notes that despite the AI hype, productivity has actually gotten worse, not better.

KEY TENSION: Whether the eventual productivity gains from AI will materialize fast enough to offset the massive, front-loaded inflationary costs of its physical buildout.
