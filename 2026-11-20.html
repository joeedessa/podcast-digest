DOMINANT THEMES

The AI Credit Cycle: Financing the Future or a Bubble in the Making? (4 shows)

THESIS: The narrative around the AI boom is shifting from unbridled growth to a more cautious focus on financing. The massive capital expenditure required for the buildout is increasingly reliant on debt markets, not just free cash flow. This is introducing credit risk, widening bond spreads for tech giants, and has now appeared on the FOMC's radar as a potential financial stability concern.

CONCERNED (Marson Zudnick, Alex Morris, Kevin Muir, John J. Hardy) The credit market is showing signs of stress that the equity market may be ignoring. Marson Zudnick — Excess Returns points to the cost of insuring Nvidia's debt (CDS) tripling in 2026 and new bond issues from companies like Meta and SpaceX pricing at significantly wider spreads. Alex Morris — Excess Returns notes that AI companies have shown an ability to "crowd out Uncle Sam" for capital, a new and potentially destabilizing dynamic. Kevin Muir — Wealthion argues the sector is in an "earnings bubble" driven by accounting that recognizes revenue upfront while amortizing costs, making valuations appear more reasonable than they are. John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call highlights that the FOMC minutes explicitly mentioned "concerns around the financial stability related to financing the AI buildout," confirming this is now a top-level macro issue.

BULLISH (Mike Wilson) The focus is evolving from the infrastructure enablers to the adopters. Mike Wilson — Morgan Stanley argues the bigger opportunity is now in companies successfully implementing AI. He notes that the 44% of S&P 500 companies citing quantifiable benefits from AI are seeing stronger earnings revisions, suggesting the theme is broadening and becoming more fundamentally grounded.

KEY TENSION: Resolution depends on whether AI-related companies can continue to access capital markets at reasonable rates. A credit market seizure or a significant widening of spreads would force a capex slowdown, validating the bears and likely triggering a broader market sell-off.

The Great Commodity Divide: Speculative Washout or Fundamental Breakdown? (5 shows)

THESIS: A violent sell-off across the metals complex is forcing a debate on whether this is a temporary, technically-driven flush of speculators, creating a buying opportunity, or a durable signal of a global economic slowdown that invalidates the bullish structural narrative.

STRUCTURAL BULLS (Ole Hansen, Rick Rule, Peter Schiff) This is a classic washout setting up the next leg higher. Ole Hansen — Macro Voices highlights that speculative net-long positioning across 25 major commodities collapsed by 77% in two months, while the underlying index only fell 10%, suggesting the selling is overdone. Rick Rule — The Monetary Matters Network emphasizes the multi-decade underinvestment in copper, which will lead to "rationing by price" due to electrification demand. Peter Schiff — The Julia La Roche Show sees the sell-off in gold and silver as a gift, driven by a misguided belief in the Fed's ability to control inflation, and reiterates his long-term thesis of a sovereign debt and currency crisis.

TACTICAL BEARS (Dale Pinkert, Kevin Muir) The price action is a clear warning sign. Dale Pinkert — Maggie Lake Talking Markets is explicitly short copper, arguing its role as an economic bellwether is signaling recession, a message more potent than long-term supply stories. Kevin Muir — Wealthion, while a long-term gold bull, warns that a hawkish Fed, rising real rates, and a strong dollar are powerful short-term headwinds for precious metals, suggesting they could fall further in a liquidity-driven market event before the ultimate bull case plays out.

KEY TENSION: The conflict will be resolved by upcoming global growth and inflation data. A confirmed recession would validate the bearish price signals, while a soft landing or re-acceleration amidst sticky inflation would support the structural bull case.

  • Copper companies will increasingly sell gold and silver byproduct streams to finance development.
  • Major gold and silver miners will become "extremely acquisitive" to replenish their depleted project pipelines.
  • Japan will be a "surprise user" of uranium within the next two years as it restarts its nuclear fleet.

SPOTLIGHT: First Majestic's $1.2 Billion War Chest and the Next Silver Opportunity

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Monty Alkahavaji, First Majestic Silver

  • First Majestic Silver is sitting on over $1.2 billion in cash and is investing heavily in expansions and the restart of its Jared Canyon gold mine (production targeted for H2 2027).
  • Despite lower Q3 silver production, margins expanded dramatically (from $13-$14/oz to $31-$32/oz) by strategically lowering cutoff grades to maximize economics at high metal prices.
  • The long-term outlook for silver is bullish due to a persistent annual supply deficit of 50-100 million ounces and new demand from AI, nuclear, and solid-state batteries.
  • The company is actively looking for M&A but is highly selective, focusing on strategic, silver-focused assets in good jurisdictions.

SPOTLIGHT: First Mining Gold: The Permitting Breakthrough at Springpole

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Paul Morris, Director of Investor Relations, First Mining Gold

  • First Mining Gold is advancing two of Canada's largest undeveloped gold projects, Springpole (Ontario) and Duparquet (Quebec).
  • The Springpole project received federal approval in June and is awaiting provincial approval, expected "very soon," potentially by the end of November 2026.
  • The company offers significant leverage to the gold price; every $100 increase adds ~$130 million to Springpole's after-tax NPV.
  • Key upcoming catalysts include the provincial approval, a definitive feasibility study for Springpole in Q3 2027, and a resource update for Duparquet in early 2027.

SPOTLIGHT: Introducing Tiernan Gold: Advancing the Volcan Gold Project in Chile's Maricunga Belt

Host: Ian Wagner Guest: Fausto Draani, CEO, Tiernan Gold Corp

  • Tiernan Gold's Volcan project in Chile holds a massive 11 million ounce gold endowment (9.88M M&I).
  • At current gold prices (~$4,000/oz), the project's NPV exceeds $4 billion with an IRR approaching 60%, based on the 2025 PEA.
  • The company is well-capitalized with ~$47M Canadian in cash, sufficient to fund it through its next major catalysts: a Pre-Feasibility Study (PFS) in H1 2027 and an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) submission in H2 2027.
  • A key de-risking factor is the company's strong water rights, possessing two permitted wells that exceed the project's needs.

SPOTLIGHT: The $85 Million Partnership Transforming Westhaven Gold

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Ken Armstrong, CEO, Westhaven Gold

  • An $85 million earn-in and joint venture agreement with Dundee Corporation fully funds Westhaven's development and exploration, meaning no further shareholder dilution from equity placements.
  • The company is completing a 50,000-meter drill program focused on infill drilling to upgrade resource categories.
  • Major catalysts are on the horizon: a new resource estimate in February 2027, followed by a pre-feasibility study (PFS) in the second half of 2027.
  • The political environment in British Columbia is seen as supportive, with the government granting nine new mine or expansion permits in the last two years.

SPOTLIGHT: Troilus Mining: The $1.4 Billion Gold-Copper Project Approaching Construction

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Carolyn Arsenault, Vice President of Corporate Communications, Troilus Mining Corp.

  • Troilus is advancing a past-producing brownfield gold-copper project in Quebec, having grown the resource from 2 million to 14.5 million ounces since 2017.
  • The project is nearing a construction decision, with a $1.2 billion debt mandate largely secured and construction permits expected in H1 2027.
  • At a base case of $3,600 gold, the project is projected to generate $7 billion in after-tax free cash flow over its life.
  • The project's financing is tied to its copper production, with offtake agreements in place with European smelters Arubis and Bolin.

SPOTLIGHT: How I Predicted the 2008 Housing Crisis (The Big Short Explained)

Host: Steve Eisman Guest: Solo episode.

  • Steve Eisman's conviction came from meticulous, data-driven research using monthly loan performance data that showed a clear and dramatic deterioration in underwriting standards from 2004 to 2006.
  • Key data points were rising 30-day delinquency rates in new mortgage pools and the increasing percentage of "no-doc" or "liar loans."
  • The entire subprime system was predicated on rising home prices enabling constant refinancing, as borrowers were only qualified on low 2-year "teaser" rates.
  • The market, regulators, and government were all unaware of the scale of the problem, ignoring publicly available data because "incentives trump ethics every time."
  • The post-crisis Dodd-Frank legislation significantly de-risked the regulated banking system by reducing leverage (e.g., Citigroup from 40:1 to 10:1).
