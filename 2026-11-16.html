SPOTLIGHT: Ep 96 - Does China's anti-involution campaign finally have teeth?

Host: Dinny McMahon Guest: Cosimo Rees, Head of Clean Energy and EV Research, Trivium

  • Thesis: Beijing is shifting from ineffective "browbeating" to a systematic, "full court press" to combat industrial overcapacity ("involution") in sectors like autos and solar.
  • New Tools: The new approach involves a coherent set of policies targeting accounting loopholes (e.g., bundling subsidies into revenue), tightening trade receivable payment terms, and adjusting tax policies (e.g., reducing VAT rebates for NEV exports, adding a consumption tax on batteries).
  • Scale of the Problem: The auto industry has capacity for 45-50 million vehicles versus domestic sales of less than 20 million, with profit margins collapsing from 8% in 2017 to 1.5% in H1 2026. The solar industry is in its 13th or 14th consecutive quarter of losses.
  • Skepticism: Cosimo Rees — Trivium China is skeptical about the short-term success of these measures in resolving the deep-seated overcapacity issues, particularly given the incentives for local governments to protect "pillar industries."
  • Global Impact: He is also skeptical that this will be the solution Europe is hoping for, as distressed Chinese companies will likely "continue to fight tooth and nail for their survival" by aggressively exporting.
