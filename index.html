SPOTLIGHT: Global Upheaval in Energy Markets Episode 5 Stephanie Duran

Host: David Greely Guest: Stephanie Duran, Acting CEO, Asia Natural Gas and Energy Association (ANGEA)

  • The Middle East conflict has severely disrupted Asia's energy supply, as 60% of its crude and 25% of its LNG previously transited the Strait of Hormuz.
  • In response, Asian nations are prioritizing energy security and affordability over decarbonization, shifting procurement strategies from "cheapest best" to a focus on reliability.
  • This is driving diversification of supply, with increasing interest in LNG from the Americas (US, Canada) to avoid choke points and secure long-term contracts.
  • A significant build-out of infrastructure (terminals, storage, gas-fired power plants) is needed across Asia to connect new supply with demand, creating major investment opportunities.
  • Cross-border Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) is emerging as a long-term solution, where countries like Singapore could export captured CO2 to neighbors like Indonesia or Australia for storage.

SPOTLIGHT: China's Bet on Physical AI

Host: Laura Wang Guest: Robin Xing, Chief China Economist, Morgan Stanley Research

  • China is focused on "physical AI," the second chapter of the AI revolution, which involves industry-wide adoption in factories, ports, and logistics.
  • A two-stage macro impact is expected: limited growth until 2028 from initial investment in enablers (chips, GPUs), followed by a broader productivity boom from 2028 onwards.
  • From 2028, physical AI is projected to add 0.3 percentage points to China's productivity gain and triple the capex contribution from today's levels.
  • This shift, combined with policy changes reducing inefficient subsidies, is expected to improve industrial profit margins from 5% to 8% in the next 3-5 years.
  • For investors, this could drive Chinese equity market earnings growth from 6% annually to over 10% by 2030, with ROE rising from 10% to over 12%.

SPOTLIGHT: BBC's HARDtalk's Stephen Sackur From Watergate to Cambridge Analytica

Host: Jimmy Connor Guest: Stephen Sackur, former Chief Presenter on BBC's Hard Talk

  • Public trust in mainstream media has plummeted from 75-80% in the Watergate era to around 25% today, making it harder for journalism to hold power accountable.
  • The modern information landscape is "chaotic, fragmented, anarchic," and big tech companies have a strategic incentive to "cozy up to political power" rather than serve the public interest.
  • The Cambridge Analytica scandal, exposed by journalist Carole Cadwalladr, revealed how private Facebook data was used to manipulate elections, including the 2016 US election and Brexit.
  • Sackur argues that valuing and supporting independent, truth-telling journalism is a crucial pillar for maintaining a meaningful democracy against a global trend of rising authoritarianism.
