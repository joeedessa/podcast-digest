TL;DR (read this first)

Ben Inker of GMO and Wolf Richter of WolfStreet.com are leading a powerful consensus that the AI-driven economic boom is a bubble set to pop. They argue the combination of an uncertain revenue model, rising capital costs, and an unprecedented wave of upcoming equity supply from mega-IPOs (SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI) will trigger a significant market correction within 18 months. The central conflict pits this bubble thesis against Ben Hunt's view that the AI ecosystem is now "too big to fail" and will ultimately receive a government backstop, socializing the losses.

  • CENTRAL TENSION: The AI capex boom is the sole engine of US growth, but an impending wave of equity supply and uncertain revenue models threatens to pop the bubble.
  • TOP THEME: The AI Bubble Pop — Multiple speakers see an imminent and significant correction driven by equity supply, slowing spend, and a credit crunch.
  • DIVERGENT: Wolf Richter avoids gold as "vulnerable" at high prices, while Michael Pento sees it replacing the US dollar as foreign creditors flee Treasuries.
  • HIGH-CONVICTION TRADE: LONG Commodities/Resources, with Peter Boockvar, Ben Inker, and Dale Pinkert all advocating for exposure to hard assets and energy.
  • CALENDAR: The next 12-18 months, which Ben Inker identifies as the window for mega-IPOs (SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI) to hit the market, supplying 6-7.5% of US market cap.

DOMINANT THEMES

The AI Bubble & The Impending Pop (6 shows)

THESIS: The massive capex spending on AI is the primary, and perhaps only, driver of US economic growth, creating a classic speculative bubble. Multiple analysts believe this bubble is set to pop within the next 18 months, triggered by a combination of an unprecedented wave of equity supply from mega-IPOs, an uncertain path to profitability for AI services, and tightening credit conditions. The key question is whether a government backstop will arrive before or after a significant market crash.

BEARS (Ben Inker, Wolf Richter, Michael Pento, Dale Pinkert) The case for an imminent pop is multi-faceted. Ben Inker — The Monetary Matters Network argues the primary catalyst will be equity supply, with upcoming IPOs from SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI set to flood the market with 6-7.5% of its total cap, an amount he claims will lead to a 24% market underperformance. Wolf Richter — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® focuses on the flawed business model, noting that AI pricing is "super deflationary" and there won't be enough revenue to justify the massive investments. Michael Pento — Competent Man Podcast - Tom Bodrovics views the AI boom as a credit-fueled inflationary impulse that the Federal Reserve will be forced to pop with higher rates. Dale Pinkert — Maggie Lake Talking Markets sees the bust manifesting in the charts, pointing to a "failing rally" in semiconductors as a leading indicator of a broader market decline.

CAUTIOUSLY BULLISH (Ben Hunt, Paul Tudor Jones) This camp acknowledges the bubble dynamics but sees a different endgame. Ben Hunt — Excess Returns argues the AI ecosystem is now "too big to fail" and that the US government, dependent on AI for growth, will ultimately provide a backstop, socializing the losses. This implies that while a correction is possible, a complete collapse is unlikely, making the core assets a long-term hold. Paul Tudor Jones (via John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call) foresees a potential rally to new highs into year-end but warns of "very challenging conditions for 2027" as buybacks dry up and IPO lockups expire.

KEY TENSION: Whether the "too big to fail" government backstop materializes before a market-breaking crash is triggered by the massive wave of equity supply and a potential slowdown in AI investment.

The Waning Power of the US Treasury Market (4 shows)

THESIS: The traditional role of US Treasuries as the global risk-free asset is under severe strain from unsustainable domestic debt levels, waning foreign demand, and emerging structural risks within the market itself. This is forcing a repricing of long-duration risk and pushing investors toward alternatives like gold and other hard assets.

BEARS (Peter Boockvar, Michael Pento, John J. Hardy) The credibility of US debt is eroding. Peter Boockvar — Sprott Money states that after 40 years, investors "now care" about US debts and deficits, a sentiment reflected in rising global bond yields. Michael Pento — Competent Man Podcast - Tom Bodrovics provides the mechanism, arguing foreign creditors are actively abandoning US Treasuries for gold due to a lack of trust in an "insolvent nation." He points to Japan, the largest foreign holder, selling Treasuries to defend the yen. John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call introduces a technical risk, citing Harley Bassman's work on negative convexity in the long end, which could cause hedge funds to abruptly unwind the popular basis trade, triggering a liquidity event.

NEUTRAL (Wolf Richter) While acknowledging the long-term issues, the current environment presents an opportunity. Wolf Richter — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® views higher yields as a return to normalcy after two decades of financial repression, making longer-term Treasuries "modestly appealing" and a "valid investment option" for the first time in years.

KEY TENSION: Whether rising long-term yields represent a healthy normalization of interest rates or the beginning of a sovereign debt crisis driven by a buyers' strike.

GUEST BRIEFS

Ben Inker — The Monetary Matters Network

Thesis: The AI sector is a classic transformational technology bubble, and an unprecedented wave of equity supply from upcoming IPOs (SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI) will be the catalyst that pops it within 12-18 months. This supply shock, combined with an increasingly price-inelastic market, will lead to a significant market downturn and credit defaults. Predictions:

  • The AI bubble will pop in the next 12 to 18 months.
  • The market will perform 24% worse than normal if 6% of market cap comes in via IPOs.
  • A scenario where AI demand slows could lead to a 35-50% market decline and massive credit defaults.

Would change view if: Not stated.

Wolf Richter — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money®

Thesis: The US economy is not heading for recession; it is "running hot" due to AI spending and government deficits, with inflation being the primary problem. This inflation is a deliberate strategy to manage the national debt. The key risk to the economy is a future slowdown in AI spending, which would trigger the next downturn. Predictions:

  • No recession in the next couple of quarters.
  • A recession will begin when AI spending starts to decline and AI stocks have a sharp sell-off.

Would change view if: AI spending begins to decline.

Michael Pento — Competent Man Podcast - Tom Bodrovics

Thesis: The US faces an economic reckoning in 2027, as rising interest rates, driven by fiscal insolvency and waning foreign demand for Treasuries, will pop the concurrent bubbles in credit, real estate, and equities. The Fed's hands will be tied, leading to a protracted battle with stagflation and a potential depression. Predictions:

  • 2027 will be a "very challenging" year for the US economy and markets.
  • In the next recession, annual deficits will rise from $2 trillion to $4-6 trillion.
  • Inflation could return "towards the double digits" in the wake of the next recession.

Would change view if: Not stated.

Ben Hunt — Excess Returns

Thesis: The AI ecosystem is "too big to fail" because it is the US economy's only viable growth engine. The government will be forced to provide a backstop, similar to the banks in 2008, to prevent a collapse. This means gains are privatized while losses will be socialized, making the core AI players resilient long-term despite near-term pain. Predictions:

Would change view if: Not stated.

The Fed is now a follower, not a leader, of the yield curve. Investors should focus on shorter-duration income, inflation protection (TIPS), and diversification into hard assets. Predictions:

  • The Fed may get one more rate increase in December.
