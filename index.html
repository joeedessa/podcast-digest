DOMINANT THEMES

The Great Divergence: An Internal Market Crash (6 shows)

THESIS: The S&P 500's strength is a facade, created by passive investment flows into a handful of AI-related mega-cap stocks. Beneath the surface, a majority of the market—the "old economy" or "Modest 7"—is already in a deep correction or recession, a divergence that is now reaching an unsustainable breaking point.

BEARS (Larry McDonald, Jesse Felder, Jim Paulsen, Jeff deGraaf) The market has "crashed internally," with many "Hall of Fame brands" down significantly, a fact obscured by passive investing's distortion of the index (Larry McDonald — The Julia La Roche Show). Market breadth is the worst it has been since the dot-com peak, with the S&P 500 hitting new highs with less than 30% of its components above their 50-day moving average (Jesse Felder — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money®). This narrow leadership, with the "Magnificent 7" rising while the "Modest 7" (the other S&P sectors) decline, is a classic late-cycle warning sign (Jim Paulsen — Excess Returns). This is manifesting as a rotation of "tech money" into defensive sectors like healthcare (Jeff deGraaf — RenMac).

AI BULLS (Jordi Visser) This divergence is not a sign of weakness but a reflection of a new economic reality. The US is experiencing robust growth driven by the AI build-out, and the S&P 500's outperformance is a rational pricing of this productivity boom (Jordi Visser — Less Noise More Signal). The correct way to view this is not as a narrow market, but as a structural shift where the US tech ecosystem is fundamentally outperforming the rest of the world.

KEY TENSION: Resolution will come when either the "old economy" recession and rising credit stress finally pull the mega-cap leaders down, or the AI productivity boom begins to lift the rest of the economy.

The Bond Market's Warning (5 shows)

THESIS: The sharp rise in long-term Treasury yields is the market's most critical signal, but there is fierce debate over what it is signaling: an impending recession, a structural shift to higher inflation and deficits, or a healthy reflection of strong economic growth.

RECESSION IMMINENT (David Rosenberg) The bond market sell-off is a massive mispricing of Fed policy. The economy is weakening and disinflation is taking hold, which will force the Fed to cut rates next year. The current high yields represent a major contrarian buying opportunity for a "bull steepener" (David Rosenberg — Jimmy Connor).

STRUCTURAL SHIFT (Peter Boockvar, Jesse Felder) Yields are rising for fundamental reasons that the Fed cannot control. Investors are demanding a higher premium due to persistent inflation, massive government deficits, and the end of yield curve control by the Bank of Japan (Peter Boockvar — Sprott Money). This is a structural breakout, and yields could target 6% on the 10-year as inflation catches up to the signal from gold (Jesse Felder — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money®).

STRONG GROWTH (Jordi Visser) The rise in yields is not a problem; it is a logical consequence of higher-than-expected global growth, driven by the AI build-out. With US nominal GDP at 6.3%, the bond market is simply adjusting to a more robust economic reality (Jordi Visser — Less Noise More Signal).

KEY TENSION: The upcoming economic data will either validate Rosenberg's recession call (causing yields to fall) or confirm the persistent inflation/strong growth theses (keeping yields elevated or pushing them higher).

SPOTLIGHT: What to Expect From the UK Budget

Host: Andrew Sheets Guest: Bruna Skarica, Morgan Stanley

  • The upcoming UK budget will be a key fiscal event focused on achieving a current budget balance within three years, allowing borrowing only for investment.
  • The UK has made significant progress on fiscal consolidation, with a projected primary deficit under 1% of GDP for 2026-27, far lower than the US (4.9%) or France (2.9%).
  • The UK economy has shown stronger-than-expected growth, driven by an 8% YoY expansion in the tech sector, benefiting from the global AI boom.
  • Despite positive economic data, negative sentiment persists, partly because the market is pricing in more Bank of England rate hikes (four) than Morgan Stanley expects (two).
  • The budget may include announcements on fiscal decentralization, potentially allowing regions to retain more income tax revenues.

SPOTLIGHT: MacroVoices #554: Yasir Arafat: Mass Manufacturing Nuclear Power Plants

Host: Erik Townsend, Patrick Ceresna, Masil Bagnan Guest: Yasir Arafat, Allo Atomics

  • Allo Atomics plans to mass-manufacture nuclear reactors, aiming for a Texas factory to produce 100 reactors (1 gigawatt total) per year.
  • The initial target market is AI data centers, which have an urgent and massive demand for reliable, clean power; the identified demand gap is 100 gigawatts by 2030.
  • Arafat projects that mass production will make nuclear power cheaper than coal and gas within the first few years of factory output.
  • The company's advanced reactors are designed to use standard low-enriched uranium (LEU), avoiding the supply chain bottleneck of high-assay LEU (HALEU).
  • Arafat emphasized that used nuclear fuel is a reusable asset, not waste, and can be recycled in fast reactors to extend the uranium supply from ~117 years to 12,000 years.
