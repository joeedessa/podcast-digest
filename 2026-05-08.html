MACRO ROOM TEMPERATURE

Mike Green's structural thesis of an "almost inevitable" 1987-style crash driven by passive investing's dominance sets a deeply bearish tone, creating a stark tension with Mark Newton's tactically bullish view that there is "no evidence to be bearish" based on current market breadth and earnings. This core conflict between structural fragility and cyclical strength is playing out against a backdrop of a severe oil supply shock, which Martin Rance — Morgan Stanley warns is rapidly depleting inventories, and Louis-Vincent Gave — Macro Voices argues is forcing a global, inflationary shift toward strategic commodity stockpiling.

DOMINANT THEME(S)

The Inevitable Crash vs. The Resilient Bull (5 shows)

A sharp divide exists on the fate of US equities. The bearish camp is led by Mike Green — The Julia La Roche Show, who argues the market is fundamentally broken and overvalued by more than 75%. His research suggests that as passive investing's market share, currently ~55%, approaches a 65-80% threshold within the next two and a half years, the market will enter a "stochastic regime" where explosive volatility and a crash become a near certainty. This view is reinforced by Mike McGlone — Wealth Building Blueprint Vladyslav Grab, who sees the stock market as an unsustainable "Ponzi scheme" at 2.4x GDP and the primary driver of inflation, predicting a potential 50% drawdown. He points to the divergence between low equity volatility and high commodity volatility as a key warning sign. Adding to the concern, Danielle DiMartino Booth — Competent Man Podcast - Tom Bodrovics warns of an impending liquidity crisis and asserts the US is already in a recession masked by official data, with risks of private credit issues bleeding into public markets.

In direct opposition, Mark Newton — Milk Road Macro maintains a "glass-half-full" perspective, stating there is currently no evidence to be bearish. He points to one of the strongest bullish reversals in history, driven by improving market breadth, strong earnings, and AI investment. While he anticipates a short-term 3-5% consolidation in mega-cap tech, he sees this as a buying opportunity and expects the market to broaden out and end the year on a positive note.

The Great Commodity Stockpiling (4 shows)

The era of just-in-time supply chains is over, according to Louis-Vincent Gave — Macro Voices, who argues that recent geopolitical conflicts are forcing a structural shift toward strategic stockpiling of physical commodities. He believes countries must now build inventories of energy, fertilizer, and industrial metals to maintain policy independence, creating a structural long-term tailwind for the entire complex. This thesis is supported by the acute, ongoing oil supply shock detailed by Martin Rance — Morgan Stanley. Rance notes that while initial inventory buffers have muted the price impact of the largest-ever supply disruption, these buffers are being consumed rapidly. He warns that if oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz do not resume within four to six weeks, the market will become "very tight" by early summer.

Broadening the lens, Craig Tinddale — Less Noise More Signal contends that decades of Western de-industrialization have created critical vulnerabilities and a dangerous dependency on nations like China for essential resources. He sees exponential demand for materials like copper and rare earths for AI and electrification clashing with a severely constrained supply, forcing a panicked re-evaluation of global supply chains and a rotation into the industrial and mining sectors that produce these foundational goods.

DIVERGENT VIEWS

US Stock Market Outlook

Mike Green — The Julia La Roche Show: Bearish / Crash Imminent Reasoning: The market is fundamentally broken due to the dominance of passive investing (~55% share), which has made it inelastic and behave like a low-float instrument. As passive share grows at 4% annually and approaches a 65-80% threshold in the next ~2.5 years, a 1987-style crash becomes almost inevitable. The market is currently overvalued by over 75%.

Mark Newton — Milk Road Macro: Bullish / Buy Dips Reasoning: There is no evidence to be bearish. The market has seen one of the strongest bullish reversals in history, supported by improving breadth and strong earnings. Historical precedents for market concentration exist and do not necessarily signal a bubble. A short-term consolidation of 3-5% in mega-caps should be seen as a buying opportunity before the market broadens out and ends the year higher.

Crude Oil

Martin Rance — Morgan Stanley: Bullish Reasoning: The market is facing the largest-ever supply shock. Initial inventory buffers are being consumed rapidly, and if the Strait of Hormuz disruption continues for another 4-6 weeks, the market will become very tight by summer, with US gasoline prices potentially reaching $4.70-$5.00 per gallon.

Mike McGlone — Wealth Building Blueprint Vladyslav Grab: Bearish Reasoning: Significant deflationary forces are at play, driven by political motivation to lower prices ahead of the November election. He expects severe demand destruction and wealth reversion, forecasting December WTI futures to be closer to $50 than $100 by the mid-terms.

Gold & Silver

Mark Newton — Milk Road Macro: Tactically Bearish, then Bullish Reasoning: Expects a short-lived decline for precious metals over the next one to two weeks. This will be followed by a rally to new all-time highs into the September-October timeframe, before a more meaningful, multi-year decline begins.

Mike McGlone — Wealth Building Blueprint Vladyslav Grab: Bearish / Peaked Reasoning: Gold and silver have likely put in multi-year peaks. Gold is at its highest level versus its 60-month moving average since 1980, and silver is at its highest ever versus the Bloomberg Commodity Index. Widespread enthusiasm and "known knowns" are already priced in, making it a time to sell, not buy.

Bitcoin

Mark Newton — Milk Road Macro: Bullish on Dips Reasoning: Has updated his outlook to be more constructive. He expects Bitcoin to hold up and perform relatively better than other cryptocurrencies. A potential pullback to the low $70,000s would present a buying opportunity within a bullish cycle for the year.

Mike McGlone — Wealth Building Blueprint Vladyslav Grab: Bearish / Short Reasoning: Bitcoin's "glory days" have passed, and its performance is now entirely dependent on the stock market's ascent. It is showing divergent weakness versus equities and is a prudent short at current levels, with a long-term key reversion level around $10,000.
