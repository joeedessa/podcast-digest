TL;DR (read this first)

The market is caught between the powerful narrative of an AI-driven productivity boom and the harsh realities of physical and financial constraints. Voices like Tavi Costa and Joe Davis champion the transformative growth story, while a growing chorus of skeptics, including Jesse Felder, Larry McDonald, and Michael Green, warn of a capital-intensive bubble, massive new equity supply, and a looming stagflationary macro environment. This tension is forcing a capital migration from over-owned tech into under-owned hard assets.

  • CENTRAL TENSION: A speculative, AI-fueled equity market is colliding with physical constraints, massive IPO supply, and a deteriorating macro backdrop.
  • TOP THEME: The AI Boom's Second-Order Effects — A multi-trillion dollar supercycle in commodities and energy, or a capital-intensive bubble destined to bust.
  • DIVERGENT: Oil prices are headed to $200 (Matt Smith) due to a supply crisis versus headed lower (John Johnston) due to ample inventory and US production.
  • HIGH-CONVICTION TRADE: Long Gold & Gold Miners, supported by a rotation to hard assets and currency debasement concerns (Tavi Costa, Larry McDonald, Cam Currie, Rick Rule).
  • CALENDAR: Next week's FOMC meeting, viewed as a seminal event that could signal a major policy shift under a new Fed chair.

MACRO ROOM TEMPERATURE

A "great migration" of capital is underway as the market's AI euphoria confronts an impending supply shock. Larry McDonald and Kevin Muir argue that a deluge of tech IPOs and secondary offerings, led by SpaceX and Google, is reversing the era of buybacks and forcing a re-evaluation of the over-owned, capital-intensive tech sector. This capital indigestion, combined with persistent inflation and geopolitical risk, is driving investors toward the tangible, undervalued world of hard assets, energy, and materials, a theme championed by Tavi Costa and Rick Rule.

DOMINANT THEMES

THEME 1 — The AI Boom's Second-Order Effects (21 shows)

THESIS: The AI build-out is a defining structural force, but the market is split on whether it will trigger a sustainable commodity supercycle or is simply a capital-intensive bubble destined for a classic boom-bust cycle. The resolution will determine leadership between tech and real assets for the next decade.

SUPERCYCLE PROPONENTS (Tavi Costa, Mayank Maheshwari, Joe Davis, Jeremy Schwartz) The AI boom necessitates a massive physical world build-out, creating a commodity super-boom. Tavi Costa — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money argues the immense CapEx for data centers, electrical grids, and power stations will create unprecedented demand for copper, silver, nickel, and energy. This is a second-derivative effect the market is under-appreciating. Mayank Maheshwari — Morgan Stanley projects a supercycle with over $5 trillion in new energy investments in Asia alone over the next five years, driven by data center power demand. Joe Davis — Excess Returns sees the economy in a 1996-1997 phase of a full AI build-out, projecting growth 50% above consensus and suggesting significant market momentum remains. Jeremy Schwartz — Maggie Lake Talking Markets believes the insatiable demand for compute is the real driver for markets, showing up in corporate profits even if not yet in productivity stats.

BUBBLE SKEPTICS (Jesse Felder, Michael Green, Dan Rasmussen, Jim Grant, Steve Eisman) The AI narrative is masking a classic capital-intensive bubble characterized by overbuilding, circular financing, and physical constraints. Jesse Felder — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money contends the boom is in a "blowoff phase," with data center build-out projections being physically impossible and facing political backlash, leading to an inevitable bust. Michael Green — Excess Returns points to circular financing, where Nvidia invests in companies that then buy its GPUs, as a feature that radically overstates profits. Dan Rasmussen — Macro Hive views AI as a cyclical commodity business prone to an "overbuild" that will lead to collapsing prices, similar to past energy or shipping booms. Jim Grant — The Meb Faber Show draws parallels to the railroad overbuilding of the 19th century, which led to a generational cascade of price declines and the Panic of 1873. Steve Eisman — Steve Eisman notes the shift from capital-light software to capital-intensive hardware is causing investors to lose interest.

KEY TENSION: Whether the massive capital expenditure flowing into AI infrastructure generates sustainable, broad-based productivity gains and profits, or if it results in a glut of capacity and a price collapse, stranding trillions in investment.

THEME 2 — The Great Migration to Hard Assets (10 shows)

THESIS: A combination of persistent inflation, unsustainable government debt, geopolitical fragmentation, and a massive new supply of financial assets is forcing a structural rotation of capital from overvalued tech stocks into undervalued hard assets and the companies that control them.

THE CASE FOR ROTATION (Larry McDonald, Rick Rule, Cam Currie, Tavi Costa, Jonathan Wellum) A "colossal migration" from financial to hard assets is underway. Larry McDonald — Macro Voices argues we are in a new inflation shock regime, and the massive supply from tech IPOs is forcing capital out of crowded growth stocks and into hard assets, value, healthcare, and energy. Rick Rule — Jimmy Connor is bullish on uranium, gold, silver, and copper, driven by decades of underinvestment and concerns about fiat currency debasement. He believes the US cannot meet its $160 trillion in obligations without a "dishonest default" via money printing. Cam Currie — Mining Stock Daily sees a "tectonic shift" among central banks toward a gold reserve standard, making the current pullback in precious metals a "great buying opportunity." Tavi Costa — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money highlights the disconnect between the clear need for critical commodities for the AI build-out and their "on sale" prices. Jonathan Wellum — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money holds gold as the "ultimate monetary asset" and a critical long-term hedge against the global debt crisis.

KEY TENSION: The timing and magnitude of the rotation depend on whether the Fed is forced to monetize debt to suppress yields, which would accelerate the debasement narrative, or if a genuine economic slowdown materializes, which could temporarily hit both financial and hard assets.

THEME 3 — The IPO Supply Shock & Market Indigestion (8 shows)

THESIS: The era of de-equitization and share buybacks is over, replaced by a massive wave of equity supply from mega-IPOs and secondary offerings that is absorbing market liquidity and creating a significant headwind for the largest tech stocks.

SUPPLY IS THE NEW HEADWIND (Larry McDonald, Erik Townsend, Kevin Muir, Steve Eisman, Michael Green, Jack Farley) The market is struggling to digest an unprecedented amount of new equity. Larry McDonald — Macro Voices points to the "indigestion" from the SpaceX and Google offerings, totaling $150 billion in immediate capital needs, but highlights the more significant concern is the $3 trillion in restricted shares unlocking over the next 12-24 months. Erik Townsend — Macro Voices frames this as the "smartest money in the world" — founders like Musk and Altman — selling their private equity to "public market bag holders." Kevin Muir — The Market Huddle argues this shift from buybacks to issuance is an underappreciated supply/demand dynamic pressuring the "Mag 7." Steve Eisman — Steve Eisman sees Google's $85 billion raise as a key indicator that capital-light software companies are now capital-intensive hardware companies, causing investors to re-evaluate. Michael Green — Competent Man Podcast - Tom Bodrovics suggests recent sell-offs are partly due to a need for liquidity to absorb these massive IPOs. Jack Farley — Forward Guidance notes the underperformance of the "Mag 7" is directly attributable to this reversal from net negative to positive equity supply.

KEY TENSION: Whether the market has sufficient liquidity and appetite to absorb this historic wave of equity supply without causing a significant price correction in the mega-cap tech sector and the broader market.

DIVERGENT VIEWS

Crude Oil Price Outlook

Matt Smith — The Monetary Matters Network: EXTREMELY BULLISH Reasoning: The global oil market is "sleepwalking into a crisis" due to the prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which has created an 11 million bpd supply deficit. Global refined product inventories, particularly in the transparent US market, are "absolutely tanking" and approaching critical lows. Cushing inventories are near "tank bottoms." This physical scarcity will force a market rupture within 1-1.5 months, and the case for $200 oil is "stronger than ever" as prices must rise to destroy demand.

John Johnston — Maggie Lake Talking Markets: BEARISH Reasoning: The bullish narrative is "fear porn" that is contradicted by market prices and data. Global commercial and official oil inventories are very high at 8.2 billion barrels. The U.S. has become an "energy fortress" with the capacity to bring new production online within months. The market itself, with low volume and falling open interest, does not support the shortage narrative. Price is truth, and the price is not reflecting a crisis.

Dale Pinkert — Maggie Lake Talking Markets: BULLISH Reasoning: Geopolitical complacency is misplaced. He anticipates oil will break out over $100/barrel, potentially leading to new highs in the energy sector. He cites a conviction from Nikki Haley that Iran will not agree to a deal, implying military action is likely, which is not priced in by a complacent market.

AI Market Impact & Sustainability

Joe Davis — Excess Returns: BULLISH / LONG RUNWAY Reasoning: The current market is in the 1996-1997 phase of a full AI build-out, not a 1999 peak. This implies significant market momentum remains. He projects economic growth 50% above consensus for the next year, driven by AI, and sees the biggest future beneficiaries being outside tech, in sectors like healthcare and financial services.

Jesse Felder — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: BEARISH / IMMINENT BUST Reasoning: The AI boom is the "blowoff phase" of a market top. Projections for data center build-outs are physically impossible and face growing political opposition. Demand was inflated by subsidies that are now being scaled back. The bust will impact not just semis but also hyperscalers, turning the "Magnificent Seven" into the "Maleficent Seven" as they compete in a capital-intensive space with dubious profitability.

Dan Rasmussen — Macro Hive: BEARISH / CYCLICAL BUST Reasoning: AI is a capital-intensive, cyclical commodity business. Like energy or shipping, it is prone to boom-bust cycles. An inevitable "overbuild" of capacity will lead to collapsing prices and a significant correction, despite the technology's inherent value. The market is priced as if this will never happen, which is a clear sign of a bubble.

Near-Term Precious Metals Outlook

Tavi Costa — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: BULLISH / BUY THE DIP Reasoning: The current market downdraft in gold and silver is a "wonderful opportunity to be adding capital." He views it as a normal correction of froth and a chance to dollar-cost average into discounted assets ahead of the next leg up, driven by the commodity super-boom.

Jesse Felder — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: CAUTIOUS / WAIT FOR SIGNAL Reasoning: Precious metals are in a corrective phase and may remain under pressure due to speculative activity and the potential for rising real interest rates from Fed tightening. He suggests waiting to re-enter until the Fed signals a cutting phase and sentiment has washed out.

Patrick Ceresna — Macro Voices: CAUTIOUS / FURTHER DOWNSIDE Reasoning: Gold is in a consolidation phase with headwinds. He expects further downside to the $3,900 or even $3,400 level before an "extraordinary buying opportunity" emerges in the second half of the year.

US Economic Outlook

Joe Davis — Excess Returns: BULLISH Reasoning: Projects economic growth 50% above consensus for the next year, driven by the productivity and investment boom from AI. He sees upside risk to the equity market.

Jesse Felder — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: STAGFLATIONARY Reasoning: Anticipates a stagflationary scenario with rising unemployment, rising prices (driven by commodities), and a slowing economy, concurrent with an AI bust. Small business surveys already show reduced hiring plans and rising prices.

Dominique — Macro Hive: DISINFLATIONARY / CONSUMER SQUEEZE Reasoning: Core CPI is disinflating, and there is a lack of generalized inflationary pressure. However, households are being squeezed as income growth lags inflation. The contraction in real consumer income over the past three quarters is historically a marker of recession.

SPOTLIGHT: How a Vibecoded Newsletter Is Making the Hay Market More Transparent

Host: Tracy Alloway, Joe Weisenthal Guest: Aiden Johnson, Founder, Haywire

  • The US hay market is highly opaque and fragmented, with prices varying significantly by region and type, making it difficult for farmers to get accurate pricing information.
  • Aiden Johnson's company, Haywire, is using AI to scrape, aggregate, and analyze fragmented public data from USDA reports and supplement it with direct calls to auction houses.
  • This data is then synthesized into a plain-English newsletter, providing actionable market intelligence and transparency to a niche commodity market.
  • Johnson notes that rising hay prices have second-order effects, impacting horse boarding costs and dairy farm operations.
  • The ultimate vision is to create a reliable benchmark for regional hay prices, which could lay the groundwork for a hedgeable, financialized hay market with futures contracts.

SPOTLIGHT: Argentina's COLLAPSE at the 2026 World Cup

Host: Jacob Shapiro Guest: Marko Papic, Juan Correa, Robert Timper

  • BCA Research's proprietary model for forecasting the World Cup uses FIFA EA Sports game data for player ratings, national team caps for experience, and "dummy" variables for host advantage and the "previous winner's curse."
  • The model's most surprising prediction is that Argentina, the defending champion, will finish third in their group and exit early due to the "previous winner's curse."
  • Portugal is a contrarian pick to reach the semifinals, while several African teams like Senegal and Morocco are projected to advance far.
  • The model predicts France will win the 2026 World Cup, leveraging its ability to attract and develop talent from diverse immigrant backgrounds.
  • The discussion highlights how football often reflects and influences societal shifts and geopolitical moments, such as the unification of Germany after the 1990 World Cup.

SPOTLIGHT: Alaska Silver Returns to Illinois Creek with a New Drill Program

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Kit Mars, CEO, Alaska Silver

  • Alaska Silver (TSX Venture: WAM, OTCQX: WAMFF) is commencing a 6,000-meter exploration program at its Illinois Creek Project.
  • The program will focus on expanding the Water Pump Creek resource and exploring the new Silver Sage target to define near-surface mineralization.
  • The company is also conducting metallurgical testing for gallium, a critical mineral, and is seeking non-dilutive financing from the U.S. Department of Defense to advance this part of the project.
  • An updated resource was released earlier in the year, and Mars noted that higher copper prices (now $6/lb) make previously uneconomic material worth extracting, which will improve future resource updates.
