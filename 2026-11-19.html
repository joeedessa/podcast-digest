TL;DR (read this first)

A strong consensus is forming around a 2027 bear market, with key voices like Felix Zulauf, Barry Knapp, and the team at TS Lombard all forecasting a significant downturn. DOMINANT THEMES

The 2027 Bear Market Thesis (5 shows)

THESIS: A strong consensus is forming among multiple independent strategists that 2027 will host a significant bear market or "panic decline." The core argument is that the Federal Reserve will continue its hiking cycle well into next year to combat persistent inflation, eventually breaking the AI-fueled growth cycle and causing a sharp contraction in liquidity and corporate profits.

BEARS (Felix Zulauf, Dario Perkins, Freya Beamish, Barry Knapp, Jared Dillian) The case for a 2027 downturn is multi-faceted. Felix Zulauf — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® predicts a "panic decline" of 20-50% for the NASDAQ 100 in late 2027 as AI investment momentum slows and liquidity gets squeezed. Dario Perkins and Freya Beamish — GlobalData TS Lombard: Perkins Vs Beamish state with "high conviction" that a bear market is coming, driven by a re-accelerating labor market forcing the Fed to hike "one a quarter" through 2027. Barry Knapp — Excess Returns draws a direct parallel to the 2018 market, where the Fed tightened into slowing earnings growth, and sees a potential 10-12% pullback by the end of this year as a prelude. Jared Dillian — The Macro Dirt Podcast is the most bearish, targeting S&P 7,000 due to technical breakdowns and a hawkish Fed.

CAUTIOUSLY OPTIMISTIC (Serena Tang, Neil Dutta) The counter-narrative focuses on the durability of the current cycle. Serena Tang — Morgan Stanley recommends a "maximum overweight" in global equities for 2027, arguing the AI capex cycle remains "intact" and growth is robust. Neil Dutta — RenMac believes the macro outlook for next year "seems okay," with 5% nominal GDP growth, though he shares the bears' concerns for the period beyond, flagging 2028 as a worry if AI productivity gains don't materialize.

KEY TENSION: The resolution hinges on the Fed's reaction function: will they pivot at the first sign of a meaningful growth slowdown, or will they remain singularly focused on inflation, hiking until the AI boom ends and a bear market is triggered?

The AI Investment Cycle: Peak or Plateau? (5 shows)

THESIS: The sustainability and ultimate economic payoff of the AI capital expenditure boom is a central debate. One camp sees an intact, multi-year cycle supporting growth and markets, while the other warns of peaking growth rates, a potential supply glut in compute, and a lack of tangible, broad-based productivity gains so far.

INTACT CYCLE (Serena Tang, Neil Dutta, Felix Zulauf) Proponents argue the cycle has legs. Serena Tang — Morgan Stanley states plainly that "The AI capex cycle is intact," forming the basis of her firm's risk-on outlook for 2027. Neil Dutta — RenMac sees spending continuing through the first 6-9 months of next year due to already-allocated funds and strong capex guidance from hyperscalers. Felix Zulauf — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® believes this continued investment will be the primary driver of one final major market rally into early 2027.

PEAKING & SKEPTICAL (Barry Knapp, David Orr, Jared Dillian) Skeptics point to decelerating growth and future risks. Barry Knapp — Excess Returns argues that "peak AI capex and peak earnings growth" have already occurred, citing a slowdown in capex growth for the big four tech firms from 125% in Q1 to around 60%. David Orr — Excess Returns holds a contrarian view, predicting a "commodity boom bust cycle" for compute, leading to an oversupply as efficiency gains outpace demand. Citing Dean Kernut, Jared Dillian — The Macro Dirt Podcast suggests the leadership change at Nvidia signals the "central bank of AI is now in tightening mode," putting the sector into contraction.

KEY TENSION: Q1 2027 earnings and forward guidance from major hyperscalers (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta) will be critical in confirming whether the capex boom is extending or rolling over.

SPOTLIGHT: Americas Gold and Silver: The Making of a Major Silver Turnaround

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Oliver Turner, EVP Corporate Development, Americas Gold and Silver

  • Americas Gold and Silver (USA on TSX, USAS on NYSE American) is a turnaround story led by a new management team with a successful track record at Klondex Mines and Corror Resources.
  • The strategy involves modernizing the Galena mine in Idaho, aiming to return it to its peak production of 5 million ounces of silver per year.
  • The Cosala mine in Mexico is described as a "cash ATM," generating free cash flow to fund the ~$80 million in capital investments at Galena.
  • The company is exploring an Antimony Plant JV at Galena, a strategic move to maximize byproduct credits and capitalize on the demand for critical metals.
  • Management is on track to meet Q4 2026 production guidance, with the full Galena run rate expected around 2028-2029.

SPOTLIGHT: Intrepid Metals: Three Different Paths to a Major Arizona Discovery

Host: Michael McCrae Guest: Matt Lennox-King, CEO, Intrepid Metals

  • Intrepid Metals (INTR) is in the middle of a 10,000-meter drill program at its Corral Copper Project in Arizona, targeting CRD, porphyry, and epithermal gold mineralization.
  • Despite a two-month delay in assay results, the program is guided by visually distinctive mineralization, with first results expected by the end of November.
  • A new epithermal gold target, "Maddie," shows high grades at surface (up to 21 g/t gold) over a large 1.5 km x 1.5 km area.
  • Teck Resources owns 14.5% of the company and is actively involved through a technical committee, providing valuable expertise.
  • The Arizona exploration landscape is viewed as "relatively uncrowded" for junior explorers, making it easier for Intrepid to stand out.

SPOTLIGHT: Mithril's Next Chapter: Putting Economics Around Copalquin

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: John Ske, CEO, Mithril Gold and Silver

  • Mithril Gold and Silver (MRL) is advancing its Copalquin project in Mexico, with a Preliminary Economic Assessment (PEA) for its high-grade "Target One" expected in Q1 2027.
  • The PEA is expected to show an after-tax NPV of $300-400 million US (at mid-$3,000 gold), with initial capex around $120 million for a mine producing 60-70k oz gold equivalent per year.
  • The company is also developing "Target Four," a new silver-dominant resource growth area, with an initial resource estimate planned for the end of 2027.
  • CEO John Ske believes the junior mining market is "beaten up," presenting good buying opportunities, and expects a significant rerating for MRL's stock on a positive PEA.

SPOTLIGHT: Nickel's Dead? Nobody Told Justin Werner

Host: Travis Ricciardo, JD Guest: Justin Werner, Managing Director, Nickel Industries

  • Nickel Industries has successfully navigated the Indonesian nickel market by partnering with Chinese firm Tsingshan and developing a fully integrated, low-cost business model.
  • The Indonesian government's policies (ore export bans, moratorium on new plants) are seen as effectively supporting a nickel price between $17,000-$20,000/ton.
  • The company is at the end of a major capex cycle and expects high free cash flow conversion (~80%) going forward, targeting over $1 billion in EBITDA within 12-18 months.
  • Indonesia is now replicating its nickel strategy with bauxite, banning raw ore exports to encourage downstream investment in alumina and aluminum production, leveraging cheap power.
  • Werner believes Nickel Industries' shares are "very cheap at the moment" given its growth trajectory and strong cash flow potential.
