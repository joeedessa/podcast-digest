MACRO ROOM TEMPERATURE

Juliette Declercq's argument for persistently sticky core inflation sets a collision course with a market showing clear signs of AI-momentum fatigue, as noted by Katie Stockton. This dynamic forces investors to navigate the dual threats of a hawkish Federal Reserve and a potential tech rollover. In response, influential voices like Adam Parker and Patrick Ceresna are pivoting towards energy as a key diversifier, while Andrew Sheets flags escalating tensions with Iran as a wild card that could disrupt the summer rally.

DOMINANT THEMES

THEME 1 — Beyond the AI Bubble: The Search for Uncorrelated Returns (8 shows)

THESIS: The AI-driven rally is showing signs of maturity and concentration risk, prompting a strategic search for diversification into sectors like energy and healthcare. These areas offer compelling fundamentals, above-average earnings achievability, and low correlation to the tech sector, providing a potential hedge against a tech rollover or a broader market correction.

PROPONENTS OF DIVERSIFICATION (Adam Parker, Patrick Ceresna, Asyl Bagnon, John Loer, Mike Preston) The core argument is for de-risking from concentrated AI and semiconductor exposure. Adam Parker — Macro Voices advocates for a 7-8% portfolio allocation to energy, citing its low correlation to tech and high probability of meeting earnings estimates. Patrick Ceresna — Macro Voices and Asyl Bagnon — Macro Voices echo this, recommending energy via the XLE ETF as a "clean rotation." Beyond energy, John Loer — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money and Mike Preston — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money observe encouraging rotation into small caps, financials, and healthcare, suggesting an environment where active management can outperform. Adam Parker — Macro Voices also highlights healthcare as deeply undervalued and anti-correlated to AI semis.

VOICES OF CAUTION ON TECH MOMENTUM (Katie Stockton, Warren Pies, Brent Kachuba, Charlie Peabody) The urgency for diversification is underscored by signs of weakness in the tech sector. Katie Stockton — Excess Returns observes a "short-term loss of momentum" in the S&P 500 and NASDAQ 100, with a "chink in the armor" for mega-caps. Warren Pies — Excess Returns notes that semiconductors are being priced as non-cyclical, a historical anomaly suggesting exuberance. Brent Kachuba — Excess Returns warns that his Core 1M indicator signals an imminent "volatility spasm" in the NASDAQ that could lead to a sharp correction. Finally, Charlie Peabody — Benny The Squirrel sees multiple signs of a market cycle peak, including M&A volumes, which historically precede downturns.

KEY TENSION: Resolution hinges on Q2 earnings season, specifically whether hyperscaler AI spending continues its aggressive growth trajectory, as Andrew Sheets — Morgan Stanley outlines in his base case, or if it falters, which would validate the rotation thesis and likely accelerate the move into uncorrelated sectors.

THEME 2 — Persistent Inflation & The Hawkish Fed (4 shows)

THESIS: The consensus view of a smooth path to disinflation is fundamentally flawed. Sticky core inflation, driven by overlooked factors like import prices, a tight labor market, and supply chain fragilities, will force the Federal Reserve to maintain a hawkish stance, with further rate hikes remaining a distinct possibility.

HAWKISH / STICKY INFLATION CAMP (Juliette Declercq, Eric Pakman, Andrew Sheets) This camp argues that underlying price pressures are more persistent than markets appreciate. Juliette Declercq — Maggie Lake Talking Markets is the most forceful voice, asserting that core PCE ex-housing will remain sticky above 4% and that rising import prices for plastics and chips are a key driver. She believes the Fed will likely hike in July to maintain credibility. Eric Pakman — Excess Returns supports this by pointing to structural issues, such as the proliferation of low-wage jobs that don't build wealth and elevated crack spreads that keep consumer fuel prices high regardless of crude oil levels. Andrew Sheets — Morgan Stanley identifies higher-than-expected inflation as a primary risk that could break the summer rally by forcing the Fed to hike sooner than anticipated.

DOVISH / DISINFLATIONARY CAMP (JD, Host on Macro Dirt Podcast) The counter-argument is that the market is over-pricing Fed hawkishness. JD — The Macro Dirt Podcast finds the pricing of two rate hikes for next June to be "nuts" and expects the upcoming FOMC minutes to be more dovish. A host on The Macro Dirt Podcast anticipates the upcoming CPI data will come in below consensus, which would support a less aggressive Fed path.

KEY TENSION: The upcoming CPI data release and the July 29th FOMC meeting will provide a critical test. A hot inflation print would validate Declercq's thesis and likely send yields higher, while a soft number would support the dovish view and could reignite risk assets.

Gold Outlook

Jeff Currie — Wealthion: Bullish Reasoning: Believes a supercycle in precious metals is underway, driven by the debasement of fiat currencies and rising global debt. He argues central banks are effectively moving towards a gold-backed system by market forces and projects gold could reach $10,000.

U.S. Equity Market Outlook (Next 6-12 Months)

Adam Parker — Macro Voices: Bullish (Choppy but upward) Reasoning: The market is not in a bubble and will be driven higher by strong corporate earnings growth. He anticipates a contraction in P/E multiples but believes earnings strength will overcome this, leading to a positive trend.

Michael Green — Wealth Building Blueprint Vladyslav Grab: Bearish (Crash Risk) Reasoning: The market is dangerously inflated by the mechanics of passive investing, which lacks a valuation filter and forces capital into appreciating assets. This flow-driven system is vulnerable to a sharp reversal, potentially leading to a "lost decade" or significant nominal losses.

Charlie Peabody — Benny The Squirrel: Bearish (20% Downturn) Reasoning: Multiple indicators are signaling a market cycle peak, including M&A volumes reaching historical highs relative to GDP and thinning earnings cushions at banks. He expects a market downturn of 20% between now and year-end.

U.S. Dollar Outlook

George Gammon — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: Bullish Reasoning: The current "fourth turning" environment, characterized by global political polarization and slowing circulation of money and credit due to counterparty risk, will lead to a stronger dollar that acts as a "wrecking ball" for other economies.

Ben Hunt — Excess Returns: Bullish Reasoning: Data shows a significant reversal in the Fed's credibility narrative, from "losing credibility" a year ago to "gaining credibility" now. This diminishes the de-dollarization thesis and provides fundamental support for a stronger U.S. dollar.

Jeff Currie — Wealthion: Bearish Reasoning: The dollar will have to lose value as a policy tool to address the nearly $40 trillion in U.S. debt combined with high interest rates. Currency debasement is the likely path forward.

SPOTLIGHT: Introduction to Copper Giant and a Window of Opportunity for Mining in Colombia

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Ian Harris, CEO and President, Copper Giant Resources

  • Ian Harris presents Copper Giant's (CGNT / LBCMF) Makoa project in Colombia as a potential billion-ton, near-surface copper-molybdenum deposit.
  • The project's extremely high molybdenum content is a key differentiator, potentially making it the second-largest undeveloped moly project globally.
  • A Preliminary Economic Assessment (PEA) is expected by year-end, which Harris believes will establish a clear Net Present Value (NPV) and address perceived jurisdictional risk.
  • Harris argues a "political window" has opened in Colombia for mining investment, and majors are now in an M&A cycle for large copper deposits.
  • The project benefits from well-developed infrastructure (power, roads, airport), which should significantly reduce future capital expenditures.

SPOTLIGHT: Kincora Copper's Sam Spring on the Companys Divestment from Mongolia

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Sam Spring, CEO, Kincora Copper

  • Sam Spring details Kincora Copper's (KCC) strategic pivot to a 100% Australian focus after divesting its Mongolian assets for $10 million US.
  • The company employs a "hybrid project generator model," using partner funding for exploration to minimize dilution while retaining significant upside.
  • Over $100 million in potential partner funding has been identified across its seven Australian assets, with over $10 million already invested by partners like AngloGold Ashanti.
  • Upcoming catalysts include drilling news from partners and the receipt of an additional $5 million from the Mongolian divestment, further strengthening the balance sheet.
  • Spring notes that North American capital markets have regained their position as the deepest pool of capital for resource companies.

SPOTLIGHT: The Bitcoin Yield Most People Still Dont Understand

Host: Pascal Hügli Guest: Chris Ritter, Chief Strategy Officer at Zeus

  • Chris Ritter argues that Bitcoin, via the Lightning Network, can function as a store of value, a consumable, and a capital asset simultaneously.
  • Users can earn a native, leverage-free yield in Bitcoin by deploying it to a Lightning node to route payments, all while maintaining self-custody.
  • This "Lightning Routing Income" (LRI) offers a new way for long-term holders to generate returns without counterparty risk from lending or derivatives.
  • A "self-custody with managed operations" model is emerging, allowing users to retain their keys while experts manage the node, solving a key operational hurdle.
  • While the network is centralizing to achieve deep liquidity, Ritter emphasizes that this does not compromise Bitcoin's fundamental properties or users' ability to exit unilaterally.

SPOTLIGHT: One of the Worlds Largest Hedge Funds on Its 86x Growth in Token Spending

Host: Joe Weisenthal, Tracy Alloway Guest: Gary Collier, CTO, Man Group; Tushara Fernando, Head of Data and AI, Man Group

  • Man Group has seen an 86x increase in AI token spending since January, indicating widespread adoption across all departments, from quant research to discretionary management.
  • The key to generating alpha with AI is combining proprietary, well-structured data with expert human oversight and access to capabilities like backtesting.
  • The firm is developing "agentic workflows" where AI systems drive the entire research process, from idea generation to backtesting, with 15-20 AI-ideated models already approved for trading.
  • The primary bottleneck for AI implementation is not compute or data, but the organizational change required to move quickly yet safely in a regulated business.
  • AI is enabling earlier systematic access to less developed markets (e.g., crypto, securitized credit) by helping overcome challenges in data connectivity and price derivation.

SPOTLIGHT: How The Federal Reserve Could Shrink Trillions From Its Balance Sheet

Host: Jack Farley Guest: Darrell Duffie, Professor, Stanford Graduate School of Business

  • Darrell Duffie outlines four techniques for the Fed to reduce its balance sheet by lowering the banking system's demand for reserves.
  • Proposals include: 1) Using temporary open market operations to smooth daily reserve fluctuations; 2) Reducing the "stigma" of using Fed liquidity facilities; 3) Adopting a liquidity savings mechanism (LSM) to net interbank payments; 4) Tiering the interest rate paid on reserves to disincentivize holding excess balances.
  • These measures would allow the Fed to shrink its assets without causing funding market disruptions like those seen in September 2019.
  • A smaller balance sheet could reduce political scrutiny and better position the Fed to expand its balance sheet during a future crisis.

SPOTLIGHT: Why This 73 Billion Investor is Avoiding Data Centers

Host: Jack Farley Guest: Josh Pristaw, Managing Director and President, Clarion Partners

  • Josh Pristaw explains Clarion Partners avoids direct data center investments due to their high cost, which compromises portfolio diversification, and uncertainty over long-term residual value.
  • The firm gains indirect exposure to the data center boom through its $42 billion industrial logistics portfolio, as subcontractors and equipment manufacturers are major tenants.
  • Clarion has high conviction in multifamily housing, driven by demographic tailwinds of millennials entering peak household formation age.
  • Senior housing is identified as a top growth sector, with an "inevitable trend" of 10,000 people turning 80 daily creating massive demand for new facilities.
  • Pristaw believes the real estate market has entered a new cycle, with prices having adjusted 20-25% from peaks, making the asset class fairly valued.
