DOMINANT THEMES

THEME 1 — The Great AI IPO Wave & Market Structure Collision (4 shows)

THESIS: A historic wave of mega-cap AI IPOs, including SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI, is set to test a market structure already dominated by passive flows and showing signs of speculative exhaustion. The sheer size of these offerings creates unprecedented liquidity demands and the potential for extreme, flow-driven volatility that could override fundamental analysis in the short term.

FLOWS & SQUEEZE POTENTIAL (Jim Bianco, Brent Kochuba) The initial trading will be a "flows game" completely detached from fundamentals, according to Brent Kochuba — Excess Returns. He argues the combination of the largest IPO ever ($75B for SpaceX), a tight initial float, and massive hype creates a perfect storm for a potential gamma squeeze as market makers hedge call options. Jim Bianco — Milk Road Macro adds that recent rule changes by Nasdaq and S&P will allow for much faster inclusion into major indices, forcing passive funds to buy the new giants and sell down existing components, creating significant market churn.

LATE-CYCLE SPECULATION & INSIDER SELLING (Ted Oakley, Mike Green) This IPO frenzy is a classic sign of a late-stage bull market, warns Ted Oakley — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money. He describes it as "greed city" and a "lemming market," noting that when it becomes this easy for insiders to sell, it's often a top signal. Historically, 70% of the top 10 IPOs in the last 20 years were lower a year later. Mike Green — Excess Returns reinforces this caution, pointing to a major shift where large tech companies are now looking to issue equity after a decade of buybacks, suggesting the dynamic of shrinking supply that fueled the bull run is reversing.

KEY TENSION: Whether the sheer mechanical force of index inclusion flows and speculative call buying can overwhelm fundamental valuation concerns and clear signs of market exhaustion flagged by veteran investors.

THEME 2 — The AI Compute Bottleneck & The Picks-and-Shovels Play (3 shows)

THESIS: While AI models and software capture headlines, the true bottleneck and a more durable investment thesis lie in the physical infrastructure required for the AI buildout. This includes compute capacity, data centers, power, and the underlying commodities, which are facing physical constraints and inefficiencies that present a different angle on the AI trade.

INFRASTRUCTURE & COMMODITY DEMAND (Ted Oakley, Joe Weisenthal, Anjney Midha) The AI boom is creating a massive demand for physical resources. Ted Oakley — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money is avoiding overpriced semiconductor and AI stocks, instead investing directly in the "picks-and-shovels": copper, energy, and natural gas, which are essential for data centers and currently cheap. Joe Weisenthal — Odd Lots highlights the tension between ephemeral AI prompts and the immense physical inputs required, from power to transformers. Anjney Midha — Odd Lots notes that compute prices for long-term rentals have doubled in just six months, signaling intense demand and a critical bottleneck.

COMPUTE EFFICIENCY & COMMODITIZATION (Anjney Midha) The current compute infrastructure is highly inefficient, with industry utilization under 70%, argues Anjney Midha — Odd Lots. His firm aims to create a "grid for compute" to standardize and optimize usage, driving it above 95%. He foresees a "jagged frontier" for AI models, where an ecosystem of specialized models will emerge, leading to a commodified market where users select the cheapest model that can perform a given task, shifting value away from a single "winner-takes-all" model.

KEY TENSION: Whether the primary value in the AI ecosystem will continue to accrue to a few dominant chip and model makers, or if it will shift to the providers of physical infrastructure, commodities, and the platforms that optimize compute efficiency.

The Attractiveness of the AI Investment Trade

Jim Bianco — Milk Road Macro: Bullish Reasoning: The AI trade still has room to grow despite its high risk and reward profile. The K-shaped economy, driven by the wealthy top 20% who own most stocks, will sustain corporate earnings and support the market, allowing the AI narrative to continue playing out.

Ted Oakley — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money & Mike Green — Excess Returns: Cautious / Skeptical Reasoning: The current AI frenzy is a speculative, late-cycle phenomenon. Ted Oakley avoids direct AI and semiconductor stocks, viewing them as overpriced and part of a "lemming market." Mike Green identifies "circular financing" within the sector (e.g., NVIDIA investing in a customer who then buys NVIDIA chips) as a sign of an unsustainable bubble, reminiscent of past capital spending manias. Both prefer to invest in the underlying physical inputs (Oakley) or position for distress (Green).

Overall Market Positioning and Strategy

Brent Kochuba — Excess Returns: Tactical Risk-On Reasoning: Near-term market moves will be dominated by flows, not fundamentals. He suggests being long SpaceX stock for its initial trading week to capitalize on a potential FOMO-driven "insane pump" and gamma squeeze dynamics.

Ted Oakley — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: Strategic Risk-Off Reasoning: The market is in a highly speculative phase mirroring past tops, making capital preservation paramount. His firm maintains a 40-45% allocation to short-term treasuries, has cut exposure to "frothy" precious metals, and advises investors with windfalls to "ice" their money in safe assets for 12-18 months, warning a "generational bear market" is increasingly likely.

SPOTLIGHT: Amerigo: A Low-Risk Way to Invest in Copper

Host: Jimmy Connor Guest: Aurora Davidson, Amerigo Resources

  • Amerigo Resources offers a unique, low-risk exposure to copper by reprocessing tailings from Codelco's El Teniente mine in Chile, avoiding traditional mining risks like exploration and development.
  • The business model is characterized by stable production (62.2M lbs of copper in 2025) and predictable cash costs ($1.87/lb), allowing for consistent free cash flow generation, especially as copper prices rise.
  • The company has a clear capital return policy, aiming to maintain a ~$30M cash balance and return all excess cash to shareholders via quarterly dividends, buybacks, and performance dividends.
  • As of March 31, 2026, the company held $57.2M in cash, leading to its highest performance dividend to date.
  • Crossing the $1 billion market cap threshold has made the stock more accessible to larger institutional investors, increasing its investability and liquidity.

SPOTLIGHT: America's $205 Billion Government Fund

Host: Jack Farley Guest: Conor Coleman, Development Finance Corporation (DFC)

  • The DFC is the U.S. government's international investment arm, with its investment capacity recently expanded from $60B to $205B.
  • Its mandate is to mobilize private capital for projects that advance U.S. foreign policy, focusing on sectors like critical minerals, infrastructure, energy, and technology in strategic geographies.
  • The DFC operates more like a Wall Street institution, using a full suite of products including equity, various loan types, and political risk insurance to de-risk projects for private investors.
  • A key goal is to create a multiplier effect, where DFC investment catalyzes significantly more private capital, offering an alternative to China's Belt and Road Initiative.
  • With plans to deploy an average of $27.5B annually over the next three years, the DFC will be one of the largest global liquidity providers, with a focus on non-wealthy countries.

SPOTLIGHT: Radically Lowering the Price of Compute

Host: Joe Weisenthal, Tracy Alloway Guest: Anjney Midha, AMP PBC

  • The AI boom faces a major physical constraint: a compute bottleneck, where demand for processing power far outstrips supply, and existing infrastructure is highly inefficient (under 70% utilization).
  • AMP PBC aims to solve this by creating a software-based "grid for compute," standardizing it into "grid credits" to drive utilization up to 95-96%, thereby lowering costs for researchers.
  • AI labs are developing custom silicon for two reasons: to control margins (as ~80% of R&D costs go to chip providers like NVIDIA) and to ensure supply chain independence.
  • The future of AI is likely a "jagged frontier" with an ecosystem of many specialized models, not a winner-takes-all market. This will lead to commodification as users choose the cheapest model that gets the job done.
  • Technical literacy is becoming essential for leaders to avoid misjudging AI capabilities and to optimize spending in a compute-constrained world.
