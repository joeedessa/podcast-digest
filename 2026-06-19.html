MACRO ROOM TEMPERATURE

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish debut has reset the board, forcing a market addicted to forward guidance to contend with a central bank prioritizing credibility over accommodation. Voices across the spectrum, from Axel Merk — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money to Joseph Wang — Forward Guidance, agree that the era of "FedSpeak" is over, replaced by a singular focus on price stability. This creates a direct collision with the powerful AI narrative, which market veterans Jeremy Grantham — Odd Lots and Ted Oakley — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money see as a historic bubble, with Grantham noting the classic late-stage divergence where speculative "junkie flyers" begin to fail. The question is no longer *if* the Fed will cut, but whether its newfound hawkishness is enough to break the speculative fever before it breaks the market.

DOMINANT THEMES

THEME 1 — The Warsh Fed's New Hawkish Era (8 shows)

THESIS: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first FOMC meeting marks a seismic shift in central banking, ending the era of forward guidance and pivoting aggressively towards a singular mandate of price stability. This new, less communicative, and more data-dependent regime is forcing an immediate market repricing of rate expectations and introduces significant uncertainty for investors accustomed to being "spoon-fed" by the Fed.

A NEW REGIME (Axel Merk, Joseph Wang, Danielle DiMartino Booth, John J. Hardy) The consensus is that Warsh is orchestrating a deliberate and significant overhaul. Warsh's statement that "the committee will deliver price stability, period," was a "mic drop moment," according to Joseph Wang — Forward Guidance. Axel Merk — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money highlights Warsh's plan to communicate less, eliminate forward guidance, and form task forces to review core Fed operations, including the balance sheet and the "ample reserve system." Danielle DiMartino Booth — The Julia La Roche Show strongly approved of removing the dot plot and forward guidance, viewing it as a move toward accountability. The goal, as articulated by Warsh and relayed by John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call, is to stop destroying the informational value of market prices, which had become mere reflections of Fed statements.

MARKET REACTION & IMPLICATIONS (Joseph Wang, John J. Hardy, Dale Pinkert, Paul Crake) The market's reaction was immediate and hawkish. The front end of the yield curve popped, with the market pricing in more than one hike this year, as noted by Joseph Wang — Forward Guidance. However, a key divergence emerged: the long end of the curve rallied, which John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call suggests could mean the market either fears the Fed will be too tough and slow growth, or it believes Warsh is a credible long-term inflation fighter. Dale Pinkert — Maggie Lake Talking Markets sees this as fuel for a major dollar breakout. Paul Crake — Benny The Squirrel notes this new Fed prioritizes financial stability above all, a lesson learned from 2008, suggesting a high bar for letting the system break.

KEY TENSION: Will the Fed's hawkish pivot and the resulting increase in volatility be sufficient to cool speculative excess in risk assets, or will the AI narrative prove powerful enough to ignore the changing monetary policy landscape?

THEME 2 — The AI Bubble Debate (7 shows)

THESIS: The market is split on whether the AI-driven rally is a sustainable technological revolution or a speculative bubble exhibiting classic late-stage behavior. While the transformative potential of AI is undisputed, extreme valuations, speculative IPO fever, and emerging physical constraints are fueling fears of a painful correction.

BUBBLE CAMP (Jeremy Grantham, Henrik Zeberg, Ted Oakley) This is a classic bubble, according to Jeremy Grantham — Odd Lots, who compares it to the railroad and dot-com manias, driven by a "fabulous idea" and easy money. He warns that a key historical precursor to a crash is now visible: speculative "junkie flyers" are starting to decline even as major indices rise. Ted Oakley — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money describes it as "lemming markets" and "greed city," pointing to the frenzy around IPOs like SpaceX as a sign the cycle is ending. Henrik Zeberg — Wealthion forecasts a "final melt-up" of another 15%+ in the NASDAQ before a severe, multi-stage crash worse than 2000 and 2008 combined.

FUNDAMENTAL & PHYSICAL CHALLENGES (Aswath Damodaran, Jacob Shapiro, Chris Semenuk) Beyond sentiment, fundamental questions are being raised. Aswath Damodaran — Excess Returns values SpaceX at $1.3 trillion, far below its trading price, arguing the market is ignoring the unproven, low-margin unit economics of its AI business. He warns of a "big market delusion" where massive, debt-funded capex could lead to a more painful correction than the dot-com bust. Jacob Shapiro — The Jacob Shapiro Podcast points to the real-world constraints of energy and water for data centers, which he calls a "Gordian knot" that requires government-level intervention. Conversely, Chris Semenuk — The Monetary Matters Network argues this AI-driven demand is the very source of a durable, multi-year investment opportunity in the under-the-radar "nuts and bolts" companies powering the US re-industrialization and electrification megatrend.

KEY TENSION: Resolution depends on whether AI companies can generate revenues and margins to justify astronomical valuations and capital expenditures before market psychology shifts or physical constraints halt the buildout.

DIVERGENT VIEWS

U.S. Equity Market Outlook

Henrik Zeberg — Wealthion: BULLISH (Short-Term Melt-Up) Reasoning: The market is in a "pure air" phase driven by psychology and capital rotation, not fundamentals. Zeberg forecasts at least another 15% gain in the NASDAQ to the $33,000-$34,000 range, fueled by liquidity and a final speculative blow-off into Q3 before a severe, multi-stage crash.

Jeremy Grantham — Odd Lots: BEARISH (Bubble Burst Imminent) Reasoning: The market is in an extraordinary, highly speculative bubble, comparable to 1929 and 2000. Grantham identifies a key historical pattern that precedes a crash: speculative, lower-quality stocks have already started to decline while the main indices make new highs. This divergence signals the end is near.

Precious Metals Outlook

Axel Merk — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: BEARISH (Near-Term) Reasoning: The new hawkish Fed under Kevin Warsh, which is credibly committed to fighting inflation, creates a direct headwind for precious metals. A more effective Fed reduces the need for an inflation hedge. Merk sold a small gold position upon Warsh's nomination, though he remains a long-term bull due to fiscal concerns.

Mel Mattison — Milk Road Macro: BULLISH Reasoning: Precious metals are set to benefit from anticipated Fed rate cuts and a weaker dollar later this year. Mattison is actively rotating profits from overextended AI names into undervalued miners (GDX) and sees silver's industrial demand as a key long-term driver.

Oil Price Outlook

Jeff Currie — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money: BULLISH Reasoning: The oil bull market is just beginning, driven by chronic underinvestment and rapidly tightening inventories. The SPR is at a 43-year low, and he projects "day zero" for critical inventory levels in mid-July. He sees the current price as a mispricing and a unique buying opportunity, with a fair value of $85 even without geopolitical conflict.

Mel Mattison — Milk Road Macro: NEUTRAL / BEARISH (Near-Term) Reasoning: The Iran MOU is expected to hold, leading to oil prices stabilizing around $70 and potentially dropping into the $60s. This will create deflationary pressures that will, in turn, compel the Fed to cut rates.

SpaceX Valuation

Aswath Damodaran — Excess Returns: OVERVALUED Reasoning: Damodaran's fundamental valuation of SpaceX is $1.3 trillion, significantly below its current market price of $2.7 trillion. He argues the market is overvaluing the AI business (XAI), which has challenging unit economics and is a low gross margin business despite a large addressable market. He calls the current price "a bridge too far."

Brent Kochuba — Excess Returns: BULLISH (Short-Term Flow-Driven) Reasoning: Fundamentals are irrelevant for SpaceX in the near term; it's a "flows game." Given the tight float, high retail interest, and significant liquidity demand, a gamma squeeze could easily push the stock up into the $200 range. He advises being long the stock, not short, over the next week.

SPOTLIGHT: Amerigo Resources: A Low-Risk Copper Play

Host: Jimmy Connor Guest: Aurora Davidson, Amerigo Resources

  • Amerigo Resources offers a unique, low-risk way to invest in copper by reprocessing tailings from Codelco's El Teniente mine, avoiding traditional mining risks and capital intensity.
  • The business model provides stable production and predictable cash costs, which in 2025 were $1.87/lb for 62.2 million pounds of copper.
  • The company prioritizes returning cash to shareholders through dividends and buybacks after maintaining a cash balance of ~$30 million.
  • Strengthening copper prices translate directly into increased free cash flow due to a stable cost structure, offering investors direct leverage to the commodity price.
  • Crossing the billion-dollar market cap threshold has made the company eligible for a broader pool of institutional investors and potential inclusion in indices and ETFs.

SPOTLIGHT: Highland Copper: A Permitted U.S. Copper Project

Host: Jimmy Connor Guest: Barry O’Shea, Highland Copper

  • Highland Copper's Copperwood project is a rare, fully permitted U.S. copper project, considered vital for domestic critical mineral production.
  • The project has received a $250 million letter of interest for financing from the U.S. Export-Import Bank, covering ~60% of the $400 million capex.
  • The project has significant leverage to copper prices: its NPV triples from $170M at $4/lb copper to $507M at $5/lb copper.
  • Annual production is projected at 70 million pounds of copper, representing 5-10% of the current U.S. domestic production shortfall.
  • Production is targeted to commence in the second half of 2029 or into 2030, positioning the company as a key domestic producer.

SPOTLIGHT: Blackrock Silver's Tonopah West Project

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Andrew Pollard, CEO, Blackrock Silver

  • The Tonopah West project in Nevada is a de-risked, high-confidence silver development opportunity with a clear path to production.
  • The updated 2024 PEA shows strong economics with an after-tax IRR of 28% and a $437 million NPV at $31/oz silver.
  • The project is on private land, which expedites the permitting process, with a target to break ground in Q3 2025 and potential ore extraction in Q1 2028.
  • The project could increase U.S. domestic silver production by 10% annually, making it eligible for potential federal government assistance under critical minerals initiatives.
  • The upside case, based on $67/oz silver, shows a dramatic increase in value to a $1.55 billion NPV and an 80% IRR.
