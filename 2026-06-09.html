MACRO ROOM TEMPERATURE

The AI infrastructure build-out, described by Stacy Rasgon on the Steve Eisman show as the "center of the universe," is forcing a profound repricing of risk, creating a stark tension between its deflationary productivity promise and its inflationary demand for power and commodities. While analysts like Lawrence Lepard — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money see AI capex as a "gargantuan tsunami of capital" fueling nominal asset growth, others like Matt Smith — The Monetary Matters Network warn the market is "sleepwalking into a crisis" as it ignores the commodity shortages, particularly in energy, that this build-out is creating.

DOMINANT THEME(S)

The AI Gold Rush: Infrastructure Bottlenecks and Market Distortions (11 shows) The narrative around AI has shifted from experimental use to an "existential race" for compute, creating significant market distortions and new bottlenecks. Brandon McBee — Odd Lots confirms that enterprise demand is now "authentic and foundational," with clients requesting five-year committed contracts for NVIDIA infrastructure, for which he sees "no material demand" for alternatives. This insatiable demand is driving the entire semiconductor sector into "overdrive," according to Stacy Rasgon — Steve Eisman, with investors sequentially chasing bottlenecks from compute (NVIDIA) to memory (Micron), power, and optics. Rasgon argues valuations are not yet "egregious" as they are supported by massive earnings growth.

However, this boom is creating new constraints. The primary bottleneck is no longer chips, but "powered shells"—energized data centers—and the skilled electricians needed to build them, a point emphasized by both Brandon McBee and Ian Dunning — Odd Lots. Stacy Rasgon — Steve Eisman identifies power constraints as a key long-term risk that could derail the entire trend. This physical-world friction is creating an inflationary impulse, with Lawrence Lepard — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money highlighting the trillion-dollar annual AI capex as a key driver of persistent inflation. The market structure is also being warped. The impending SpaceX IPO, valued at a potential $1.75 trillion, is viewed by Dave Noddick — Excess Returns as a trade to avoid due to "bizarre and unpredictable" supply/demand dynamics from its small float and accelerated index inclusion. Meanwhile, Andrew Sheets — Morgan Stanley observes that while AI is driving historic dispersion among individual stocks, geopolitical risks are causing broad asset classes like stocks and bonds to move in near-perfect, and unusual, correlation.

Energy & Commodities: A Looming Supply Crisis (6 shows) A cohort of analysts is sounding the alarm on a severe, underpriced supply crisis in commodities, particularly oil. Matt Smith — The Monetary Matters Network delivered the most urgent warning, stating the oil market is "sleepwalking into a crisis" as the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz has removed over a billion barrels of supply in three months. He argues the market is "comatose," ignoring critically low US product inventories, and predicts oil could reach "$200 or $300" if the strait remains closed. This view is complemented by Jacob Shapiro — Jacob Shapiro, who notes that new pipelines will make the Strait of Hormuz obsolete for most traffic in 12-18 months, fundamentally altering Iran's leverage.

Beyond energy, the structural bull case for metals is gaining traction. Michael Oliver — Wealthion forecasts an "explosive" move in silver to $300-$500, driven by a looming government bond crisis that will force Fed intervention and devalue fiat currency. The Virtual Copper Conference, covered on Jimmy Connor, detailed a structural deficit for copper, with John Cipaglia — Jimmy Connor citing UBS projections for a 520,000-ton deficit by 2027 and a $15,000/ton price target. This demand is driven by electrification and the AI build-out. The macro backdrop for this is the ongoing de-dollarization trend, with Trevor Hall — Mining Stock Daily reporting that central bank gold holdings ($4 trillion) have now surpassed their US Treasury holdings ($3.9 trillion) for the first time in three decades.

Fed Policy at a Crossroads (4 shows) Analysts are sharply divided on the Federal Reserve's next move, creating a significant macro uncertainty. Following a surprisingly strong non-farm payrolls report, Neil Dutta — RenMac forcefully argued for a hawkish pivot, predicting the Fed will drop its easing bias this month and could adopt a tightening bias by July, reversing last year's "insurance cuts." John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call echoed this, noting the strong jobs data pushed the 2-year Treasury yield to a new cycle high, implying a high likelihood of a rate hike. In stark contrast, Lawrence Lepard — Adam Taggart Thoughtful Money presented a non-consensus call for an imminent 50 basis point rate *cut* in June, believing the new Fed chair will prioritize industrial policy and focus on lower inflation measures like the Dallas trimmed-mean PCE. This fundamental disagreement over the Fed's reaction function—boomflation vs. a "run it hot" policy—is central to the outlook for rates and risk assets.

SPOTLIGHT: Virtual Copper Conference - Master Class on Copper

Host: James Conner Guest: John Cipaglia (Sprott Asset Management), Ross Beattie (Lumina Metals), and representatives from Amerigo Resources, Atex Resources, Evolve Royalties, Faraday Copper, and others.

  • Structural Deficit: The core theme was copper's critical role in global electrification (grids, EVs, renewables) and new demand from AI data centers, which is causing it to decouple from traditional industrial metals. John Cipaglia cited Robert Freeland's statistic that the world will need to mine as much copper in the next 18-22 years as has been mined in all of history.
  • Price Outlook: UBS projects a 520,000-ton copper deficit by 2027 and a price of $15,000 per ton by March 2027. Ross Beattie noted that current record-high prices reflect the premium required to source necessary supply, with inflation reinforcing the robust outlook.
  • Supply Constraints: A key challenge is the long lead time (15-25 years) from discovery to production for new mines, which fundamentally constrains the supply response to higher prices.
  • Investment Vehicles: Sprott has launched the Sprott Physical Copper Trust (COPP), the first exchange-listed vehicle for direct investment in physical copper, avoiding the negative roll yield associated with futures.
  • Company Highlights: The conference featured numerous junior and mid-tier copper companies, including Amerigo Resources (reprocessing tailings from Codelco), Atex Resources (high-grade discovery in Chile), and Faraday Copper (consolidating a large undeveloped US project in Arizona).
