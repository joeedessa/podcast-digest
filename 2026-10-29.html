Ben Carlson — The Compound remains on the fence, acknowledging compelling arguments for a potential bust.

KEY TENSION: Resolution depends on whether future hyperscaler earnings and guidance confirm sustained, profitable demand that justifies current and future CapEx, or if slowing investment and margin pressure validate the bond market's skepticism.

THESIS: The market is coalescing around the idea that the Federal Reserve is at or near the end of its hiking cycle, but disagreement persists on the timing and catalyst for a definitive pivot. The debate centers on whether economic weakness and debt constraints will force the Fed's hand soon, or if sticky inflation will necessitate a "higher for longer" stance with one final hike.

DOVES (Trey Reik, Trey Reich, Dale Pinkert) This camp believes the Fed is done. Dale Pinkert — Maggie Lake Talking Markets believes the 10-year yield's peak at 5.33% may be a significant top, with rates poised to fall to a "four or lower handle" as the economy slows.

HAWKS (Michael Gapin, Matthew Hornbach) This group sees a more measured, data-dependent Fed that still has work to do. Michael Gapin — Morgan Stanley characterizes the Fed's approach as a "leisurely hike," not an aggressive tightening cycle. Matthew Hornbach — Morgan Stanley notes that while technical flows caused the recent spike above 5.25% in the 10-year, the yield's direction will ultimately be determined by the market's perception of the Fed's 2027 policy path.

KEY TENSION: The release of upcoming inflation and labor market data will resolve this; a sharp deterioration would validate the doves, while persistent strength and sticky inflation would support the "leisurely" hawk case.
