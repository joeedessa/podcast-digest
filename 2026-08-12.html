DOMINANT THEMES

THEME 1 — The AI Investment Cycle: Productivity Boom or Speculative Bubble? (3 shows)

THESIS: The market is intensely divided on whether the trillion-dollar-plus investment in AI infrastructure represents the beginning of a multi-year productivity supercycle or a speculative bubble fueled by circular financing and hype. The resolution of this debate will determine the future valuation of the market's largest companies and the trajectory of the entire tech sector.

BOOM CAMP (Dom Rizzle, Matt Caruso) This camp argues the AI buildout is fundamentally sound and has significant runway. Dom Rizzle — Excess Returns believes the capital cycle is only halfway through, with hyperscaler capex set to accelerate in 2027, driven by strong and improving ROICs at AWS, Azure, and GCP. He sees the current environment as analogous to 1998, presaging a stronger rally to come. Matt Caruso — Markets Unscripted views AI as the "ultimate productivity tool," creating a boom similar to the 1990s internet revolution. He argues the trillions flowing into the US for AI investment act as a "massive stimulus," supporting liquidity and the credit cycle.

BUBBLE CAMP (Ed Zitron) This camp views the AI narrative as a massive fraud. Ed Zitron — Adam Taggart | Thoughtful Money® claims there is no proof of broad productivity gains and that 70% of hyperscaler AI revenue is a circular loop from unprofitable labs like OpenAI and Anthropic spending their venture funding on compute. He argues the underlying LLM technology is unreliable and the business model is non-existent. He predicts a "cataclysmic" crash, historic impairments at Microsoft, Google, and Amazon, and a "global funding crisis" when the bubble bursts.

KEY TENSION: The key tension will be resolved by hyperscaler earnings reports over the next 2-3 quarters. If they can demonstrate a broadening base of AI-related revenue from a diverse set of profitable enterprise customers, the boom thesis will be validated. If AI revenue growth remains concentrated in a few unprofitable startups or begins to decelerate, the bubble thesis will gain credibility.

THESIS: With long-term Treasury yields hitting their highest levels since 2007, the Federal Reserve's credibility is on the line as it navigates persistent inflation against a backdrop of a surprisingly resilient economy. Tomorrow's CPI report is a critical catalyst that could force the Fed's hand and determine the next major move for equities, bonds, and the dollar.

He warns of "bond vigilantes" reacting to growing government debt. Chris Vermeulen — Competent Man Podcast - Tom Bodrovics sees "very, very strong bull flag patterns" in 5, 10, and 30-year yields, pointing to "dramatically higher pricing" that he believes will cause a financial crisis. John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call notes the 30-year yield poking at 5.28% and worries that rising yields could destabilize risk sentiment, especially in equities.

MARKET MECHANICS & LIQUIDITY (Michael Howell, Jason Shapiro) This group focuses on the market's internal dynamics. Michael Howell — The Julia La Roche Show states that high nominal GDP growth (7-8%) is incompatible with current bond yields, which are "headed higher." He sees the Fed and Treasury engaging in "Treasury QE" at the short end, a policy that will "end badly." Jason Shapiro — Markets Unscripted is less concerned about inflation itself but is watching the market's reaction to the CPI data as a tactical signal, noting the Dow has "more sensitivity to inflation than the Nasdaq."

KEY TENSION: The release of tomorrow's US CPI data. A hot number, as John J. Hardy — Saxo Market Call suggests is possible due to July oil prices, would validate the hawkish camp and likely push yields higher, pressuring equities. A soft number would give the Fed breathing room but might be interpreted by the market as a green light for more risk-taking, potentially creating its own set of problems.

SPOTLIGHT: Blue Moon Metals Expands US Critical Minerals Strategy

Host: Ian Wagner Guest: Christian Cargill Simard, CEO, Blue Moon Metals

  • Strategic Focus: Blue Moon Metals is aggressively acquiring and developing tungsten, antimony, germanium, and gallium projects in the Western US to build a secure domestic supply chain, capitalizing on US national security interests and government decrees restricting purchases from China.
  • Project Pipeline: The company is advancing four key projects: the newly acquired tungsten/antimony portfolio, the Apex germanium/gallium mine (production expected next year), the Nusira copper project in Norway, and the Blue Moon gold/zinc project.
  • High-Grade Assets: Simard emphasized the exceptionally high-grade nature of the assets, noting the Apex mine's rock value is equivalent to 4-5 ounces of gold per ton, and some tungsten deposits are equivalent to 400% copper.
  • Financial Position: The company is well-capitalized with over $100M in cash and a $140M project finance package, with Simard stating they are "fully financed to build all four mines." A strategic financing for the tungsten projects is expected by the end of the current quarter.
  • Stock Performance: Despite a stream of positive news and improving fundamentals for critical metals, Simard expressed perplexity at the stock's stagnant performance, attributing it partly to institutional shorting activity, but expects it to "look a lot different" by year-end.

SPOTLIGHT: Sterling Metals' Soo Copper Discovery

Host: Michael McCrae Guest: Matt Wilson, CEO, Sterling Metals

  • Project Significance: Sterling Metals is advancing its Soo Copper Discovery in Ontario, which sits within the prolific Midcontinent Rift system. The project benefits from excellent infrastructure and has shown significant mineralization, including a discovery hole of 265 meters of 1.05% copper equivalent.
  • Exploration Progress: An ongoing 30,000-meter drill program is aimed at defining the deposit's footprint, with mineralization currently extending 400 meters vertically over a 500-meter strike.
  • Strategic Importance: Wilson highlighted the project's potential to bolster Canada's domestic copper supply, which is critical given rising demand from AI and electrification and the country's current consumption of 300,000 tons annually.
  • Near-Term Goal: The company aims to have a clear understanding of the project's volumetric potential by September/October and to advance economic studies and resource definition within the next 12 months to demonstrate viability within the current copper cycle.

SPOTLIGHT: Heliostar Metals Aims for Billion-Dollar Valuation

Host: Trevor Hall Guest: Charles Funk, CEO, Heliostar Metals

  • Operational Update: Heliostar reported a strong Q2 with record gold production (14,800 oz) and growing cash. Higher-than-guided All-In Sustaining Costs (AISC) of $2,287/oz were attributed almost entirely to a non-cash option grant.
  • Growth Project: The company's most critical task is advancing the Anapola project, with a feasibility study underway and an underground permit submission planned for September.
  • Non-Dilutive Financing: Funk emphasized a clear path to triple production by building Anapola without equity dilution, citing an attractive debt and project financing market. This execution is key to their goal of reaching a market cap "well north of 800 million Canadian."
  • Macro View: Funk noted that gold mines are "as profitable as they've probably ever been," and government money printing is "the greatest friend of a gold producer" as it devalues currencies.
