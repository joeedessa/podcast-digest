Stephanie Pomboy's thesis of a "higher for longer" rate regime, driven by a private sector AI credit boom crowding out government debt, is clashing directly with the market's persistent bid for risk assets. The primary tension is whether the lagged effects of restrictive policy, as flagged by Jim Paulson, will finally break the momentum trade, or if the "World War AI" capex cycle identified by Ben Hunt can power through rising yields. SPOTLIGHT: Why Private Credit Got Entangled With Insurance

Host: Tracy Alloway, Joe Weisenthal Guest: Andrew Granato, Assistant Professor of Law at UT Austin; Pranjal Dral, JD, PhD Candidate in Financial Economics at Yale University

  • Thesis: Private equity firms are increasingly buying life insurers to use their long-duration liabilities as a source of "permanent capital" to fund illiquid private credit loans, often to other companies within the PE firm's portfolio.
  • Systemic Risk: This trend shifts risk from private funds into the regulated insurance industry, which is backstopped by a state-level guarantee fund system that functions as a "stealth taxpayer bailout."
  • Opacity: Valuations for these private credit assets are opaque, relying on private letter ratings, creating concerns about overvaluation and skewed incentives similar to the 2008 crisis.
  • Regulatory Gaps: The state-level guarantee funds are post-funded (surviving insurers pay after a failure), are not risk-weighted, and are "completely untested" for a large, national insurer failure.
  • Proposed Solutions: Reforms could include a "tax on opacity" for hard-to-value assets, pre-funding the guarantee system like the FDIC, and applying a "Source of Strength Doctrine" to make parent companies liable for a failed insurer's obligations.

SPOTLIGHT: How to Spot a 20-Bagger Early

Host: Travis Ricciardo, JD Guest: Rob Cohen, Gold Fund 1832 Scotiabank

  • Thesis: To generate alpha in gold mining, avoid "index hugger" strategies and focus on a concentrated portfolio of 30-35 early-stage opportunities in safe jurisdictions.
  • Geographic Focus: Australia, particularly Western Australia, is a key market due to its mineral richness and less "combed over" geology compared to Nevada or Canadian gold camps.
  • Political Risk: Geopolitical risk has become a primary screen, leading to an exit from jurisdictions like Mali, Burkina Faso, and Mexico in favor of the "safest jurisdictions as possible."
  • Key Attributes: Success requires deep geological expertise ("the rocks you can't fix"), a willingness to invest heavily on-market in high-conviction names, and identifying companies with quality management and disciplined capital allocation.
  • Past Successes: Cohen identified multi-baggers like Northern Star (from $1 to $20+), Spartan Resources, Founders Metals, and Great Bear Resources by focusing on these principles.

SPOTLIGHT: Don't Buy Real Estate in Canada

Host: Jimmy Connor Guest: Ron Butler, Mortgage Broker

  • Thesis: The real estate markets in Ontario and British Columbia are in a "severe downturn," with prices in "pure decline" and expected to remain flat for years after bottoming in 2027.
  • Condo Market Collapse: The condo construction industry in Toronto and Vancouver has collapsed, with pre-construction units selling for 50% less than 2021 prices. Butler predicts zero new condo construction starts in Toronto by March 2028.
  • Rental Market Disaster: A combination of population decline in major provinces and a surge in purpose-built rental supply is creating a "rental price disaster," with rents falling for 19 straight months.
  • Investment Advice: Avoid buying condos as an investment in the Greater Toronto Area for the next 2-3 years due to guaranteed negative cash flow from falling rents and rising costs.
  • Macro Concerns: Butler is critical of the decline of Canada's manufacturing sector, predicting "no automobiles assembled" in Southern Ontario within 10 years, and rising bond yields pose a "bad omen" for fixed-rate mortgage holders.
